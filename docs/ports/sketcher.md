# Sketcher

Constrained 2D sketches solved by PlaneGCS in a worker, turned into faces, and driving pad, pocket, and revolve: milestone M3 (docs/roadmap/m3-sketcher.md). This note is the study that plans it. PlaneGCS is linked, not ported (CLAUDE.md); everything around it (the sketch model, the gate, what a solve means, faces, the features) is ported as behavior.

Sources: FreeCAD at `876271d660` (main, 2026-10-04) and the oracle's release, FreeCAD **1.1.3** (tag `145529fe74`, not in the maintainer's clone; its PlaneGCS was read from the release's source archive). Paths are relative to `src/Mod/Sketcher/App/` unless they say otherwise. On main, in 2026 (after 1.1; 1.1.3 has one `SketchObject.cpp`), FreeCAD split `SketchObject.cpp` into `SketchObject.cpp` (recompute, the shape, restore), `SketchObjectConstraints.cpp` (solve, constraint edits), `SketchObjectGeometry.cpp`, `SketchObjectExternal.cpp`, and `SketchObjectOperations.cpp` (editing tools); line numbers are main's.

**Main is not 1.1.3.** FreeCAD's main moved to 1.2 at `e8f826d0ed` (2025-11-13). Main has behavior the oracle doesn't: signed point–line, circle–line, and circle–circle distances and oriented tangents (`3c8a254356`, `f432d1c6a7`, `5a6be36bc3`, migration `e1e72c9195`), reference constraints evaluated after the solve instead of solved (`92894ea3a8`), a changed elimination threshold in the conflict analysis (`0b44a3f6c8`), point–point–line perpendicularity (`be1ec84709`), group and text constraints, new face makers (`24ab301685`, `7186d30f6e`), and a refactored, two-sided revolve. It also has a regression 1.1.3 doesn't (`a502762119`, below). Silico follows 1.1.3, as it follows 1.1.3's OCCT, except that reference constraints are evaluated after the solve, as on main (below); this note says where main differs.

## PlaneGCS

### Building it

Five `.cpp` files (`GCS`, `Constraints`, `Geo`, `SubSystem`, `qp_eq`), and `Util.h`'s typedefs. It needs:

- **Eigen 3.4** (FreeCAD requires ≥ 3.4.0; the oracle's FreeCAD 1.1.3 bundles exactly 3.4.0). `Eigen/Core`, `QR`, `Dense`, `Sparse`, `OrderingMethods`. The `FullPivLU::compute` override in `GCS.cpp` only compiles for Eigen < 3.3 and is dead with 3.4.
- **Boost, headers only**, for one call: `boost::connected_components` over an `adjacency_list`, splitting the system into independent parts (`GCS.cpp` `initSolution`). Two other Boost includes are unused. The oracle bundles Boost 1.86.
- **Stubs for four FreeCAD headers:** `SketcherGlobal.h`/`FCGlobal.h` (an export macro), `Base/Console.h` (`log` and `warning`; even at the default debug level, every redundancy solve logs two lines), `FCConfig.h`, and on main `Base/Tools.h`. No Qt, OCCT, or Python.
- **C++20** for 1.1.3 (main needs C++23 for `std::unreachable`), with `NDEBUG` as FreeCAD's release builds have it, and `-pthread` on Linux (below).

### Its API, as Silico uses it

- Parameters are raw `double*` the caller owns. Geometry structs hold pointers: `Point{x, y}`, `Line{p1, p2}`, `Circle{center, rad}`, `Arc` (circle plus `startAngle`, `endAngle`, and `start`, `end` points tied to them only by `addConstraintArcRules`), `Ellipse{center, focus1, radmin}` (centre, a focus, and the minor radius, not axes and an angle), arcs of ellipse, hyperbola, and parabola, and `BSpline` (poles, weights, knots, multiplicities, degree, periodic).
- Each `addConstraint*(…, tag, driving)` adds error rows. **The tag is the protocol**: a tag ≥ 1 is a sketch constraint, and the diagnosis reports conflicting, redundant, and partially redundant constraints as sorted lists of tags. **Tag 0 is never reported** (GCS.cpp's popularity contest skips it), so FreeCAD uses 0 for implicit rows (the arc rules) and Silico's tags start at 1. Negative tags are temporary, lower-priority rows (dragging, the SQP fallback). `driving = false`, with the value pointer passed to `declareDrivenParams`, makes a reference constraint.
- The life cycle is `declareUnknowns` → `initSolution` (saves the starting values, diagnoses, splits into independent parts, and substitutes parameters for every tagged `Equal`, so coincidences cost nothing) → `solve` (each part from the saved starting values, never from a previous attempt) → `applySolution` or `undoSolution`. `diagnose()` called on its own can leave parameters changed; always go through `initSolution`.
- **Defaults:** DogLeg, at most 100 iterations, convergence 1e-10 (DogLeg succeeds when the largest residual is ≤ 1e-10), QR pivot threshold 1e-13, dense QR below 1000 parameters and sparse (COLAMD) above (`c8106982de`, in 1.1.3 as `b7e3bb1f7c`: the sparse QR misjudged rank on aligned slots).
- **Status:** `Success`, `Converged` (minimized, but the error isn't zero: a failure), `Failed`. A dropped redundant constraint whose error is above convergence on the values the solve started from (GCS.cpp's `System::solve`, before the solution is written back) downgrades `Success` to `Converged`, so the status depends on where the sketch was drawn; the sketch worker reports any solve with equations dropped as solved except them, whichever it was.

### Diagnosis: degrees of freedom, conflicting, redundant

`diagnose()` (GCS.cpp) builds the dense Jacobian of the driving rows with tag ≥ 0 (tag-0 rows, an arc's rules, count toward the rank, which is why an arc has 5 degrees of freedom; they're only never removed or reported) and takes a full-pivot Householder QR of its transpose: degrees of freedom = unknowns − rank. When there are more rows than the rank, it groups the dependent rows, and a **popularity contest** removes, from each group, the constraint in the most groups (ties: the one with fewer rows, then the higher tag; every row of a removed tag goes together, `b626caffdc`; tag 0 and internal-alignment rows are never removed, `bffb82d531`, `aa67808f24`). It solves the rest. A removed constraint whose squared error is then below 1e-10, so whose error is below **1e-5**, is **redundant** (`GCS.cpp:5588` in 1.1.3, `convergenceRedundant`); the groups without one are **conflicting**, every tag in them reported. If that solve itself fails, nothing is redundant and every group is conflicting. So FreeCAD calls two dimensions that disagree by under 1e-5 (a length of 10 and a horizontal distance of 10.000005 on a horizontal line) redundant, not conflicting. A constraint with several rows (a coincidence is two) of which only some were redundant is **partially redundant** (`65fb44df6c`). When over-constrained, degrees of freedom = unknowns − non-redundant rows (negative). No unknowns gives −1; no driving rows gives 0.

A second QR, of the untransposed Jacobian, finds the **dependent parameters** (which coordinates the constraints don't fix): FreeCAD colours geometry "fully constrained" from them. FreeCAD runs it on a `std::async` thread, joined before its result is read.

### Determinism

Recompute must be bit-identical on macOS ARM and Linux x86 (ARCHITECTURE.md, invariant 5). PlaneGCS gets the deterministic recipe (`-ffp-contract=off`, the libm shim, the pinned clang and libc++) and `EIGEN_DONT_VECTORIZE`, as ARCHITECTURE.md already plans. Beyond the recipe:

| Risk | Where | What Silico does |
|---|---|---|
| **The order of unknowns is the order of their addresses.** `SubSystem::initialize` intersects two `std::set<double*>`, and the SQP solver sorts its parameters by pointer. That order is the Jacobian's column order, which decides pivoting ties. FreeCAD allocates each parameter with its own `new double`, so its own order depends on the allocator. | `SubSystem.cpp:59-70`, `GCS.cpp:4542` | The worker allocates every parameter (geometry, constraint values, the SQP copies) from one array, reserved once, in Silico's canonical order. Address order is then index order everywhere. |
| **Eigen's matrix-product blocking depends on the CPU.** Eigen 3.4 reads cache sizes by CPUID on x86 and uses fixed defaults on ARM, so a product deeper than about 336 (Levenberg–Marquardt's `JᵀJ` on a sketch with that many constraints) sums in a different order. The `EIGEN_DEFAULT_L*` macros only change the fallback. | `Eigen/src/Core/products/GeneralBlockPanelKernel.h` | `Eigen::setCpuCacheSizes` with fixed values when the worker starts, and a stress case large enough to cross the threshold. |
| `diagnose()` starts a thread (`std::async`, default policy) for the dependent parameters. It's joined before its result is read, so results don't depend on scheduling, but ARCHITECTURE.md wants workers single-threaded. | `GCS.cpp:4938, 5015` | Patch to `std::launch::deferred`: a single-threading patch, not a determinism one. OCCT's patch policy doesn't cover PlaneGCS; extending it is open decision 7. |
| Pointer-keyed sets and maps elsewhere (`redundant`, `conflictingMap`, `pIndex`, `c2p`) | `GCS.h` | Only looked up, or their output is re-sorted by tag: no effect. Checked by the stress corpus, not assumed. |

No randomness, time, `unordered_*`, or OpenMP. The oracle's FreeCAD.app is built with NEON and fused multiply-add, so Silico's results differ from the oracle's in the last bits: oracle comparisons use tolerances, as for OCCT.

### Failure modes the worker guards

- **No input checks.** NaN and infinity go straight in; degenerate geometry (a zero-length line, a zero radius, repeated knots, concentric circles in a tangency before `86d5eea787` / `7023e83843`) gives NaN by division by zero; every solver then stops and reports failure. B-spline indexing is unchecked, with undefined behavior for a periodic spline whose first multiplicity exceeds degree + 1 and a `size_t` underflow under two points (`Geo.cpp:830-857, 1118-1140`; one such crash fixed in `e7e410323e`). The core refuses non-finite and degenerate input before a request is sent (the validation gate, M3.2), and the worker refuses non-finite output, except in an unbuildable solve's values (`SolvedInvalid`, M3.3), which only name what can't be built and reach no output (docs/design/m3.3-solving.md).
- **No `throw`, but exceptions anyway:** `std::map::at` in the conflict analysis, `bad_alloc` (the diagnosis Jacobian is dense, constraints × unknowns, and dense QR is O(n³)), and asserts (gone under `NDEBUG`). The worker catches everything at its boundary; anything else is a crash, which the core already turns into an error.
- **Long solves:** every loop is bounded (100 iterations, the inner Levenberg–Marquardt loop 50, the popularity contest by its groups since `aa67808f24` fixed an infinite loop), but the diagnosis and the four-solver fallback chain can take seconds on a large sketch. `SKETCH_TIMEOUT` applies.
- **`addConstraintProportional` is half right.** Its error, `p1 − ratio·p2`, is right, but its gradient for `p2` ignores the ratio, and `initSolution` substitutes any tagged one whose two parameters are both unknowns as a plain equality. FreeCAD builds `Diameter` with it (radius = 0.5 × diameter, `GCS.cpp:1169-1176` in 1.1.3). A driving diameter is a fixed value, so only the radius's gradient counts and it's right; a reference diameter is an unknown, gets substituted as diameter = radius, and FreeCAD doubled it back when writing it out (`727dc9fa9e`, until main evaluated reference values instead). Silico sends a diameter as a radius of half its value (exact in binary), and never uses `Proportional`.
- **Main's status regression:** `a502762119` (2026-09) made `System::solve` assign each independent part's status instead of keeping the worst, so a failed part followed by a successful one reports success. 1.1.3 doesn't have it. The worker checks every constraint's residual after a solve anyway.

## The sketch model (Sketch.cpp, Constraint.cpp)

### Geometry and its parameters

| Kind | Parameters, in FreeCAD's order | Implicit rows (tag 0) | Degrees of freedom |
|---|---|---|---|
| Point | x, y | | 2 |
| Line segment | start x, y; end x, y | | 4 |
| Circle | centre x, y; radius | | 3 |
| Arc of circle | start; end; centre; radius; start angle; end angle | arc rules: start and end on the circle at the angles (4) | 5 |
| Ellipse | centre; focus 1; minor radius | | 5 |
| Arc of ellipse, of hyperbola | start; end; centre; focus 1; minor radius; two angles | arc rules (4) | 7 |
| Arc of parabola | start; end; vertex; focus 1; two angles | arc rules (4) | 6 |
| B-spline | poles; weights; start; end. **Knots aren't unknowns.** | start and end coincide with the first and last poles only for a non-periodic spline whose end multiplicities exceed the degree (`Sketch.cpp:1584` in 1.1.3) | 3 per pole, plus 2 for each end that isn't tied (so 3n + 4 for a periodic spline: its end points are always unknowns, `Sketch.cpp:1529, 1542`) |

- **Arcs reach the solver counter-clockwise** in the sketch plane (`getStartPoint(emulateCCW)`, `getRange(emulateCCW)`, from `0767cd35f8` after reversed arcs broke), angles in radians with end > start. **The solver never normalizes angles:** its arc rules hold only their sines and cosines, so a solve can leave the end angle below the start angle, or a turn past it. Writing it back, FreeCAD trims the circle from the start angle to the end angle taken a whole number of turns past it (OCCT's periodic trim): the counter-clockwise arc from the solved start to the solved end, whichever way the angles went: angles exactly a whole turn apart give a full circle, and exactly equal ones can't be built (`Geom_TrimmedCurve::U1 == U2`, 1.1.3). That's the arc Silico defines by its points, so there's no "arc flip" to detect (M3.3, docs/design/m3.3-solving.md); in Silico, an arc whose solved ends meet can't be built.
- **Ellipses** are (centre, focus, minor radius): the major radius is written back as √(|focus − centre|² + b²). A solution whose minor radius passes the major one makes OCCT refuse the ellipse, and the solution is rolled back (`e423c308af` fixed the order of the setters).
- **B-splines need a second solve** after write-back, since some of their constraints read OCCT state (`0641e243e0`). FreeCAD also nudges a lone weight of exactly 1 to 0.99 × another before solving (`80e7ece429`), so OCCT doesn't renormalize: a heuristic Silico won't copy.
- **Construction geometry is solved like any other** and only left out of the shape.
- **External geometry, and the sketch's axes and origin, are fixed parameters**, not constraints: no arc rules or B-spline end rows, which would over-constrain them (`c6de527391`, `adfcd154c6`). FreeCAD's ids: `H_Axis` −1 (its start is the root point), `V_Axis` −2, externals −3 and below.
- **Internal-alignment geometry** (ellipse axes and foci, a parabola's focus and focal axis, B-spline control points as construction circles whose radius is the weight, knot points) is ordinary geometry tied to its parent by an `InternalAlignment` constraint with an index. FreeCAD only `assert`s those indexes (`Sketch.cpp:4611, 4640`): undefined behavior in a release build.

### Constraints

`ConstraintType` (`Constraint.h:52-77`): `Coincident, Horizontal, Vertical, Parallel, Tangent, Distance, DistanceX, DistanceY, Angle, Perpendicular, Radius, Equal, PointOnObject, Symmetric, InternalAlignment, SnellsLaw, Block, Diameter, Weight` (and on main `Group`, `Text`). A constraint names up to three elements, each a geometry and a position (`none` the edge, `start`, `end`, `mid` the centre), plus a value, `isDriving`, and `isActive`.

**A constraint's meaning is its type and the shape of its references.** `Distance` alone is point–point, point–line, point–circle, circle–circle, circle–line, a line's length, or an arc's length, chosen by which elements and positions are set. `DistanceX` with one element and a position fixes a coordinate. `Tangent` with positions is endpoint-to-curve or endpoint-to-endpoint. `Angle` on one line is its direction, on two lines the angle from the first to the second (counter-clockwise positive), and on one arc its sweep (`6a3437e28e`). A combination no branch handles returns −1, which FreeCAD reports as "malformed". Silico's format makes each form explicit and the gate refuses what doesn't fit, so the solver never sees a malformed constraint.

Values: lengths in mm, angles in radians (Silico's file stores degrees, converted at the worker boundary), signed where the type says so: `DistanceX` is x(second) − x(first), and angles are counter-clockwise. Radius, diameter, and weight must be positive and a distance non-zero, but FreeCAD checks that only when a value is edited (`setDatum`, `85b2fec8f6`), never when a constraint is added.

**Choices made from the current geometry.** These make the same document solve differently from different starting coordinates, so Silico stores each choice in the constraint:
- An endpoint tangency or perpendicularity stores which of its two directions it means in its value (tangent −π/2 or +π/2, perpendicular π/2 or 3π/2). The value 0 (files from before `3838bddcdb`, 2014) means "decide from the geometry at every solve". FreeCAD decides once when the constraint is added (`AutoLockTangencyAndPerpty`).
- A line–circle tangency and point–line, circle–line, and circle–circle distances keep the side they started on in 1.1.3 only because the solver starts there. Main stores the side (`Orientation`) and makes the distances signed. 1.1.3's PlaneGCS can't hold a side through a large change (`testCircleToLineDistanceOriented` is main's test of exactly that). A circle–circle tangency's side is Silico's to give, not read from the geometry as `System::addConstraintTangent(Circle&, Circle&)` reads it (`GCS.cpp:1111-1125`), but FreeCAD reaches the same place: it sends `outside` whenever each centre starts outside the other circle (`d ≥ r1` and `d ≥ r2`, `GCS.cpp:1151`), and if other constraints drive them together, the centres cross under 1e-7 apart, where `ConstraintTangentCircumf::error` (`7023e83843`, #24227) measures r1 − r2 whatever the side, and 1.1.3 lands on two equal circles on one centre and accepts them (measured on the oracle: c1 held at the origin with its radius dimensioned 10, c2 r 12 drawn 30 apart, tangent, then c2's centre dimensioned onto the origin: `solve()` 0, both r 10 at the origin; with a coincident on the centres in place of the dimensions it lands on both r 12.64 at x 27.8, accepted too). Silico refuses that solution on purpose, as an invalid result naming the tangency and both curves: concentric circles touch outside only at radius 0.
- A symmetry of an arc's endpoints about a line through its centre adds only the midpoint row (`365af33fc6`), decided from the current geometry. Silico's `symmetric` stores no such choice: M3.3's translation decides it from the references alone, never from where the geometry is drawn: an arc's own ends about a line its centre is held on by the sketch itself (as an end of that line, or by an `on` of the centre on it). FreeCAD's test from the drawing also under-counts once the centre comes off the line (the oracle: a degree of freedom more than the sketch has); about a line nothing holds the centre on, Silico sends both rows, and a centre the other constraints put on the line makes the perpendicular row redundant, which decision 5 checks.
- B-spline curve parameters are seeded from the closest point.

### Reference constraints, inactive constraints

A reference (driven) constraint's value is an output: in 1.1.3 its value is an unknown (`bc85670ce9`, 2015), and since `d4bfa82161` (2025-11, in 1.1.3) reference constraints sit in the lower-priority subsystem, so any independent part with both driving and reference constraints is solved by SQP; on main it's evaluated from the solved geometry (`92894ea3a8`). It never counts toward degrees of freedom or conflicts (`96f6c46949`, `7055b5ef0f`). A driven angle is written back reduced with `fmod(v, 2π)` (`ba78b194b3`, after two other reductions). An inactive constraint, and a `Block`, adds no rows. **Silico evaluates a reference constraint's value from the solved geometry, as main does, rather than solving for it as 1.1.3 does** (decided in M3.2's review, 2026-10-16): the reference rows never put a part under SQP, and a value may be past what the worker holds. A deliberate divergence from the oracle's release, and not only in the reference's value: in 1.1.3 a reference's stored value is an unknown in SQP's lower-priority part, so it pulls free geometry toward itself (the oracle: a line from the origin drawn to (10, 3), 10.44 long, made horizontal, solves to (10, 0) alone, to (10.2202, 0) with a reference length of its drawn 10.44, and to (13.6356, 0) with a reference of 20), where Silico, as main, leaves the geometry where the driving constraints put it. So the oracle's sketch cases with reference dimensions are fully constrained, where the two agree. M3.3 reproduces 1.1.3's other reference outputs: a reference angle reduced into (−2π, 2π) with `fmod(v, 2π)` (`ba78b194b3`, `eefc51ee7a`), and a reference diameter reads twice the radius even where the radius is fixed (`727dc9fa9e`, in 1.1.3: before it, a diameter whose radius was fixed read twice the diameter, 4r; evaluating from the solved geometry gives it by construction).

### Tags

**One sketch constraint is one tag: its index + 1, so reports name constraints.** FreeCAD keeps a running counter instead, and that broke the mapping at least four times: untagged coincidences (`7cbb16f2e8`), a knot alignment that didn't count (`bffb82d531`), a parabola axis that counted twice (`cdf0097dec`), and skipped constraints (`20a83b82f6`). Main still shifts every later tag when a constraint is malformed. Silico sets each tag from the constraint's position, never a counter.

### Solving, and what a solve means

FreeCAD's `SketchObject::solve` (`SketchObjectConstraints.cpp:78-183`) diagnoses first and then:

| Outcome | When | Solved? | Recompute |
|---|---|---|---|
| Over-constrained | degrees of freedom < 0 | no | error, with the conflicting constraints |
| Conflicting | conflict groups | no | error, with them |
| Malformed | a constraint the solver couldn't build | no | error, with them (`150e50c700`, `2bd2041bfe`: they used to be ignored) |
| Redundant | redundant constraints, none of the above | yes, but **the geometry isn't written back** | error, with them |
| Solver failure | DogLeg, then Levenberg–Marquardt, BFGS, and SQP (anchoring every unknown at its start with tag −1) all fail to reach `Success` | | error |
| Invalid solution | a solve succeeded but OCCT refuses the geometry it describes; rolled back, next solver tried (`fc128d357d`, `2b88396504`) | | error if every solver ends there |
| Success | | yes | the geometry is written back, and "fully constrained" is degrees of freedom = 0 (`b7945b6a15`) |

Partially redundant is a warning only (`b01313c0d8`) when the geometry needn't move; when it must, PlaneGCS checks the dropped equation on the starting values and the solve fails (the oracle: a point coincident with the origin and given its x twice, drawn at (1, 2), gives `solve()` −1). On an error FreeCAD keeps the previous shape; Silico's recompute fails the feature (SolidWorks' behavior, and recompute's since M2.4). Every attempt starts from the stored coordinates, and a failure leaves them as they were. Redundant constraints are never deleted on recompute; FreeCAD's auto-removal is an editor preference, off by default.

**Silico differs on redundancy:** SolidWorks solves an over-defined sketch whose extra dimensions agree and marks them, and FreeCAD's refusal to write back a solve it did makes a model's result depend on whether a constraint is spelled twice. Silico follows SolidWorks: a sketch whose only problem is redundancy solves and keeps its result, and the redundant constraints come back as data (a warning naming them). Since PlaneGCS calls constraints redundant when they disagree by up to 1e-5, keeping its result would quietly take one of two dimensions that disagree: Silico's own tolerance for "agree" is decision 5: each redundant constraint's gap, the least distance a point would move for it to hold, within max(1e-8 mm, 1e-9 × the extent of the part of the sketch the constraint is in, the diagonal of the box around its points, 1e-9 mm² over T, the least d + (r1 ± r2) of that part's circle-to-circle tangencies, since PlaneGCS measures those in mm², not counting those concentric to it, T counting as 1e-5 mm at the least) (docs/design/m3.3-solving.md). A part is the unknowns the sketch's rows join, every row alike, as the drag's: PlaneGCS solves each apart (`System::initSolution`), so slack never crosses them.

### Dragging

`initMove` diagnoses once, adds tag −1 rows pinning the dragged element to the cursor (centre pins weighted 0.01 so an edge moves before its centre, `4d7a9435c1`), then each mouse move rewrites the targets and calls the solver with DogLeg only, always from the state at `initMove` (the part of the sketch holding a pin is solved by SQP, since rows tagged below 0 go to the auxiliary subsystem; DogLeg solves only the parts without one); it re-initializes after drifting 20× the first step, refuses a sketch with conflicts, and clears the temporary rows before any normal solve (`751929c87b`, `eff8529ec6`). Bugs not to copy: a `size_t` underflow writing out of bounds in relative mode (`Sketch.cpp:5495`), a B-spline centroid that averages every other pole, and dangling constraint pointers after hiding constraints mid-drag (`51b37da53e`). In Silico a drag is a session in the worker (begin, move, end), the final result a normal solve: M3.7 measures its latency.

M3.7 ports `initMove` and `moveGeometries` for one point in absolute mode (`silico_sketch::Drag`, the protocol's `DragBegin`, `DragMove`, and `DragEnd`): a begin refuses a sketch with conflicts (no session; the reply names them); it pins a point, a line's end, or a circle's or arc's centre with one coincidence to its target, and an arc's end with that plus its centre pinned where it starts, both rows rescaled by 0.01 (since M5.3, a drag session pins by the point's values, so a sketch point that arcs share as an end, the core's way, pins each of their centres once, M5.0's open item 16; extend's one move keeps FreeCAD's pins by geometry, as `extend` calls `moveGeometry` on the line); then `initSolution`, which keeps the diagnosis. A move sets the target, runs `solve(true, DogLeg)` (SQP on the pinned part), and keeps only a `Success` whose geometry can be built, as `Sketch::internalSolve` keeps a solution only if `updateGeometry` can make its curves: Silico decides it in Rust, and a line of no length to the solver or a circle or arc whose radius isn't above 0 is reported as `SolvedInvalid` with no values (the values come back only for a kept `Success`; otherwise none, and the client keeps its last frame). A drag takes at most `MAX_DRAG_UNKNOWNS` (256) unknowns in the part of the sketch it moves (the unknowns its constraints join to the points its pins hold: the dragged point, and an arc's centre for its end), not in the whole sketch, and its session holds only that part, every other value held where it began: FreeCAD's `System::solve` solves every part of the sketch again each move, which costs seconds a move beside a part that doesn't solve, and gives the same frames as Silico's from a solved sketch (the closing solve solves the rest). The whole sketch is diagnosed once at the begin, for its conflicts and degrees of freedom, as FreeCAD's; the part is diagnosed again for its own redundant equations. SQP's dense matrices and up to 100 iterations make a move of a chain linked end to end and pulled far take 0.76 s at 256 unknowns on a loaded machine, 6 s at 512, and 64 s at 1,024, past a request's time. A solve ends the session, as FreeCAD's normal solve clears `initMove`. Not ported: the re-initialization after drifting 20× the first step (`RecalculateInitialSolutionWhileMovingPoint`), which measures from `initToPoint`, a point the GUI's `initTemporaryMove` never sets, so it fires rarely and by history; dragging whole edges, groups, and relative drags, and B-splines (M5's sketch mode). So every move solves from the begin state, and its result is a function of that state and the cursor alone.

That has a cost FreeCAD shares, confirmed with its `moveGeometry` (one move from the solved sketch): far from the begin state, SQP can stop at the wrong stationary point or fail. A 10 mm line's end dragged about its fixed start to a cursor 149° round lands opposite the cursor (FreeCAD: (8.574929257125444, −5.144957554275264) for (−10, 6)), and a free arc's end dragged to (30, −40) or (−11, −7) fails in both. A smooth drag of an 8-link chain's end fails one frame in twenty (tests/drag.rs). M5 may revisit re-beginning a long drag from its last good frame. One divergence, deliberate: a frame solved to geometry that can't be built (a line of no length, a radius not above 0) is reported as `SolvedInvalid` and the client keeps its last good frame, where FreeCAD's drag reports it as a success and draws the drag's start (and keeps a radius of exactly 0). M3.7 had a second, a release where the move fails keeping its last good frame for the closing solve; since M5.3 the core's release follows FreeCAD's `commitDragMove`: the sketch is moved from the begin state to the final cursor and solved as a whole, and if that move fails the begin state is kept, as FreeCAD's returns to where it was before the drag (`silico_core::drag`).

M5.3 drags a document's sketch point by its ID (`silico_core::drag::sketch`). The begin state is the revision's solve of the sketch, the model's if it's held, or else solved on the drag's own worker; a sketch whose solve fails other than by a conflict begins where its file draws it, as FreeCAD's drag begins from its geometry. The release's whole-sketch solve is then solved again as a load is (M3.4's fixed point), so the coordinates written are ones the file gives back, and the next rebuild finds no drift. FreeCAD writes the solver's values to its geometry as they come, and has no stored coordinates to settle. Three divergences, deliberate. A sketch point that arcs share as an end pins every one of their centres, where FreeCAD's vertex belongs to one arc, whose centre alone is pinned, so its result depends on which vertex was picked: two radius-5 arcs about (∓5, 0) whose ends meet at (0, 0), dragged to (0, 10), land in 1.1.3 at (−0.000276, 9.999447) for the point, (−2.236344, 5.527311) for the picked arc's centre, and (0.178946, 5.002660) for the other, which swings free; Silico's are (0, 9.998895) and (∓2.236266, 5.526858), symmetric (the review's probe; for one arc, 1.1.3 matches δ = (d − 5)/(1 + 1e-4) to 1e-9). A release whose sketch then doesn't solve as a whole keeps the begin state, with the failure as its reason, where FreeCAD commits the move and fails at the next recompute, since a Silico file holds a solve (M3.4). And a release where the point began is a click, which moves nothing, as FreeCAD enters a drag only after the cursor moves (`6eeaae57b8`). A bug not to copy: in 1.1.3, Esc during a point's drag calls `commitDragMove(xInit, yInit)`, which a point's drag leaves at 0, so the point lands on the sketch's origin (`ViewProviderSketch.cpp:1060`); Silico's Esc keeps the begin state, as `c44158f90b` meant.

#### Dragging an edge: a whole line, a circle's or an arc's size (M5.3, open item 17)

**What FreeCAD does.** A press on an edge rather than a point drags the curve (`ViewProviderSketch::initDragging`, its position `none`), one way for each kind (`Sketch::initMove` and `moveGeometries`, `Sketch.cpp` at `876271d660`):

- **A line moves whole, relative to the press.** Both ends are pinned at full weight, each target its start plus the cursor's offset from where the press landed (`drag.relative`, set for a line since group dragging, `70972b3926`, 2024-12; ellipses and conics joined it in `2cd45b07f7`, 2026-01, after 1.1.3). The App's own absolute form puts the line's midpoint at the cursor, which is the same move made from the line's midpoint (1.1.3: `moveGeometry(0, none, (5, 4))` of (0,0)–(10,0) gives (0,4)–(10,4)). Relative mode skips the re-initialization after drifting 20× the first step (`RecalculateInitialSolutionWhileMovingPoint`, `ad855c99e3`), as Silico skips it everywhere.
- **A circle's edge sizes it.** A point held on the circle (`addConstraintPointOnCircle`, full weight) is pinned to the cursor, and the centre where it starts a hundred times more weakly, so the circle grows or shrinks to pass through the cursor before its centre moves.
- **An arc's edge** does the same on the arc's circle (`addConstraintPointOnArc`), its centre as weakly pinned. Its ends follow as the solve leaves the arc's angles, which nothing pins.
- Several selected curves drag together, rigidly, in relative mode (`70972b3926`; an arc's mid point keeps a rigid drag, `2cd45b07f7`). B-spline edges move one piece (`a88f69a9f0`, `1f4e6ad899`). Silico has neither: no group drag in M5, and no B-splines.

FreeCAD's own tests move points only (`TestSketcherSolver.py`'s `moveGeometry(…, 2, …)`, and an ellipse moved with nothing asserted), so the cases below are the oracle's.

|---|---|
| A free line (0,0)–(10,0), relative by (3, 4) | (3,4)–(13,4) |

**What Silico does (recommended).** The same, with shared points (M3.2's D2): a line's ends are points, so a neighbour that shares one follows it, as FreeCAD's coincidence pulls it.

- **A line:** both of its points pinned at full weight, each moved by the cursor's offset from the press. The drag carries the press, in the sketch's coordinates; a move's cursor is where the pointer is now. Every frame and the end are functions of the begin state, the press, and the cursor alone.
- **A circle or an arc:** a target held on its circle at full weight, moved to the cursor, and its centre pinned where it starts at 0.01. For an arc, its ends land where the solve leaves them; a drag's tests pin the radius and the centre only.
- **Refused:** an edge drag of a point, which has no edge, naming the point. A construction curve drags as any curve does, as FreeCAD's do.
- **Not ported:** group drags, B-splines, and an arc's rigid mid-point drag (M5's plan has no multi-select drag).
- **Far moves:** SQP from the begin state can stop short of a far cursor, as it can for a point (*Dragging*, above), and in both: a circle of radius 404 about (0, 48203) dragged to (0.49, 820609) is a failed frame here and "Not able to move point" in 1.1.3. One case parted them: a circle of radius 1e-6 mm (the gate's floor) dragged 860 km to its far side is a failed frame here, where 1.1.3 sizes it (radius 859512.94) from the same pins in the same order; at 1,000 mm, a billion times its radius, the two agree to the last digit (radius 999.9999990112242).

M3.7's measurements (benches/drag.rs, an M4 Pro, 300 frames of 0.05 mm, p50/p99): sketches of the stress corpus's sizes (up to about 100 constraints) drag well inside a 60 Hz frame, 16.7 ms; a 6×6 grid of rectangles (576 unknowns, where the dragged point's part is small) 0.045/0.084 ms per move against 82/157 ms for a full solve; a 120-link chain (480 unknowns, one part; measured before the drag limit, which now refuses it) 71/166 ms, slower than a full solve's 47/119, since SQP's dense work outweighs the diagnosis it saves. The round trip through the worker adds about 0.02–0.07 ms, so neither shared memory nor an in-process solver would help. **Decided (the maintainer, 2026-10-16):** PlaneGCS stays linked in the worker, with the drag session; no port, no shared memory, no in-process solver. For big connected sketches the UI (M5) solves only the newest cursor, one request in flight, and drops stale frames, which leaves each frame a function of the begin state and the cursor alone. The gap to commercial solvers is algorithmic, not transport (ROADMAP.md, after the slice).

## The sketch feature (SketchObject)

- **The shape:** the solved, non-construction geometry, in geometry order; edges joined into wires greedily (an edge joins where one of its ends is strictly within 1e-7 of any vertex already in the wire, not only a free end; the joined vertex moves and its tolerance grows; `TopoShapeExpansion.cpp` `makeElementWires`; docs/ports/profile.md), each wire passed through `ShapeFix`; non-construction points become free vertices; one wire, or a compound. An empty sketch has an empty shape and no error; an edge shorter than 1e-7 only warns. Bugs fixed: the shape built from stored instead of solved geometry (`d2637ec881`), every edge added twice (`46a11b6538`), null edges from degenerate geometry and a crash on lone points (`4e5f0a3850`, `c8046b1c60`).
- **Validation in FreeCAD is weak.** `evaluateConstraint` checks only that geometry indexes are in range (`SketchObjectConstraints.cpp:2172-2233`; `7d8b1f30c3`, `6a211b9dbe`, `6a1cb47e30`, `d83c02387c` after crashes). Kinds, positions, and arity reach the solver and come back malformed. Worse, while any index is out of range `Constraints.getValues()` returns **an empty list** (`PropertyConstraintList.h:106-111`, `c35d8be9a3`), so the sketch solves with no constraints at all and only a console line says why. Silico's gate refuses instead, naming the constraint and the reference: every reference exists and is of a kind and position the constraint's form accepts, internal-alignment indexes are in range, values are finite and in range, and a driving dimension names at least one geometry of the sketch's own (`3d7871fca5`).
- **Restore is lenient.** Unknown constraint types are dropped, shifting later indexes (`462ec49297`); exceptions are swallowed; duplicate geometry ids are renumbered; a missing external reference keeps its constraints without error (`27ca64a201`). Silico's strict reader refuses each of these, except that a broken external reference is an error naming it, constraints kept.
- **Deleting geometry** (an editor operation, but the behavior SolidWorks users expect): constraints on it go, later indexes shift down (`0016d6f2f8`, `aeabffeed8`: every element slot, not just the first two), and coincidences through a deleted point are re-pointed so its neighbours stay joined (`SketchObjectConstraints.cpp:1036-1247`). With stable IDs Silico doesn't renumber, but keeps "neighbours stay joined" when the command API deletes.
- **Stored geometry is the last solve** (M3.4, docs/design/m3.4-stored-coordinates.md): `execute()` solves from the geometry the document holds and writes the solve back (`solve(true)`, `SketchObject.cpp:353`), so a saved file holds its last solution, and the next solve starts there. Silico solves every sketch on every rebuild and reports a solve that differs from the file as drift, so a solution must be its own. Measured over the stress corpus: DogLeg and Levenberg–Marquardt return their own solutions bit for bit (0 of 178 moved); **BFGS alone doesn't** (28 of 29 moved, up to 6e-12 relative, most still moving at a third solve); DogLeg, first in a load's order, returns BFGS's solutions untouched (0 ulps). Left: a sketch whose DogLeg and Levenberg–Marquardt both fail from BFGS's solution would drift on every load, loudly; none did in 347 runs, and FreeCAD shares it.
- **Names:** the sketch's own names are `Edge<n>` counting construction geometry, `Vertex<n>`, `ExternalEdge<n>`, `H_Axis`, `V_Axis`, `RootPoint`; the shape's indexed edges follow wire assembly, so FreeCAD's later features name them through the element map, whose names are each geometry's facade ID (`g<id>`, a vertex `g<id>v<pos>`, with the `SKT` op code), not `Edge<n>`. FreeCAD reuses a geometry id when a new curve's endpoints match a deleted one (`geoHistoryLevel`): a heuristic. Silico names sketch edges from their stable IDs (M2.7).

Out of scope for M3: the editing tools (trim, extend, split, fillet, offset, mirror, arrays, carbon copy), external geometry projected from other features (it needs naming), `Block`, `SnellsLaw`, and the `SketchAnalysis` checks.

## Profiles to faces (FaceMakerBullseye)

PartDesign makes faces with **FaceMakerBullseye** (`AllowMultiFace`, set for every new feature, `FeatureSketchBased.cpp:144-147`; the older FaceMakerCheese has no islands and only serves old files; the switch was `7786e24fcd`, #6366):

1. Every wire must be closed: OCCT's `BRep_Tool::IsClosed`, a **vertex-parity** test, so a T-junction or two loops sharing an edge (one wire after the greedy assembly) is "Wire is not closed.", and a figure-8 passes.
2. The plane is fitted (`BRepLib_FindSurface`), "Wires are not coplanar" if none fits; its normal's sign is arbitrary. PartDesign takes the extrusion direction from the sketch's placement, never from the face.
3. Wires are sorted largest first by the squared diagonal of their bounding box, with a tolerance (1e-7) that isn't a strict weak order (`std::stable_sort` keeps tied wires in input order).
4. Each wire, in that order, is classified against the faces made so far by **one vertex** (`FaceDriller::hitTest`): on a face's material it becomes a hole there; inside a hole it becomes a new face (an island); otherwise a new face. That's even–odd nesting to any depth. The loop runs newest first without stopping at a hit, so the oldest face that hits wins. In a sketch, separate wires never share a vertex (assembly would have joined them): what decides is whether a wire's first vertex lies on a face's outer boundary (a separate, overlapping face) or on a hole's boundary (another hole of that face) (`2161725848`; 1.1.3 `FaceMakerBullseye.cpp:132-147`, the hole case `:244-251`). Loops that share a point become one wire, which Bullseye takes as one outline: a hole at a corner gives 110.5 where 89.5 is right.
5. Outer wires are made counter-clockwise about the plane and holes clockwise, whatever the input's orientation (`getWireDirection`; `9c17fe359b` fixed a crash when its face failed).
6. Stand-alone points are ignored (`26ebefd651`). An empty result is "Cannot make face from profile". Several disjoint regions give several faces, and a pad then makes several solids.

**FreeCAD never checks for wires that cross, overlap, or touch.** Classifying one vertex gives an invalid face (a warning, after `ShapeFix`), two faces that pad later fuses into their union, or a face OCCT calls valid with the wrong area (a hole crossing the boundary gives 80 where the union is 110; two overlapping rectangles give −0, and a zero-volume pad, `Valid`). SolidWorks refuses such a contour. Silico refuses it as data, naming the sketch elements that cross or touch: a deliberate divergence. Silico also passes the sketch's plane rather than fitting one, and orders regions and loops by their curves' IDs (docs/design/m3.5-profile.md): nesting comes from containment, so the order only names the faces.

**Region profiles (a sketch's internal faces).** FreeCAD has a second path. With a sketch's `MakeInternals` on (1.1.3: off by default, `SketchObject.cpp:168`; main: on for new sketches since `be1d53cf5f`, 2026-03), the sketch also builds `InternalShape`: crossing and overlapping geometry split into regions, `InternalFace1..n` (1.1.3: `FaceMakerRing` over a `WireJoiner`, `:485`; main: `FaceMakerBuildFace`, `24ab301685`, after the old pipeline missed regions where three or more shapes overlap, and `0939408c21` for self-intersecting B-splines and dangling edges). A pad can take one region as its profile (`Profile = (sketch, ["InternalFace1"])`, resolved in `FeatureSketchBased.cpp:216-246`). The oracle confirms it: two overlapping rectangles, (0,0)–(20,10) and (10,5)–(30,15), give three regions of 50, 150, and 150 mm², and a pad of a 150 mm² region by 5 is a valid 750 mm³ solid. Fusion picks profile regions the same way, and SolidWorks has Contour Select. So refusing crossing profiles (below) is the whole-sketch profile's rule; choosing regions of a crossing sketch is a separate, later capability (open decision 8, docs/roadmap/m3-sketcher.md). Main's `TestSketchInternalFaces.py` (45 tests: T-junctions, figure-8 B-splines, cross patterns, dangling chains, three overlapping circles, names stable after recompute) is its test suite.

FreeCAD's tests (`tests/src/Mod/Part/App/FaceMakerBullseye.cpp`, added in `88c80b3ed8`, 2026-04, after 1.1 but testing behavior 1.1 has) are analytic: a 4 × 3 rectangle (12), with a hole of radius 0.5 (12 − π/4), several holes, nested islands (two faces, 100 − 16π + 4π), 25 holes in a 100 × 100 square (10000 − 25π), a tilted plane, reversed wires. None tests an error.

## Pad, pocket, revolve (PartDesign, 1.1)

M3.6's port note, docs/ports/sketch-features.md, covers these in full and corrects this summary where noted.

- **Direction:** pad along the sketch's normal, pocket along its negation; `Reversed` flips the final direction once (`Direction` shows it unreversed: `9283aa0f72` reverted `1da079b34b`'s write). Length must not total zero (`|L + L2| < 1e-7`, "Cannot create a pad with a total length of zero.", `a346c266e7`, after `6167e9f77a`); negative lengths extrude backwards (the per-side minimum was removed in `b2520d489a`). SolidWorks refuses a depth ≤ 0 and offers reverse; Silico follows SolidWorks.
- **Modes:** one side; symmetric (**one** prism from −L/2 to +L/2, not two fused, `c384a52fa5`); two sides (one prism from +L back to −L2, `a01cab21cc`); through all for pocket (2.02 × the diagonal of the base and the profile's box, `43de589732`); up to face and up to first later.
- **Pocket with no base** silently becomes its tool (since `ecf7e51ab3`), and a groove with no base its revolved solid (`df5c75d6b0`). Silico refuses both.
- **Revolve modes in 1.1.3:** revolution has `Angle`, `UpToLast`, `UpToFirst`, `UpToFace`, `TwoAngles` ("Angles of revolution nullify each other" when they sum to zero); groove has `Angle`, `ThroughAll`, `UpToFirst`, `UpToFace`, `TwoAngles`. A revolution's `UpToLast` is documented as its `ThroughAll` (`FeatureRevolution.h:86-94`), but `df5c75d6b0` dropped its branch, so 1.1.3's code throws "Unknown method for generateRevolution()" (read, not run). Up to first is refused ("not yet supported"). M3.6 builds `Angle` and symmetric; the rest wait. Main's two-sided revolve with signed angles is newer.
- **Revolve:** angle in (0°, 360°] ("too large", "too small" below 1e-12 rad, `3694da3102`, `c0de42335b`); right-handed about the axis, which `Reversed` reverses (a profile at +X about +Y turns toward −Z); symmetric turns the profile by −angle/2 first. The axis is a sketch axis (`H_Axis`, `V_Axis`), the n-th construction line segment (`AxisN`), an origin axis, or a line or circle edge (a sketch's edges included); it must not be parallel to the sketch's normal (|cos| at 1e-12, both senses, `501c3dc657`, `fdf66b3e88`); it may touch the profile or run along an edge, but not cross it ("Revolve axis intersects the sketch", `8371982dfc` and the 2012 refinements). Groove is a revolve that cuts.
- **The result** fuses with or cuts the base, with an automatic fuzzy value (1e-6 × the diagonal, `cf8ad66373`). The single-solid rule ("Result has multiple solids", counting every solid since `eb52dd4a9a`) applies only when the body's `AllowCompound` is false, and it's true by default since `406c3ba42c`.
- **Refine** has been on by default since `e490531510` (2024): oracle comparisons of face and edge counts set it explicitly.

## Where Silico differs on purpose

- PlaneGCS at 1.1.3, the oracle's, not main: none of main's signed constraints, and not its status regression.
- Constraint forms explicit in the file, refused at the gate when they don't fit; never "malformed" at solve time; never a hidden, unconstrained solve.
- Every choice FreeCAD makes from the current geometry (tangency direction, sides) stored in the constraint.
- Tags from constraint positions, never a counter.
- Redundant-only sketches solve and keep their result, the redundancy reported (SolidWorks), where FreeCAD fails the feature, with Silico's own tolerance for constraints that agree (decision 5, docs/design/m3.3-solving.md).
- Crossing, overlapping, and touching profile wires refused, naming the elements.
- No drawn size of 1e-7 mm or less (OCCT's `Precision::Confusion()`), refused at the gate and on every solve, construction geometry too: a line's ends, a circle's radius, an arc's centre to its start and to its end, and its chord, measured as the kernel request measures a curve (`Curve::size_problem`). FreeCAD accepts any line whose ends are apart by more than `gp::Resolution()` (`GeomLineSegment`, `Geometry.cpp:4755` at `e8f826d0ed`) and only warns "Edge too small" at 1e-7 or less (`SketchObject.cpp:316-348`); its solver then miscounts: below about 1e-13 mm PlaneGCS's rank check takes a tiny line's rows for zero (a line of 1e-50, 1e-20, or 1e-14 mm: DoF 3, solve -2; 1e-12 and up: DoF 1), a silently wrong result. Silico refuses at the kernel's own zero, as M3.5's profile does (the M3 review, round 1). So FreeCAD's collapsed line of `TestCoincidentCommandGui` (its ends 1e-10 apart once Levenberg-Marquardt solves it) is an invalid result in Silico, naming the line.
- An arc whose start and end are at one angle from its centre but not at one place passes the gate, which refuses only ends drawn at one place. An example is an end left at another radius, as a solve or a trim can leave one 2e-7 mm off its circle. Such an arc has no sweep (`arc_from_points` makes none), so the solve's translation refuses it, naming the arc, as M3.3 decides. The profile refuses it again after a solve, so it never reaches the kernel or a measure. FreeCAD 1.1.3, whose arc's ends at one angle are always at one place, treats an arc at one angle two ways (probed 2026-10-27, each sketch recomputed):
  - With equal angles, `ArcOfCircle` raises `Geom_TrimmedCurve::U1 == U2`. A coincidence of an arc's start with its end is redundant (solve −2) and is stored at equal angles, so the sketch fails to build ("Error build geometry", `Touched, Invalid`).
  - With angles exactly a whole turn apart, the arc builds as a full circle: one closed edge, 2π r long, whose pad is a valid solid. Silico refuses that one on purpose, since its arc is its points, and the gate's refusal says to draw a circle. A FreeCAD importer will meet this divergence.

  Every Silico path refuses the arc as data, so the gate is left as it is.
- Pad and pocket depth > 0, with `reversed`; pocket without a base refused.
- No heuristics: no weight nudge, no geometry-id reuse by endpoint matching, no auto-removal, no fix-up of faces.
