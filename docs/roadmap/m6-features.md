# M6 – Modeling features

**Status: draft, for the maintainer (2026-11-09).** Written from ROADMAP.md's *After the slice* (holes, loft, sweep, helix, draft, shell, patterns, mirror) and the maintainer's ask of 2026-11-09 ("other modeling tools like patterning"). Its other sources:

- docs/ports/patterns.md (#280). Its naming, a face's direction, and its refusals were decided by Fable on 2026-10-29, with the maintainer's veto open. Its kernel layer is built (#291), and every boolean since runs with FreeCAD 1.1.3's automatic fuzzy value (#383).
- ARCHITECTURE.md: invariants 2, 3, 5, and 9, *Topological naming*, and the OCCT patch policy.
- The M4.1 and M5.0 designs, and docs/ux/.
- FreeCAD's PartDesign in ~/Git/FreeCAD, main at `876271d660`. That's the port note's baseline: nothing has touched the pattern files or their tests since, and every hash the note cites resolves.
- The maintainer's call of 2026-11-09: the app no longer makes primitives, and agents are steered to build sketch-first. The core, the format, and MCP still read primitives. MCP's half is in progress on Main's branch `mcp-sketch-first`.

It's built after M5's slice closes (M5.12, then M5's milestone review), as ROADMAP.md requires. Each decision at the end carries a recommendation. M6.0 turns them, as taken, into the first family's design, which the maintainer approves before anything else is built.

**Goal:** a person in the app and an agent over MCP repeat and mirror what they've built, sketch first:

- a hole pocketed once and patterned along one or two directions or around an axis;
- half a part mirrored;
- an edge of copy (2, 1) chamfered by its name, that name found again after the pattern's spacing or its base changes, or failing loudly, never re-pointed.

Then come the features printed and machined parts need next, in the order below: shell and hole in M6, then draft, sweep, loft, and helix (open decision 1). Each is:

- a document type through the one gate;
- a command first, so MCP has it before the app;
- built by a kernel request that returns a shape and its raw history (invariant 2);
- named from that history in the core;
- compared with FreeCAD 1.1.3.

**Exit tests:**

- **The plate (spec below), built through MCP** from a recorded session, and **in the app** by a scripted `egui_kittest` session. Each rebuilds to the same fingerprints, feature for feature; its volume is 29,400 + 352.395833π ≈ 30,507.084161 mm³ by hand; and its 3MF and STEP pass M4.8's checks.
- **The oracle:** every case in `tests/oracle/patterns.silico` matches FreeCAD 1.1.3, with `Refine = False`.
- **Scheme 5 still reads:** `naming-scheme-5.silico` is read under scheme 6 with every reference found, and `silico fmt` changes its `naming` line alone.
- **Shell and hole:** each one's first cases match by hand and against FreeCAD.
- **By hand:** the maintainer builds the plate in the app, recorded in `docs/walkthrough/m6.md`.
- **Review:** the council approves with `/adversarial-review --milestone`.

## The order

| Place | Feature | Kernel risk | Naming risk | User value | Why here |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Patterns and mirror | Low, and built: one boolean of placed copies, deterministic on both platforms (#291) | The highest of all, and decided. A copy reaches the solid from an origin the solid already holds, which breaks N2's tree, so the grammar gains a form (scheme 6) | High: bolt circles, grids, symmetric parts | The kernel is done and the naming decided. Each later feature that makes a tool (hole, sweep, loft, helix) can be patterned as it lands |
| 2 | Shell | Medium, and known. It's one OCCT call; patch 0001's regression has a posted fix; offsets fail where the thickness passes a face's curvature. FreeCAD's file: 28 commits, 10 of them fixes by subject; 11 tests on main | Low. Each offset face comes from one face, and each rim face from a removed face's edge | High: enclosures and printed housings, and nothing in Silico can hollow a part today | Its debt is known and its fix posted. It takes faces by name, which M2.8 and M5.9 already find |
| 3 | Hole | Low. The core draws the profile, and the kernel's revolve and cut build it, so there's no new request. FreeCAD's file: 169 commits, 68 fixes, mostly its tables and modelled threads | Medium. Its faces come from a profile the core draws, not a user's sketch, so they need names by role and sketch point | High: every mechanical part, and the pattern's commonest seed | After patterns, so a hole can be patterned at once. After shell, because a pocketed circle and a groove already make most holes, while nothing makes a shell |
| 4 | Draft | Medium to high. `DraftAngle` refuses faces next to fillets and curved neighbours. FreeCAD's file: 61 commits, 19 fixes; 1 test | Low: each drafted face is modified one from one | Medium: moulded and cast parts, but little for printing | It's small, and reuses the pattern's direction references and the shell's face sets |
| 5 | Sweep | High: `MakePipeShell`'s orientation modes, twist, self-intersection. FreeCAD's file: 77 commits, 28 fixes; 2 tests | Medium to high: walls generated from two sources, a profile edge and a path edge | Medium to high: pipes, handles, channels | It needs a path, an open chain read from a sketch, which the profile refuses today. Helix reuses its machinery |
| 6 | Construction planes, then loft | Medium. `ThruSections` is steadier than a pipe, but its sections' vertices must be matched, or the loft twists. FreeCAD's file: 64 commits, 26 fixes; 11 tests | Medium to high: walls named by two sections' edges, paired by the core | Medium | Its sections sit on planes Silico can't place yet (open decision 9) |
| 7 | Helix | The highest. A helix is a B-spline (or a 2D line on a cylinder) swept by a pipe with an auxiliary spine, and the stress test has no helix, so its determinism is unproven. FreeCAD's file: 79 commits, 33 fixes; 8 tests | As sweep's, plus the seams between turns | Low to medium: springs, and Hole's modelled threads | It builds on sweep, and its determinism is proven in the stress test first |

The FreeCAD counts are from main: commits to the file, those whose subject says fix, crash, regression, wrong, or fail, and the `def test` lines in its `PartDesignTests` file. The 605-case stress test already holds shells, offsets, lofts, and sweeps (ARCHITECTURE.md, *Deterministic kernel*). Open decision 2 weighs other orders.

## What earlier milestones leave

- **The patterns note's kernel step (#291).** These are built:
  - `Op::Copies` and `check_copies`, with `MAX_COPIES` 999;
  - `solid_faces` in every result's `Topology`;
  - the fake kernel's symbolic copies.

  `/break-it` found no crash, hang, or wrong result: 999 copies in one boolean take about 9 s on a loaded Mac, against the 60 s timeout. Left over:
  - the core step, now M6.1 to M6.3;
  - the note's decisions 1, 2, and 4 (open decision 3);
  - its open question (open decision 4);
  - its two probes (M6.3);
  - the oracle file and the scheme-6 model (M6.4).
- **The fuzzy value (#383).** Every boolean, the copies' included, runs with FreeCAD 1.1.3's automatic fuzzy value, so a far copy grows it for the whole step (open decision 4).
- **Booleans that change nothing are refused** (#386), as S7 refuses a sketch feature's.
- **Fingerprints hold the centre of mass** (#389), so a mirrored copy that lands on the wrong side drifts. M6.3's and M6.4's fingerprints get this for free.
- **Primitives leave the app** (2026-11-09). FreeCAD's tests seed with `AdditiveBox` and padded squares, which M6.3 ports as sketched pads with the same volumes and topology. `copies.features` never offers a primitive, but `copies: solid` takes any base. MCP's sketch-first lines (Main's `mcp-sketch-first`, in progress) are what M6.5 extends.
- **The oracle harness builds no sketch on a face** (`crates/silico-oracle/src/features.rs`, `Plane::Face`). M6.4's cases sketch on origin planes. Shell's and hole's cases, whose seeds sit on faces, need FreeCAD's flat-face attachment taught to the harness with Silico's frame (A4's F1 and F2), in M6.10 or M6.12, whichever comes first.
- **The MCP figures are stale.** docs/mcp.md says `tools/list` gives 30 tools and about 55 KB of input schemas, and m4.9's *Size* says 49 KB. The contract holds 45 tools and about 103 KB compact, with `add_feature` at 24.7 KB. Each new feature type grows `add_feature` in every agent's context (M6.5).
- **`Candidates::of_name` is O(B) per broken name** (M4's carried low). A pattern multiplies B, so M6.3 times it at the cap. If it's too slow, the fix is its own PR with the maintainer, since its contract is protected.
- **Sketch elements aren't references outside their features yet,** so FreeCAD's sketch axes and lines can't be pattern directions or mirror planes. They're later, and additive: adding a reference kind renames nothing (patterns.md decision 3).
- **FreeCAD's main has moved on** in ways Silico doesn't follow: it moved the patterns into Part's extensions (`c334ac5062`), added skips (`cdb4624675`) and a cap (`293726c5d8`), and gave Thickness a recto-verso mode (`f4a9a68df2`). Silico follows 1.1.3, the oracle's, taking main's fixes where a port note says.
- **The timeline's marker** (M5's *Study*) stays out of M6.

## Open decisions

Each has a recommendation, for the maintainer, or for Fable under his standing brief. Decisions 3 to 7 are taken as recommended for M6.0, which writes them into docs/design/m6.0-patterns.md. Decisions 1 and 2 are the maintainer's, and change nothing in the first family.

1. **Where M6 ends.**
   - Options:
     - (a) M6 holds all eight features, ROADMAP.md's *After the slice* as one milestone.
     - (b) M6 ends after hole (M6.13), and draft, sweep, construction planes and loft, and helix become M7: outlined above, detailed when M6 ends.
   - **Recommended: (b).** M6 as drafted is fourteen PRs, about M5's size. The four after it need machinery the first families will shape: a path read from a sketch, construction planes, helices in the stress test. ROADMAP.md details one milestone at a time, and a milestone review over thirty PRs would be a heavier council than any so far.
2. **The order.**
   - Options:
     - (a) Patterns and mirror, shell, hole, draft, sweep, loft, helix.
     - (b) Hole before shell: hole's kernel risk is the lowest of the six (no new request), and it's the pattern's commonest seed.
     - (c) By kernel risk, highest first (helix, sweep), as ROADMAP.md's "biggest risks first".
   - **Recommended: (a).**
     - Shell leads the rest because nothing in Silico can hollow a part, its debt is known and its fix posted, and it takes faces by names M2.8 and M5.9 already find.
     - Hole comes next because a pocketed circle and a groove already make most holes.
     - (c)'s risks are real, but sweep and helix need machinery first, and the stress test already holds pipes and lofts.
3. **The port note's recommendations** (its decisions 1, 2, and 4).
   - Options:
     - (a) As recommended:
       - three types sharing `copies` and the kernel's `Copies`, with no MultiTransform;
       - originals are pads, pockets, revolves, and grooves, or the whole base (`solid`), and each later feature that makes a tool (hole, sweep, loft, helix) joins as it lands; fillets, chamfers, booleans, and patterns are refused, naming them;
       - extent and spacing, each positive, the sense in `reversed`, with no per-gap spacings.
     - (b) FreeCAD's set: dress-ups patterned as what they added and removed, at two more booleans for each original.
   - **Recommended: (a).** A fillet naming each copy's edges does what a patterned fillet does. SolidWorks' geometry pattern refuses faces merged with the part. A pattern of a pattern is, for now, a whole-solid pattern or mirror of the result. *Taken as recommended (M6.0).*
4. **A base split under the fuzzy value** (patterns.md, *Open questions, for PR B*). A copy far from the base grows the boolean's box, and so its fuzzy value, for the whole step. In the note's case, a pin r 2 on a 20 × 20 plate, copied 1e-3 along x and a kilometre away, the seed's pin parts from its plate: three solids where the union is two. FreeCAD 1.1.3 does the same.
   - Options:
     - (a) Name instances as off the base only while the base's faces all lie in one solid. When they don't, refuse the step as a base in pieces, naming no instance, with the fuzzy value and the pattern's span in the message.
     - (b) Name the instances against the solid that holds most of the base's faces.
     - (c) Never name instances: refuse every result of more than one solid alike.
   - **Recommended: (a).** It names an instance only where the rule is exact (a fuse never splits its base unless the fuzzy value does), and refuses loudly where it isn't. Loosening it later breaks no document. *Taken as recommended (M6.0).*
5. **Where an instance's place is read** (M6.5, M6.7). The app's skip boxes, and its marks for refused and idle instances, need each instance's place. So does an agent choosing `C2,1`.
   - Options:
     - (a) No read: the app computes places from the document and `elements`' description of the reference.
     - (b) A `pattern` read, as `sketch` is for a sketch: the resolved direction, axis, or plane in 3D, and each instance's index, placement, anchor, and state, from the model's kept data, asking no worker.
     - (c) The same, in `timeline`'s entry.
   - **Recommended: (b).** (a) puts the core's arithmetic in a client, where it can drift from what the rebuild placed. (c) puts up to a thousand entries in every timeline read. It's a new command and tool, so its snapshot and `tools.json` are the maintainer's. *Taken as recommended (M6.0).*
6. **Symmetric patterns.** Fusion's *Direction* offers Symmetric, and so does its circular *Distribution*. SolidWorks has Direction 2's *Symmetric* and bidirectional circular patterns. Instances on the far side need negative offsets in their names, which scheme 6 doesn't have.
   - Options:
     - (a) Not in M6: added later as a superset, scheme 7 reading 6 as 6 reads 5.
     - (b) Negative offsets in scheme 6 now, before it ships, with the feature later.
   - **Recommended: (a).** Reading as is makes a superset bump cheap (patterns.md decision 10), and a grammar that accepts names no feature makes is surface no test can reach. *Taken as recommended (M6.0).*
7. **The dialogs' *Type*** (M6.6). Fusion opens each pattern command with its Type set, and lets a person "Select a different pattern Type on the fly" while the dialog is open. In Silico a type is the feature's, and `set_field` can't change it: switching replaces the feature and renames every copy.
   - Options:
     - (a) Fusion's three commands and its *Type* row, live while a new pattern is being made, where no name exists yet to break, and fixed in Edit Feature.
     - (b) Three dialogs with no *Type* row.
     - (c) The row live in Edit Feature too, the replacement listing the names it breaks.
   - **Recommended: (a).** It keeps Fusion's layout and SolidWorks' behavior: the count includes the original, and a skip goes by instance. Neither has a *Compute Type*, since Silico only copies the tool, or a mirror's *Operation*: Join only, until bodies. *Taken as recommended (M6.0).*
8. **Shell, and OCCT patch 0001** (M6.8; the patch policy; the stress reference is protected). Patch 0001 (PR #1600) iterates arc-join offsets in the order they were created, for determinism. It brought a regression: shelling away a blind hole's floor is invalid in every run, where FreeCAD 1.1.3's OCCT, without the patch, fails about one run in ten, by memory address. A fix was posted on the PR (2026-10-14).
   - Options:
     - (a) Fold the fix into patch 0001, probed first, re-recording the stress entries it moves with the maintainer's OK.
     - (b) Keep 0001 as it is, and let the result check refuse the invalid shell.
     - (c) Drop 0001.
   - **Recommended: (a),** if the probe shows it valid and deterministic on both platforms. 0001 brought the regression, so its fix belongs inside it. (b) refuses a common enclosure operation every time. (c) gives up the offsets' determinism (invariant 5).
9. **Construction planes, before loft.** A loft's sections sit on several planes, and a sketch today is on an origin plane through the origin, or on a face.
   - Options:
     - (a) A construction plane feature (Fusion's Offset Plane, SolidWorks' Reference Plane), offset from an origin plane or a planar face by a length, sketched on, and usable as a mirror plane.
     - (b) An `offset` on a sketch's plane.
     - (c) Lofts only between planes Silico has.
   - **Recommended: (a).** One reference serves sketches, mirror, and later draft's neutral plane. (b) gives every sketch a field for loft's sake, and (c) needs a solid's face at each section's height. Its frame follows A4's F1 and F2, decided with loft.

## Notes for docs/ux

These are added in M6.0's PR. The SolidWorks pages are the ones patterns.md §6 read in full; the Fusion pages were read on 2026-11-09.

**docs/ux/solidworks.md, §5, a new *Patterns and mirror*:**

- **A linear pattern** has one or two directions. Each direction takes:
  - "a linear edge, line, axis, dimension, planar faces and surfaces, conical faces and surfaces, circular edges, and reference planes";
  - Reverse Direction;
  - a spacing, "the spacing between pattern instances";
  - a number of instances, which "includes the original features or selections".

  Direction 2 adds *Pattern seed only* and *Symmetric* ([Linear Patterns](https://help.solidworks.com/2026/english/SolidWorks/sldworks/t_Linear_Patterns_Overview.htm), [Linear Pattern PropertyManager](https://help.solidworks.com/2017/English/SolidWorks/sldworks/hidd_lpattern.htm)). The help doesn't say which way a planar face or a circular edge sets the direction. Silico takes a face's outward normal (patterns.md decision 3).
- **Instances to Skip:** "Skips the pattern instances that you select in the graphics area … The coordinates of the pattern instance appear" ([Selecting Instances to Skip](https://help.solidworks.com/2026/english/SolidWorks/sldworks/t_selecting_instances_to_skip_patterns.htm)). The help doesn't say what becomes of a skipped instance's coordinates when a count changes. In Silico, its index never moves (decision 7).
- **Geometry pattern** "copies, but does not solve, the patterned feature. End conditions and calculations are ignored"; without it, each instance is solved ([Geometry Pattern](https://help.solidworks.com/2026/english/SolidWorks/sldworks/c_Geometry_Pattern_Concept.htm)). Silico's patterns are always geometry patterns.
- **A limit:** "The upper limit on the number of instances for patterns is 65500", and a large number is confirmed in a dialog box ([Limits on Number of Instances](https://help.solidworks.com/2026/english/SolidWorks/sldworks/c_limit_number_instances.htm)). Silico refuses more than 1,000 (decision 8).
- **A circular pattern's axis** is an "Axis, Circular edge, Linear edge or linear sketch line, Cylindrical face or surface, Revolved face or surface, Angular dimension". *Equal spacing* "Sets Angle to 360°", and otherwise the angle is the one "between each instance" ([Circular Pattern PropertyManager](https://help.solidworks.com/2026/english/SolidWorks/sldworks/HIDD_CPATTERN.htm)). Its number includes the original ([`FeatureCircularPattern5`](https://help.solidworks.com/2026/english/api/sldworksapi/SolidWorks.Interop.sldworks~SolidWorks.Interop.sldworks.IFeatureManager~FeatureCircularPattern5.html)). A second direction may be symmetric ([Bidirectional Circular Patterns](https://help.solidworks.com/2026/english/SolidWorks/sldworks/t_bidirectional_circular_pat.htm)).
- **Mirror** takes "a plane or planar face", and features, faces, or bodies. A second plane may be given, and it offers Geometry Pattern, and *Merge solids* for bodies ([Mirror Feature PropertyManager](https://help.solidworks.com/2026/english/SolidWorks/sldworks/hidd_dve_feat_mirror.htm)).
- **Splits:** "When pattern or mirror features split single bodies into multiple bodies, you can select which bodies to keep" ([Patterns and Mirroring](https://help.solidworks.com/2026/english/SolidWorks/sldworks/c_Patterns_and_Mirroring_top.htm)). The help doesn't say what a boss instance off the body does. Silico refuses it (decision 6).
