# M5 – The app

**Status: approved, its decisions taken (2026-10-29, under the maintainer's standing brief, each standing unless he vetoes it): as recommended, except open decision 4, open decision 11's look, and open decision 13's (b) and (c), whose text below is what was taken.** Written from ROADMAP.md's M5, ARCHITECTURE.md (invariants 3 and 4, *Rendering and UI*, the pool's start policy, and the drag rules planned for M5 under *Process model*), the M4.1 design's notes on what M5 owes (decisions 1, 9, 17, 22, 29, 30, 31, and 33, sections 3, 4, and 10, and section 9's table of the app's commands), M4.6's render design, and what M4 left open. The decisions at the end each carried a recommendation; M5.0 turns them, as taken, into a design for the maintainer to approve before anything else is built, as M4.1 did for the command API. Several recommendations amend an approved M4.1 decision; each says which.

**Goal:** a person does in a window what an agent does through MCP: opens a document, sketches on a plane or a face, pads, pockets, revolves, fillets and chamfers, sees each rebuild as it lands, picks faces and edges by clicking them, undoes, saves, and exports a 3MF or a STEP file. The app is one client of the core's command API (invariant 3), in a process of its own beside any `silico serve` (decision 30's (a)), and its UI thread never waits on a rebuild, a worker, or a file (invariant 4). Visuals and UI follow Fusion 360; behavior follows SolidWorks; nothing copies FreeCAD's UX.

**Exit tests:** the vertical slice end to end in the app, driven by a scripted UI test that builds the M4.9 bracket from the same spec, and its 3MF and STEP pass M4.8's checks with the bracket's volume by hand; the same bracket built through MCP rebuilds to the same fingerprints, feature for feature; the UI keeps drawing frames while a rebuild, a drag, and an export are held in the fake kernel; every new capability is a command first, with its contract snapshot, so MCP reaches it too; the maintainer builds the bracket in the app by hand, recorded in `docs/walkthrough/m5.md`; and the council approves with `/adversarial-review --milestone`.

## Study

- **SolidWorks' behavior, written down** (`docs/ux/solidworks.md`), from SolidWorks' own help and its documented behavior, never from guesses: selection and pre-selection (what highlights on hover, what a click, a Ctrl-click, and a box select take, and the selection filters); sketch inference and automatic relations (horizontal, vertical, coincident, tangent, midpoint, and when each is offered); dragging an under-defined sketch, and what a release keeps; how features react to an edit upstream, and rebuild and error feedback (the tree's error and warning marks, *What's Wrong*, a feature that fails but leaves the rest); undo and redo (what's undoable, and across what); the unsaved state, Save, and a file changed on disk while it's open. Each item is one line of behavior and its source, so a test can be written from it.
- **Fusion 360's layout, written down** (`docs/ux/fusion.md`): the browser tree, the timeline bar, the toolbar's workspaces and tabs, feature dialogs docked at the right with in-canvas manipulators, the view cube and the navigation bar, and the look (density, colour, type). Screens are described, not copied. The timeline's marker (showing the model as it stood at a feature) is out of M5: it needs a command for the model up to a feature, which no step builds, and the slice doesn't need it.
- **The viewport on `re_renderer` 0.38.1,** from Rerun's viewer, which embeds it in egui the way the app will: how its view builder draws into an egui paint callback, its outline and picking passes, and what crisp edges, silhouettes, and hidden-line display need beyond what M4.6 drew. And the per-instance picking ID M4.6's design flagged (each face a draw of its own), measured on the largest parts the corpus has. The viewport grows from `silico-render` (ARCHITECTURE.md's crate table), so agents' renders and the viewport stay one renderer (M4.6's R1). Written: `docs/study/viewport.md` (#223). Per-instance picking stays inside a frame on CTC 02, the corpus's largest part. A renderer of Silico's own, through `re_renderer`'s public traits, gives each face an ID at one mesh's cost. Today every camera move would rebuild every mesh, because the light and the eye offset are baked into vertices. Silhouettes and hidden or dashed lines aren't in `re_renderer` (open decision 4).
- **The toolkit's versions:** egui, `eframe`, `egui_tiles`, and `egui_kittest` at the versions `re_ui` 0.38.1 pins (egui and eframe 0.36.2, `egui_tiles` 0.17.1, whose `egui-wgpu` uses `wgpu` 30, as `re_renderer` does, so one `wgpu` serves the viewport and the UI). `re_ui` brings more than styling (`re_entity_db`, `re_log_types`, `re_analytics`, `re_uri`): the study checks its tree under `cargo-deny` and whether anything reaches the network, and takes only its style constants if it does. Written: `docs/study/toolkit.md` (#223). `re_ui`'s condition is nearly met: its stack is already in Silico's lock through `silico-view`, it adds OFL-1.1 of its own, and its HTTP image loader can be removed after setup. Its style constants are recommended as a judgement (open decision 11). The toolkit alone needs three `deny.toml` decisions (open decision 13).
- **`egui_kittest`,** egui's test harness: driving the app through its accessibility tree (find a button by its label, click it, read a panel's text) without comparing pixels. Written in `docs/study/toolkit.md` (#223), section 4: it does, without a GPU. Panes need an accessible label from the app, one call each. Clicks in the viewport need the `wgpu` feature and lavapipe on CI (open decision 7).
- **`docs/ports/sketch-edit.md`** (#208), from FreeCAD's sketch editing, for behavior only (what each operation does to the geometry and its constraints, never its UX): adding and removing, `SketchObject::split`, `trim`, `extend`, `delConstraintOnPoint` and how a shared point is pulled apart, and merging touching ends; their tests and the bug fixes in their history. Silico's shared points (M3.2's D2) decide the rules where FreeCAD's differ, and its open decisions go to M5.0. A sketch corner's fillet and chamfer (`SketchObject::fillet`) aren't in it: open decision 12 says whether they're in M5, and if they are, the note gains a section.

- **Inserting, moving, and suppressing features, and the finer sketch edits** (M4.1, section 5's *Not in M4*): M5.1 and M5.10. And `--merge-touching` (decision 17): M5.10.
- **The pool's rules for drags** (ARCHITECTURE.md, planned for M5): M5.3.
- **The app's undo, unsaved state, and live co-editing** (decisions 9, 1, and 30, each left to M5), and `format` inside a transaction (decision 31, left to M5 if the app keeps its unsaved state as one): open decisions 1 to 3 and 8.
- **Open lows the app makes matter more,** from the M4 close's carried-forward list. A long-lived window and frequent rebuilds turn these from rare into common, so each is fixed in the step that first leans on it:
  - the refusing pool never retries finding its worker, so an app started before the worker was built keeps the error (M5.4);
  - the per-session command queue is unbounded (M5.4: the app coalesces a burst of edits rather than queueing each);
  - the `cancelled` error code, still `internal` for a cancelled render (M4.6's open item), which the app's stop button shows (M5.4);
  - `Candidates::of_name` costs O(B) per broken name, felt where M5.8 shows a broken reference's candidates (a protected contract, so its own PR with the maintainer, if M5.8's benchmark shows it).

## Follow-ups after M5

- **A vertex picked through a thin wall** (N5, decided 2026-11-08). A vertex's picking disc, and Select Other's cast, take it toward the eye by 1.2% of the view's height (`vertex_reach`), depth alone, so a vertex behind a wall thinner than that can be picked through it and is listed unhidden; it's accepted for M5 (docs/design/m5.0-app.md, section 9, part 3a). The fix is an adjacency check, not depth alone: a vertex pick is dropped when a face nearer than an edge's reach (`edge_reach`, 2 pixels of the view's depth) that isn't one of the vertex's own faces covers it. The faces at a corner are the vertex's own, so a corner stays pickable, and a pick through a wall in front of it is dropped. The cast takes the same rule, so the hover and Select Other still agree.

## Open decisions

Each was written with a recommendation, for the maintainer at M4's close, and was taken as recommended (2026-10-29) unless its text says otherwise.

1. **The unsaved state** (M5.0, M5.4; decision 1 left it to M5). The app could save every edit at once, as M4's edits outside a transaction do, or hold a transaction as its unsaved changes. Holding one from the moment a document opens has a cost under M4.1: inside a transaction nothing is reloaded and only `commit` reads the file (section 4), so the app would never see an agent's commit, even with no edits of its own, which ARCHITECTURE.md's "the app watches open files and reloads when they change on disk" promises. Recommended: the app begins a transaction at a document's first edit, not at its open, as its unsaved state; Save commits it, Revert rolls it back, Close asks if it holds edits and rolls back before closing (decision 22 refuses `close` inside one), as SolidWorks does. With no edits held, the session reloads an agent's commit as decision 2 does today; with edits held, the app learns of a change on disk from a field of `status` the core adds (M5.4: the session's file compared off the UI thread, an amendment to section 4, which today reads the file only at `commit`), and offers what open decision 3 says.
2. **Undo** (M5.2). An amendment to decision 9, which says "no undo stack in the API": an undo stack only in the app breaks invariant 3 (the app could do what the API can't), and one over committed states is a second history beside git, which decision 9 refused for good reason. Recommended: `undo` and `redo` as commands, inside a transaction only, over its edits, each a new revision equal to an earlier state; the stack bounded in section 6's table; once committed, history is git's. Decision 9 then reads "no undo across commits".
3. **Live co-editing** (M5.0; decision 30). (a) As M4 decided: the app and `silico serve` share a document through its file, the app reloading what an agent commits when it holds no edits, and, when it holds some, told of the change (open decision 1) and offering to reload (dropping its edits) or to keep them (its commit is then refused as a conflict, decision 2, until it reloads or saves elsewhere); (b) `attach`, several clients on one session in one process, so a person watches an agent's edits live, with ownership rules on every command. Recommended: (a) for M5. The slice needs a person and an agent on one part, not at once; (b) can come after, with M4's shapes unchanged, when a reason to watch live appears.
4. **Faces and picking at scale** (M5.6, M5.7; amended by `docs/study/viewport.md`).
   - **Today.** `re_renderer`'s picking ID is per instance, so `silico-render` makes each face a mesh and a draw of its own.
   - **Per-instance picking passes on CTC 02,** this decision's first test, measured on the corpus's largest part (664 faces) with every frame waiting on the GPU:
     - CTC 02: 2.0 ms a frame against 1.0 ms for one mesh;
     - 6,640 faces: 14.6 ms of CPU a frame (28 ms with the GPU's time added), against 1.4 ms.
   - **What it doesn't pass is a camera move.** `silico-render` bakes M4.6's camera-fixed light and the eye offset (M4.6's f64 precision) into each vertex for one view. So every orbit rebuilds every mesh: 3.1 ms on CTC 02, 33 ms on 6,640 faces.
   - **Recommended** (an amendment for the maintainer): a renderer of Silico's own inside `re_renderer`'s phases, through its public `Renderer` and `DrawData` traits, with no fork. A body in one draw, each face's number in the picking layer, the light computed in its shader, and the eye offset as a per-body transform. The study's probe gave per-face IDs at one mesh's cost, and matched per-instance picking on every pixel. It ran on Metal only, and its colour wasn't compared.
   - **Taken instead: keep `re_renderer`'s meshes and move the bodies, not the camera.** M5.6 draws with `re_renderer`'s own mesh and line renderers, a mesh for each face and per-instance picking (the body the object, the face the instance), as `silico-render` does today. The camera stays fixed in the world; each frame, each body's transform is the view's rotation and the body's box centre less the eye, computed in f64 and cast to f32, its positions stored about that centre once, at upload. So a camera move rebuilds no mesh, only the edges' line batches, whose transform is batch data, as Rerun rebuilds them each frame. The light is `re_renderer`'s (`simple_lighting`), world-fixed and so fixed to the camera here, per pixel, not M4.6's formula per vertex: the look of agents' renders changes, but pixels aren't results (M4.6's R5) and no test compares them. The render worker draws the same way (R1), and `silico-render` loses its per-view baking.
     - Why: the rebuild on every orbit is caused by the baking, and moving the bodies removes it with a deletion and a transform a body a frame; CTC 02 passes this decision's own test inside a frame (2.0 ms); 6,640 faces is ten copies of the corpus's largest part; and silhouettes, the third reason for a renderer of Silico's own, are out of M5. That renderer would be about 260 lines against `re_renderer`'s pipeline descriptors, which move with its versions.
     - Silhouettes and hidden-line display are out of M5: neither is in `re_renderer`, each is a pass of its own, and Fusion's default style shows visible edges only. Edges stay blended as M4.6 draws them; hover a frame late is accepted, with Rerun's larger picking rectangle.
   - M5.7's benchmark on the corpus's largest part (CTC 02, with its 1,647 edges drawn, which the study didn't measure) stays as the check. If it misses a frame, the study's renderer, with its probe as the design, is the fallback.
     - **Measured (#385, 2026-11-04): it passes, so per-instance picking stays and the fallback isn't triggered.** The renderer's frame alone, as the app draws it, on CTC 02 (664 faces, 1,647 edges), its median in ms, every pick answered a frame later (`cargo bench -p silico-render --bench picking`). 5. **Sketches on faces in M5** (M5.9). Recommended: yes, as M5.9, before sketch mode: a SolidWorks user sketches on faces constantly, and A4's frame is decided (F1, F2), its `default_x` already reported. The alternative, origin planes only until after M5, makes the app's sketching unlike every CAD tool.
6. **The viewport's meshes** (M5.5). M4.6 already meshes each body for a view at half a pixel, on a ladder of powers of two, in a cache the sessions share (`meshes.rs`). (a) The viewport takes the same: meshed again only when the view's pixel size crosses a power of two, finer near the floor a body allows, one look for a person and an agent; (b) one mesh for each rebuild, at a deflection relative to each part's size, as FreeCAD's and Fusion's default display quality is, the viewport then meshing otherwise than `render` of the same view. Recommended: (a), which exists and keeps M4.6's R1 (one renderer, one look); (b) only if the ladder's remeshing shows as a stall while zooming.
7. **How the app is tested** (M5.4 on). Recommended: `egui_kittest` through the accessibility tree (labels, roles, values), the viewport through its ID buffer against projections by hand, as M4.6's renders are; never a screenshot compared with a stored image, which GPUs and fonts would break. The UI thread's freedom is tested by a frame counter that keeps climbing while the fake kernel holds a request. From `docs/study/toolkit.md`, section 4:
   - every pane is given an accessible label (`egui_tiles` gives none);
   - without the `wgpu` feature nothing is drawn, so a test that selects by clicking in the viewport needs `wgpu` and lavapipe on CI, or selects by name instead;
   - a UI that keeps repainting while a command runs is stepped with `step` or `run_steps`, since `run` panics past its step limit.
8. **`format` inside a transaction** (M5.4; decision 31 left it to M5). With open decision 1, a person with unsaved edits who opens a document with drift can't accept it: a commit never writes drift (decision 28), and `format` is refused inside a transaction. Recommended: `format` allowed inside a transaction, accepting the drift it names into the transaction's state, saved by its commit like any edit; outside one, as today.
9. **Drags in the command API** (M5.3; amends M4.1 sections 3 and 10). A drag's moves can't wait in the session's queue one by one, and which of them a worker solved depends on its speed. Recommended: `drag_begin`, `drag_move`, and `drag_end` as a fifth kind outside the session's queue (beside `status`, `read_logs`, `cancel`, and published reads), a drag's intermediate frames declared diagnostics like progress and pixels (section 10), never results; `drag_end` a function of the begin state and the final cursor alone: it solves the sketch dragged to the final cursor from the begin state, and if that fails, keeps the begin state and says so (never the last frame that happened to be solved); its result one edit through the gate at a new revision, undoable as any edit.
10. **Warm start** (M5.3). Workers are the process's, not a session's: kernel workers shared by every session, up to one a core, started when a request first needs one (ARCHITECTURE.md), and sketch workers lent to each rebuild. Starting every worker at each open would start a core's worth of OCCT workers in the app and again in each `silico serve`. Recommended: a client's choice at its start, not the core's at each open: the app starts one kernel worker and one sketch worker when it launches, so a person's first rebuild doesn't wait on a process starting, and the rest on demand as today; `silico serve` and the CLI unchanged. The cost, one OCCT process and one PlaneGCS process idle while the app runs, is stated in ARCHITECTURE.md's pool paragraph.
11. **The toolkit** (M5.4). Recommended: `eframe` (with `wgpu_no_default_features`, so the workspace chooses wgpu's backends), `egui_tiles`, and `re_ui`'s look, at the versions `re_ui` 0.38.1 pins, so one `wgpu` and one look serve the viewport, the UI, and agents' renders. The Rerun crates' pin moves only with `re_renderer`'s.
    - **The condition** was `re_ui` itself only if its tree is clean under `cargo-deny` and offline. `docs/study/toolkit.md` finds it nearly met:
      - its gRPC/HTTP stack is already in Silico's lock through `silico-view` and passes the gate;
      - its one failure of its own is OFL-1.1, for the Inter font, which a copied look needs too;
      - its HTTP image loader fetches nothing unless shown an `http(s)` image, and can be removed after setup.
    - **Recommended:** `re_ui`'s style constants (its theme files and the mapping onto `egui::Style`), copied with attribution (MIT/Apache, and Inter under OFL-1.1), not `re_ui`. This is a judgement against linking Rerun's data model and a gRPC/HTTP stack the app never calls, and against following `re_ui`'s widget API.
    - **Taken:** the crates as recommended, not `re_ui`. **The look changed:** only the values the app sets (the palette, panel margins, bar heights, a list item's height, and corner radii), as Rust constants in one module (`silico-app/src/theme.rs`) with Rerun's MIT/Apache notice, applied to `egui::Style` in one function of Silico's; not the theme files, a loader, or a port of `re_ui`'s mapping. The layout follows Fusion, not Rerun, so the rest of Rerun's tokens would be code to own for nothing. `eframe`'s default fonts off, and Inter (OFL-1.1) for text, with JetBrains Mono (OFL-1.1) for names, IDs, and values, as the approved prototype (docs/design/m5-ui) sets them, each notice beside its font. The look is dark, as the prototype draws it; a light theme waits for its own tokens.
12. **A sketch corner's fillet and chamfer** (M5.10; SolidWorks' Sketch Fillet and Sketch Chamfer, FreeCAD's `SketchObject::fillet`). Rounding or cutting a corner where two sketch curves share a point: an arc or a line in its place, the corner's constraints carried or dropped. (a) In M5.10, with a section of its own in `docs/ports/sketch-edit.md` first; (b) after M5. Recommended: (b). The slice's parts round their edges with the 3D fillet and chamfer M4 already builds (the bracket's inside corner, M4.9), and a sketch fillet is a port of its own: FreeCAD's has been reworked since 1.1.3 (`6d06b61c7e`, which builds it on split), so it needs its own study of which version to follow.
13. **`deny.toml` for the app** (M5.4; three maintainer calls, from `docs/study/toolkit.md`, section 2). Any app crate, with or without `re_ui`, fails today's `deny.toml` on:
    - (a) **The wgpu ban.** wgpu is allowed only through `silico-render` and `re_renderer`, "until M5's viewport", and `eframe` and `egui-wgpu` are its parents in the app. Recommended: the app crate added as a wrapper.
    - (b) **Font licences.** OFL-1.1, for Inter or any font the app ships, and Ubuntu-font-1.0 for egui's default fonts. Recommended: OFL-1.1 allowed; Ubuntu-font-1.0 only if egui's default fonts are kept. **Taken:** OFL-1.1 as a cargo-deny `exceptions` entry for the font's crate alone, so no other crate brings a font licence in unseen; Ubuntu-font-1.0 never enters, egui's default fonts being off (open decision 11). A veto line for the maintainer.
    - (c) **Three unmaintained advisories** (RUSTSEC-2026-0370, 0371, and 0372: `zvariant`, `zbus_names`, `zvariant_derive`). They come only through `eframe`'s Linux screen-reader bridge (`accesskit_unix`). The gate doesn't check advisories today (`deny.toml` defers it: it needs the network). Recommended:
    - keep screen readers on Linux, and ignore the three by name with that reason;
    - check advisories on CI, but not as an unconditional gate step, which would make the gate's verdict depend on the day RustSec publishes rather than on the tree (Worker 2's view);
    - in the gate, only when `Cargo.lock` or `deny.toml` changes against the base, so a dependency is judged when it's introduced, with its ignores in the same PR;
    - a daily job on a control runner that checks main and fails visibly on a new advisory;
    - the gate's step reads the database that job fetched (`--disable-fetch`), and offline local runs skip it.
    - Main has no hits today.
    - **Taken, narrower:** the three ignored by name with the reason ("unmaintained, reached only through eframe's Linux screen-reader bridge; not a vulnerability"), and the daily job on a control runner, checking main and failing visibly; the gate unchanged (`bans licenses sources`), with no conditional step and no shared database. A PR that brings a new advisory is flagged by the next morning's run, and its fix is a follow-up ignore or replacement. The conditional step would make the gate's path depend on a diff, for a case the daily job catches within a day.
