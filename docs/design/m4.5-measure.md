# M4.5 design: measures

M4.5 measures a model as the command API's section 7 gives it (docs/design/m4.1-command-api.md): `mass_properties`, `distance`, and `angle`. What each measure means was decided in docs/ports/measure.md (*Decided*, Fable, 2026-10-23, the maintainer's veto open), with the full record in `m4-measure-fable.md`. This note says how they're built, in three steps, each its own PR:

1. **`mass_properties`**, from each rebuild's summary, asking the kernel nothing (built).
2. **The protocol's `Op::Distance`** (#150): OCCT's `BRepExtrema_DistShapeShape` on two elements in a worker, given them in one order by their own B-reps, the witness chosen by coordinates among the pairs within OCCT's precision, "not done" a refusal as data (built).
3. **`distance` and `angle`** in the core: names found as M2.8 finds a reference, `center_distance` and `normal_distance` from `describe`'s measures, and the angles by `libm`'s `atan2` (built).

## `mass_properties`

`command::measure::mass_properties` gives each solid's volume, area, and centre of mass from its rebuild's summary (M1's `measure`, OCCT's integration asked for a relative 1e-9, decision 4, with the known wrong results docs/ports/measure.md lists; volume and area as the rebuilt fingerprint holds them, to a millionth of a mm³ and mm², so a micrometre cube's volume is 0; the centre, which no fingerprint holds, to the nanometre): a feature's solid, built, consumed or not, or each body (the solids no later feature consumed, `Model::bodies`) in timeline order. A session runs it on the model a read names, current or published (`Sessions::mass_properties`, decision 33). Its wire types are `command::wire::measure`, its snapshot `tests/contract/commands/mass_properties.json`.
- **The fingerprint's numbers.** The volume and area are `Fingerprint::of`'s, the one rounding of a summary to 6 decimals, so a measure and the document's fingerprint never disagree; the centre is rounded to the nanometre (`Length::rounded`).
- **Refused as `elements` refuses a solid** (`command::elements::built` and `summarized`, shared): a feature the document doesn't have, a sketch, or one blocked, naming `feature` when the request named it; one that failed gives its failure. A solid that couldn't be measured gives why, as `check` says it: the kernel's refusal of its values, or the worker's failure to measure it (a crash, a timeout, a reply no shape has), naming its feature, and `feature` when the request named it. Every solid that can't be measured is said, in timeline order, the first 100 listed and all counted (`Problems`).
- **Why no kernel request:** each built feature's solid is summarized as it's rebuilt (M2.6, M4.4's `Engine::measure`), and a published model holds every summary, so a read of it costs nothing and never waits on a worker.
- **Tests:** a box, a cylinder, and a sphere, each body's three numbers by hand from its dimensions; M2.1's example (its block, consumed, its pin, and the cut), by hand; the design's example written byte for byte from the example rebuilt on workers; on the fake kernel, the bodies' order, the numbers equal to the fingerprints', every refusal, and the session's choice of model.

## The angle reads 30 for a 30° wedge

Trevin's choice (2026-10-25, relayed by Main): `angle` is built exactly as the decided formula gives it, `atan2(|n1 × n2|, −n1 · n2)` over two planar faces' outward normals, with no flip. A 30° wedge's two faces read 30, and the test that pins it cites this choice.

## `distance` and `angle`

`command::measure::distance` and `angle` take two elements by name and a feature (or none); a session runs them on the model a read names (`Sessions::distance`, `Sessions::angle`). Their wire types are `command::wire::measure::PairRequest`, `DistanceAnswer`'s and `AngleAnswer`'s, with snapshots `distance.json` and `angle.json`. The design's examples are each written byte for byte: the requests read back, the two `distance` refusals by the command on the fake kernel, and the results and `angle`'s refusal from real solids rebuilt on workers (silico-kernel's tests/measures.rs).
- **Found by name** in the live solids (the bodies, in timeline order) or, with `feature`, that feature's solid, consumed or not: each name parsed as the rebuild parses names (every feature's maker, a sketch's too, since a pad's face is named from its sketch's line), then looked for in each solid's element map. An unstable name is taken (a measure is answered once and stored nowhere). Every name that isn't one, or names nothing there, is `reference_broken`, naming `elements.N`, with candidates, every one said in order; a name of a solid is refused; nothing reaches the kernel until both are found.
- **Candidates** are M2.8's (the elements whose names share the most of what the broken name is made from), for any kind, not only edges; and, for a name of an element by its number that shares nothing (`k7f2qx3m.box.Face7` of a box of six faces), the elements of its kind its feature made numbered nearest it (`Face6`), so the design's example's candidate is had. A fillet's broken edge by its number gets the same (`Edge13` of twelve: `Edge12`).
- **`distance`** asks the kernel for the least distance and its witness on the read's own kernel client (decision 33, as #125's review settled it: one client a read, dropped when it ends; `Read::kernel`), the second element's solid sent only if it's another's. `center_distance` and `normal_distance` come from each solid's description, unrounded: a vertex's point, a circle's or arc's centre (its axis's origin), and a cylinder's axis as an infinite line; points, a point and a line, or two lines (skew: the least between them; parallel, exactly, the distance of either's point from the other). `normal_distance` needs both a straight edge or a planar face, at an `angle` of exactly 0 or 180 at the nanodegree, and is the second's reference point's (a face's centre of area, an edge's midpoint) distance from the first's line or plane; but for an edge and a face, the edge's midpoint's distance from the plane, whichever comes first (from `/break-it`: Fable's rule, taken literally, measures the face's centre from the edge's line when the edge comes first, so an edge lying in a face read 0 one way and 5 the other; a correction of the decided rule's wording, raised with Main). Two cylinders' axes are parallel, for `center_distance`, as `angle` reads lines: 0 between them at the nanodegree, so axes a bit off parallel aren't taken as meeting far away (they were read 0 apart). Everything is rounded once, at the end. OCCT finding no distance is said in the design's words, naming both elements.
- **`angle`** is the core's, from the descriptions, asking the kernel nothing: two planar faces, `atan2(|n1 × n2|, −n1 · n2)` over their outward normals, the wedge both point into, so a 30° wedge reads 30 (Trevin's choice, 2026-10-25, relayed by Main: no flip; its test cites it); two straight edges of one solid sharing exactly one end (the same point to the bit: a valid solid's edges meeting at a point share its vertex), the angle at it, the vertex named by its own name, as `elements` lists it (unstable, it may be: a read's answer is stored nowhere); any other two straight edges, 0 to 90 between their lines; a straight edge and a planar face, 0 to 90. Straight is `describe`'s: a line, or a Bézier or B-spline of two poles. Anything else is refused, `invalid_input` naming `elements`, the element, its kind and type as the cause (a degenerated edge as one: a pole or an apex, with no curve), and what applies to it. A name that's a solid's is refused as one, and a body that couldn't be described is said once, naming `feature` only if the request did; a refusal with no candidates suggests `elements`.
- **A read's kernel client, and close.** Each read is handed a client of its own (`Read::kernel`, a `ReadClient` that makes every request at the generation the client began at), tracked by its session. Closing the session, or dropping the sessions, retires every one: advanced past every generation, so a request in flight then, or asked for after (a read still finding its names), is superseded, and the read answers `invalid_input` naming `session`, "the session closed before the kernel measured the distance". A client tracked after the close is retired as it's tracked. Decision 34's `cancel` (Worker 2's) retires one read's client the same way, and will say `cancelled`.
- **The decided oracle cases** (`m4-measure-fable.md`, *Oracle*; approved by the maintainer, 2026-10-25) are `tests/oracle/measures/` (protected): a document of solids (`model.silico`) and 61 pairs of their elements by stable name (`cases.json`), each with Silico's answer worked by hand. `crates/silico-oracle/src/measures.rs` measures each by the core's `distance` and `angle` on the document as Silico rebuilds it, and by FreeCAD 1.1.3's `MeasureDistance` and `MeasureAngle` on the same features as FreeCAD builds them, each element found in FreeCAD's build by its geometry as Silico describes it (its point; its curve, length, ends, and middle; its surface, area, and centre), never by its number, which the two builds needn't share (Silico's cylinder numbers its faces and edges otherwise than Part's). They're compared by the decision's rules: `distance` equal to `MeasureDistance` for every pair but two circles or arcs, where `MeasureDistance` is `center_distance` and `distToShape` is `distance`; two planar faces' `angle` 180 less FreeCAD's, folded where FreeCAD reads 0 (its reading of parallel and opposite normals alike), for two faces of one body only: the rule holds while 1.1.3's turn of each normal away from the middle of the faces' centres flips neither outward normal (a convex corner) or both (a concave one), and faces of two bodies, with no corner between them, may have one flipped and not the other, so a case of them is refused as the case file's mistake (a pair of one body whose turn flips one reads as a difference, never a pass, and is checked by hand besides); two straight edges folded, min(a, 180 − a); an edge and a plane at |90 − FreeCAD's|. Silico's witness points are measured on the elements by FreeCAD too; where the least distance is met at many pairs (faces over each other, parallel edges, opposite quarter arcs), a pinned witness is OCCT's choice among them, not a hand value, as each such case's note says, and the cases with no single pair (crossing faces, a face and itself) pin none. Main's measure tests are ported as cases (testCircleCircle, testCircleCircleWithPlacement, testTwoBoxesMovedByContainers, testEdgeEdgePerpendicular, testFaceFacePerpendicular, testFaceEdgeLineToPlane, EdgeParallelToFaceNormal, testCurvedFaceDistance, and QuickMeasure's two disc distances); testCurvedFaceDistance's sphere face, which 1.1.3's selection gate refuses, is measured all the same, as the gate is the GUI's and `execute` doesn't check it (FreeCAD reads 15 through `MeasureDistance` from Python). So are most of the port note's pairs FreeCAD measured that the decision's cases didn't have (not yet a box face 10 beyond another, or a convex cylinder's side to a plane): a vertex 10 over a top's middle, √150 beyond its corner, and 5 under it inside the box; crossing faces; a face and itself; a circle and a line; and the segments (0,0,0)–(10,0,0) and (5,−1,1)–(5,1,1), 1 apart. Each answer is in the determinism sweep (`oracle-reference.json`), as the command API writes it.
