## Already decided

Cited, not reopened:

- **The kernel layer is built** (#291, §7, *Built*). `Op::Copies { base, tool, op, placements }` (`silico_protocol::copies`): `op` is `Fuse` or `Cut`; each `Placement` a `Translation`, a `Rotation` (radians, right-handed), a `Mirror` (origin and normal), or a `TurnedAndMoved`; `check_copies` refuses no placements, more than `MAX_COPIES` (999), a value that isn't finite, a point past a kilometre, a direction that isn't a unit vector, and a placement that leaves the copy on the tool. The worker places each copy (a location for a move or a turn, a rebuilt copy for a mirror), runs one boolean with each copy a tool of its own, and returns the history with the base as input 0 and copy k as input k, each copy's elements numbered as the tool's. Every result's `Topology` holds `solid_faces`.
- **Every boolean runs with FreeCAD 1.1.3's automatic fuzzy value** (#383, docs/ports/booleans.md), the copies' included, so a far copy grows it for the whole step.
- **D2, D6, D7** (M2.1): every feature makes one solid and consumes what it takes; every field is written, optional ones left out, never `null`; the canonical layout.
- **S5, S7, S8** (M3.6): a result of more than one solid is refused, `kernel_failed`; a feature that changes nothing is refused by volume, `kernel_failed`, in S7's words, as M2's booleans are (#386); no refine.
- **N2 to N9** (M2.7), and M2.8's references by name: a stored name is found in its base's element map, or fails as `reference_broken` with candidates, never re-pointed.
- **M5.0's chain, suppression, and edits** (section 7 there): the `base` links are each body's chain; suppression rewrites no reference; a child can't be moved before its parent (solidworks.md, §5, *Editing upstream*).
- **M5.9's face frame** (docs/ports/attacher.md, F2): a planar face's origin is the global origin projected onto its plane, o = n(n·p).
- **Behavior follows SolidWorks, visuals and UI follow Fusion** (CLAUDE.md), where both are silent, FreeCAD, then the recommended option (M5.0, section 12).
- **The decisions taken** (the plan, open decisions 3 to 7):
  - **3(a).** Three types, sharing `copies` and the kernel's `Copies`, with no MultiTransform. Originals are pads, pockets, revolves, and grooves, or the whole base (`solid`); fillets, chamfers, booleans, and patterns are refused, naming them. Extent and spacing, each positive, the sense in `reversed`, and no per-gap spacings.
  - **4(a).** Instances are named as off the base only while the base's faces all lie in one solid. Otherwise the step is refused as a base in pieces, naming no instance, with the fuzzy value and the pattern's span in the message.
  - **5(b).** A `pattern` read, as `sketch` is for a sketch: the resolved direction, axis, or plane in 3D, and each instance's index, placement, anchor, and state (`built`, `skipped`, `changed_nothing`, `off_base`), from the model's kept data, asking no worker.
  - **6(a).** No symmetric patterns in M6. Scheme 6 has no negative offsets; a later scheme 7 reads 6 as a superset.
  - **7(a).** Fusion's three commands and its *Type* row, live while a new pattern is being made and fixed in Edit Feature. No *Compute Type*, and no mirror *Operation*: Join only.

## 2. The document

Three feature types, each consuming its `base` (D2) and making one solid (§7, *The document*). After `id`, `type`, `v`, and `name` (D8), and `suppressed` where it's true (M5.0, open item 4), each writes its fields in the order its table gives, then `note` and `fingerprint` where it has them (D6).

### 2.1 The fields

Every field below is required and always written (D6): there are no defaults, so `reversed` is written `false`, as an extent's is (S1), and `skip` is written `[]`.

**`linear_pattern`:**

| Field | What it holds | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `base` | A feature's ID | A feature before this one that makes a solid, not already consumed (M2's base rules), consumed by this one (D2) |
| `copies` | `{"type": "features", "features": [...]}`, or `{"type": "solid"}` | `features`: one or more IDs, none repeated, each a pad, pocket, revolve, or groove in `base`'s chain: `base` itself, or a feature reached from `base` by its `base` links (open decision 3(a), §7). Copying a feature reads its tool and consumes nothing, so D2 holds. `solid`: `base`'s whole solid |
| `directions` | One or two directions, in order: direction 1, then direction 2 | Each as below. An instance's index counts steps along `directions` by place, as written; each direction's fields and the directions' order are the pattern's values, so an edit to them moves instances and renames none (section 3) |
| `directions.N.along` | `x`, `y`, `z`, the model's axes from the origin, or an element's name | The model's axes, from the origin along +X, +Y, or +Z, as the origin's planes are `xy`, `xz`, `yz`; the sense is `reversed`'s alone (Fable, 2026-11-10). Not a revolve's `x_axis`, which is its sketch's, nor a face sketch's `+x`; or a straight edge or a planar face of `base`'s solid, by its stable name, found as a fillet's edge is (M2.8). A face points along its outward normal, exactly as `describe` reports it; an edge from its start to its end as `describe` gives them (decision 3) |
| `directions.N.reversed` | `true` or `false` | Turns the direction round (§7) |
| `directions.N.distribution` | `{"type": "extent", "length": L}` or `{"type": "spacing", "length": S}` | `extent`: L from the first instance to the last. `spacing`: S between neighbours. Each a `Length` above 0 and at most a kilometre; the direction's span, L or (count − 1) · S in nanometres, at most a kilometre too (section 4.1). The value's key is the quantity's kind in both forms, as a sweep's `extent` names it (S1; Fable, 2026-11-10) |
| `directions.N.count` | A whole number | At least 1, the original included (solidworks.md, §5, *Patterns and mirror*). The directions' counts multiplied are at most 1,000 (decision 8). A direction of count 1 places nothing, and its reference is still checked (§2) |
| `skip` | The instances left out, each an array of offsets | In the names' index form (section 3), one array an instance, each in its one spelling, never the original, each in range, none repeated; read in any order and written sorted (D5; decision 7). `[]` for none |

**`polar_pattern`:**

| Field | What it holds | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `base`, `copies` | As a linear pattern's | |
| `axis` | `x`, `y`, `z`, the model's axes from the origin, or an element's name | An origin axis, through the origin, its sense `reversed`'s alone; or a straight edge of `base`'s solid, from its start to its end, or a circular edge, about its circle's axis through its centre, each as `describe` gives it (§7, decision 3) |
| `reversed` | `true` or `false` | The instances turn right-handed about the axis, or left-handed when `reversed` (FreeCAD's `Reversed`, PP:57-130) |
| `distribution` | `{"type": "extent", "angle": A}` or `{"type": "spacing", "angle": A}` | `extent`: A above 0° and at most 360°; exactly 360 spreads the instances over a whole turn (decision 5). `spacing`: A above 0° between neighbours, the instances reaching less than a whole turn (decision 5) |
| `count` | A whole number | 1 to 1,000, the original included (decision 8) |
| `skip` | As a linear pattern's, each instance one offset | |

**`mirror`:**

| Field | What it holds | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `base`, `copies` | As a linear pattern's | |
| `plane` | `xy`, `xz`, `yz`, or an element's name | An origin plane, or a planar face of `base`'s solid by its stable name. A plane has no sense, so a face's orientation is moot (decision 3) |

A mirror has no `skip`: its one copy left out would be the identity. It has no second plane, no *Operation*, and no *Merge solids* (open decision 7(a); §6, *Where it differs*, 5).

**In canonical form** (D7, `document/write.rs`):

- A pattern holds an object (`copies`), so its members are written one to a line. A direction holds its `distribution`, so its members are written one to a line too. `skip` is written an instance to a line, and `[]` for none; `copies.features` and a `distribution` on one line.
- Lengths are millimetres and angles degrees, each a plain decimal of at most 6 and 9 places (D4); counts and offsets are whole numbers.
- `copies.features` and `skip` are sets: each is read in any order and written sorted, `copies.features` by ID and `skip` as section 3 orders instances, direction 1's offset first, and a repeat is refused (M3.2's D5; Fable, 2026-11-10). The originals are applied in timeline order whatever the list says (§3, *What stays and what breaks*), so the order changes no result and no name, and a file has one spelling of each. Once the gate passes, the model holds both sorted, so `set_field`'s `skip.N` and `copies.features.N` and `text` address the same item; an error's path from the gate, `copies.features.N` or `skip.N`, counts by place in the text it read.
- `NAME_LISTS`, the lists the canonical form writes an item to a line, are restated as the lists a person adds to and removes from one item at a time: a dress-up's `edges`, and a pattern's `skip`, which joins them (Fable, 2026-11-10), so a skip added or taken back is a line of the diff. An `along`, `axis`, or `plane` that's a name is one string, not a list.

**Not in M6** (§7; open decisions 3, 6, and 7): symmetric directions and bidirectional circular patterns; per-gap spacings; dress-ups, booleans, and patterns as originals (a pattern of a pattern is a whole-solid pattern of its result); sketch elements as references (later, and additive: decision 3); a cylindrical face as a polar axis, and a circular edge or a conical face as a linear direction (SolidWorks takes them; additive); a mirror's second plane; *Compute Type*; Pattern on Path; and refine (S8).

### 2.4 Where the instances go

Every instance's placement is computed in the core from its index and the document's integers, never accumulated, so no instance's place depends on another's rounding (§7; M6.3).

**The step's key** holds what its kernel request is a function of, by the fillet's rule (`Step::Dress`, which holds its base's key and a digest of its base's naming key and its edges as written): its inputs' keys, and its own values as the document writes them; where a value is a name, its base's naming key and the name as written (Fable, 2026-11-10). A pattern's step is `Copies { base: Key, tool: Key, op, references: [u8; 32], and the integers }`:

- `references` is the digest of the base's naming key and each reference as written, `along`, `axis`, or `plane`, a word or a name alike, one rule for both;
- the line holds `reversed`, the distribution's kind and value (in nanometres or nanodegrees), each count, and `skip`;
- no round is added: the step resolves its names when it runs, as a fillet does.

**The references are resolved once,** in the `base` feature's map and description, when the pattern's first step runs: it names the base feature's map as a fillet does (`Plan::sources`, `map_of`); finds each name (`references::look_up`, or `reference_broken` with candidates); describes that one element (`KernelRequest::describe`) and checks its kind; and checks the parallel directions and, for each placement, `check_copies`' predicates (section 4.2). Every step of the chain, one for each original, reuses those placements and sends its request: a later step's input is the step before's result, an intermediate no map names.

**The naming key.** The pattern's `Naming` variant holds its op code and, for each step, which input it copies and whether as a tool or as the solid (`C` or `S`); `naming_key` hashes that as it hashes a sweep's faces key. The originals' naming keys are among the inputs: the base's, then the originals' in timeline order, each once.

- **The direction, axis, or plane** is resolved once, in the base feature's map and description (above): an origin word is exact; a name is found in `base`'s element map and read from that element's description (M4.4): a face's `normal`, an edge's `start` and `end`, a circle's `axis`. `reversed` negates a linear direction or a polar axis's direction. A mirror's face gives its plane through o = n(n·p), the global origin projected (F2).
- **Linear:** instance (i, j) moves by i · s₁ · d₁ + j · s₂ · d₂, where dₙ is direction n's unit vector and sₙ its step: L / (count − 1) for `extent`, with i · L taken exactly in nanometres and divided once, or S for `spacing`. One `Translation` a copy. The plate's corner holes step 40 and 40: instance (2, 1) moves by (80, 40, 0).
- **Polar:** instance k turns by k · A / count when the extent is exactly 360°, by k · A / (count − 1) for any other extent, 359.999999999° included, or by k · A for `spacing`, in nanodegrees (k · A exact, then one division), converted to radians at the boundary by `silico_protocol::radians` through the libm shim (decision 5). One `Rotation` a copy. So 360° ÷ 4 is at 0, 90, 180, and 270, and 180° ÷ 4 at 0, 60, 120, and 180 (M6.3).
- **Mirror:** one `Mirror`, the plane's origin and normal.
- **The tool** is the original's sweep step (its `Prism`, `PrismThroughAll`, or `Revolution` key, reused, never rebuilt), or for `solid` the base's result (§7). A pad's, a revolve's, or a solid's copies are fused, a pocket's or a groove's cut.
- **Several originals** are one step each, in timeline order, each chained on the last (§7, FreeCAD's per-original loop): a pad's copies are fused, then a later pocket's are cut.
- **The identity.** A count of 1 in every direction, or every copy skipped, makes a step with no placements: it runs its resolution and checks, asks the kernel nothing more (no copies request), and passes its base's result and map through, and S7 isn't asked (§2, decision 6).
- **Time at the cap.** One step places up to 999 copies (decision 8), which the kernel layer measured at about 9 s on a loaded Mac for simple copies (#291). M6.3 benchmarks a step at the cap with a realistic tool, slots pocketed in a 40 by 25 grid (1,000 instances, 999 copies), and records its time beside the kernel's 60-second timeout. **The bar:** under half that timeout, 30 s, on the CI runner. If it doesn't pass, M6.3 lowers the cap (decision 8's 1,000 instances, `MAX_INSTANCES`, and with it `MAX_COPIES`) to the largest count that does, before merge and with the maintainer's OK, since the cap is in the format; no document holds a pattern yet, so lowering it breaks none. That's recommended over revisiting the design: one boolean of every copy is what keeps the step deterministic and cached (§7).

- **What stays and what breaks** is §3's table. ## 4. Refusals and the warning

No error code is added: every refusal takes `invalid_input`, `kernel_failed`, `reference_broken`, or `invalid_result`, as the sketch features' do (M3.6's *Errors*; patterns.md, *Protected files*). The warning `instances_changed_nothing` is **a new warning code**, appended to `warning-codes.txt` (section 11). Each example below is one member of an envelope's `errors`, or of `check`'s `problems` or `warnings`, in the error contract's shape (M4.1 section 2). A message quotes a document's string to its first 40 characters, then `…` after the closing quote; `elements` holds it whole to 200 (M4.1 section 2) where a refusal lists it. The gate's refusals of a stored name list no elements, as its refusals of a fillet's edges don't (`document/validate.rs`); the rebuild's list the name, as `reference_broken` does.

An instance is named in `elements` as index text, its offsets joined by commas with no space (`3`, `2,1`), so a warning's elements paste into `skip` as `[[3], [2, 1]]` (decision 7, §3 rule 9).

**Each face's orientation, a kernel change for M6.3.** Refusing a face with no outside needs the face's orientation in its solid, which no built reply reports: `describe`'s `Description` gives a planar face's normal as its surface's, reversed where the face is reversed, and an internal or external face's as its surface's alone (`silico_protocol::describe`, `Surface::Plane`), and `Topology` holds no orientation (`silico_protocol::history`). Only the export's mesh reads it (`bridge.cpp`, which leaves `TopAbs_INTERNAL` and `TopAbs_EXTERNAL` faces out of a printable mesh). So M6.3 has `Description`, or `Topology`, report each face's orientation as a raw fact (forward, reversed, internal, or external), checked by the core as the rest of a reply is, with a test of its own on the real kernel: a fuse that leaves a face inside its solid reports it internal, and a box's faces report forward or reversed as OCCT holds them.

**A hang kept.** A copies step whose worker hangs (`worker_timeout` once it had started) is kept for the session, as a description that hangs is (`hung` and `HUNG_KEPT`, `recompute.rs`), with the same advice, `check` with `fresh` to try it again; so an edit downstream of it doesn't hold a worker for the kernel's 60 seconds at every rebuild. It isn't kept on disk: a hang isn't a fact about the shape (`cache::lasting`).

**Every verdict kept.** The model keeps each instance's verdict for each step, off the base, changed nothing, or built, not only the first 100 an error's `elements` list. The `pattern` read's `off_base` and `changed_nothing` states come from those verdicts (section 5).

Before the request is sent, every placement is checked by `check_copies`' predicates, and each failure is worded for its instance: parameter `directions`, the instance's index in `elements`. The gate's rules (section 4.1) leave only a grid's two directions together to reach these.

Which copies lie off the base is read from the step's `solid_faces`: the base's faces (history input 0) must all lie in one solid, and then each instance any of whose copy's faces lies in another solid is off the base (§7, *The kernel*). Where the base's faces lie in more than one solid, no instance is named, and the step is refused as a base in pieces (open decision 4(a)).

## 5. The `pattern` read

A pattern as the named model built it, for the app's instance boxes and marks (M6.7) and for an agent choosing `C2,1` (open decision 5(b)). It's a published read on the session's read lane, as `sketch` is (M5.0, section 7): it answers from the model's kept data, the resolved references, the placements, the profiles, and each step's verdicts, and asks no worker. With `current` it waits for the revision's rebuild, as every read of the current model does (M4.1 section 2).

The result holds:

- **`pattern`** and **`type`**: the feature's ID and its type.
- **The references, resolved in 3D,** the sense of `reversed` included: resolved from the base's kept description (`Model::description`) and map by the function the step uses (section 2.4), asking no worker; `null` for what the type hasn't, or where the reference didn't resolve:
  - `directions`, a linear pattern's: each `direction`, a unit vector, and `step`, its spacing in millimetres, `null` for a direction of count 1, where L / (count − 1) is undefined;
  - `axis`, a polar pattern's: its `point`, its `direction`, and `step`, the angle between neighbours in degrees, `null` for an extent of count 1;
  - `plane`, a mirror's: its `origin` (F2's) and its `normal`.
- **`anchor`**: where the original sits: the centre of area of its profile on its sketch's plane; for several originals, Σ(areaᵢ · centreᵢ) / Σ areaᵢ over their profiles, each centre placed in 3D on its own plane, which depends on no order (Fable, 2026-11-10); for `solid`, the base's centre of mass (M6.5). A profile's centre is worked out in the core from its loops, which the model keeps with each sketch's solve (M3.5), closed-form for lines, arcs, and circles; the base's is its measure's.
- **`instances`**: every instance but the original, in `skip`'s order, each with:
  - `index`, as `skip` writes it and as its copies' names carry it after `C` or `S`;
  - `offset`, a linear instance's move, or `angle`, a polar instance's turn, the other `null`, and both `null` for a mirror's copy, a reflection in `plane`;
  - `anchor`, the original's anchor placed;
  - `state`, the first of these that holds (Fable, 2026-11-10):
    - `skipped`: `skip` holds it;
    - `off_base`: any original's step named it, its copy lying apart from the base (section 4.2);
    - `changed_nothing`: any step named it, its copy the warning's (section 4.3);
    - `null`: any step didn't run, or read no instance, so the rebuild gave no verdict for it;
    - `built`.
- **`problems`** and **`problems_total`**: the pattern's own problems, as `check` gives them, the first 100 and their total; none when it built. For a pattern blocked on another feature, the error a read of its solid gives, as `sketch`'s `plane_problem` gives it for a face's feature that didn't build. Where the base's kept description was refused, failed, or hung (`Described::Refused`, `Described::Failed`, `HUNG_KEPT`), so no name in it can be read, the error `elements` gives for that solid: its `why_undescribed`, worded as `command::elements` words it, its `feature` the base. A read never leaves its geometry `null` with no problem to say why.

Lengths and angles the read computes are rounded once, at the end, to the nanometre and the nanodegree; directions are unit vectors of `f64`, `-0` written as `0` (M4.1 section 2, *Numbers*). Nothing guesses: a reference that didn't resolve leaves its geometry `null` and `instances` empty, as `sketch` gives `plane: null` (M5.0, section 7). The list isn't capped: a pattern has at most 999 instances besides the original (decision 8).

## 6. Timeline edits and suppression

**A pattern is a child of each original** in `copies.features`, as SolidWorks' pattern is of its features (solidworks.md, §5, *Editing upstream*; the plan, M6.0 and M6.2), and of each maker of the names it stores. Every rule follows from M5.0's chain rules (section 7 there), from the text alone. `copies: solid` names no original: the pattern is its base's consumer, as any feature is. The examples are the plate's, at revision 15, inside a transaction.

**`Operation::stored_names`** gains `directions.N.along`, `axis`, and `plane`, where they're names (M6.2). `NameField` gains a variant for each; a direction's names either kind (an edge or a face), an axis an edge, and a plane a face. So the gate's stored-name rules, M5.0's lineage rule (open item 2b there), the references, and suppression's makers all read them as they read a fillet's edges and a sketch's face.

**`add_feature`'s `before`** (M5.1): a new pattern takes its base's consumer's place, rewiring it, as any feature does. Each original must come before the new place and in its base's chain, or the gate refuses it (section 4.1: "which comes after this feature").

After the move, each original must still be in the pattern's base chain, and each maker of a stored name, `directions.N.along`, `axis`, and `plane` among them, must still be in what its base is built from (M5.0's lineage rule), or the move is refused, naming the feature and the original or the maker.

## 9. Determinism

M4.1 section 10 and M5.0 section 11 hold, with these:

- **Keys:** a step's key holds the document's integers and the references as written, never a placement's bits (section 2.4).
- **Placements** are functions of the document's integers and the references' descriptions, computed per instance from its index (section 2.4), with angles through the libm shim, so both checked platforms place every copy alike.
- **Names** come from the tool's map and the step's history, never from OCCT's lists (N10).
- **Orders:** `instances`, `skip`, and the warning's and the refusals' elements are in `skip`'s order; `copies.features`' originals are applied in timeline order whatever the list's order.
- **The read** answers from the model's kept data, so a published read of one revision gives the same bytes every time.

## 12. Decided

Decided by Fable, 2026-11-10; the maintainer may veto. The draft asked each as an open item; each is written into the design above.

1. **`skip`** is one list a pattern, after `directions`, not one a direction: a grid cell (`[2, 1]`) belongs to no one direction (decision 7, §3's rule 9).
2. **`distribution`** is a direction's key, and a polar pattern's, its value key the quantity's kind in both forms, as a sweep's `extent` names it: `{"type": "extent", "length": 80}` or `{"type": "spacing", "length": 8}`; `{"type": "extent", "angle": 360}` or `{"type": "spacing", "angle": 30}`.
3. **`copies.features` and `skip`** are read in any order and written sorted (M3.2's D5).
4. **Several originals:** an instance is named when any of its copies is, the message naming each original's instances.
5. **The read's `state`** is the first that holds: `skipped`, `off_base` (any original's step named it), `changed_nothing` (any step named it), `null` (any step didn't run or read no instance), `built`.
6. **The anchor of several originals** is Σ(areaᵢ · centreᵢ) / Σ areaᵢ over their profiles, each centre placed in 3D on its own plane, which depends on no order.
7. **Placements beyond §7's list:** at the gate, a direction reaching past a kilometre and a step OCCT takes for none; at rebuild, every placement checked by `check_copies`' predicates before the request, each failure worded for its instance (`directions`, the index in `elements`).
8. **The chain:** an original must be in `base`'s chain alone. A cut's or a common's tool chain turns an original's sense round, so only the base chain keeps "the original's tool is in the solid, with its own sense" exact; a fuse's tool chain is a later loosening, which breaks no document.
9. **Open decision 4(a)'s fuzzy value and span** go in the cause of a fuse whose base comes apart; a cut that cuts its base in pieces keeps decision 6's and S5's words.
10. **The golden documents:** (a). The six documents' `naming` line becomes 6, with the maintainer's OK on M6.1's PR, and the literal `"naming": 5` at `golden.rs` line 204 becomes 6.
11. **The read** isn't paged; `after` and `limit`, as `elements` has, come when a pattern of hundreds meets an agent.
12. **A direction of count 1** is allowed, its reference still checked, and decision 11's parallel refusal applies to it.
13. **The dialogs' first values** are FreeCAD 1.1.3's defaults, unless the prototype shows better ones.

**Decided (Fable, 2026-11-10, review round):**

1. **Direction edits:** an index counts steps along `directions` by place, as written; every direction's field, `along` included, and their order are the pattern's values, so an edit moves instances and renames none, a swap kept by index, and `C0,1` in a pattern of one direction `reference_broken` with candidates.
2. **Whole-solid copies** are marked `S` (`S<instance> <name>`), a feature's tool's copies `C`, under one instance grammar and its refusals; an `S` copy is always fused, a `C` copy's side is its inner type word's, and switching `copies.type` isn't refused, every copy's name breaking loudly with candidates.
3. **The origin's axes** are `x`, `y`, `z`, as its planes are `xy`, `xz`, `yz`, their sense `reversed`'s alone; `x_axis`, `+x`, and the like are refused as neither an origin word nor a name.
4. **A step's key** holds its inputs' keys and its values as written, a reference as a digest of the base's naming key and the reference as written, never a placement's bits (`Step::Dress`'s rule); the step resolves its names when it runs, and the naming key holds which input each step copies and whether as tool or solid.
5. **`copies.features` and `skip`** are sets, read in any order and written sorted, a repeat refused; `skip` is written an instance to a line, joining `NAME_LISTS`, and the unsorted refusal goes.
6. **A scheme-5 header over a pattern or a copy name** isn't refused: it's read like any, and its save writes 6.
