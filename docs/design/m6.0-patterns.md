## Already decided

Cited, not reopened:

- **The kernel layer is built** (#291, §7, *Built*). `Op::Copies { base, tool, op, placements }` (`silico_protocol::copies`): `op` is `Fuse` or `Cut`; each `Placement` a `Translation`, a `Rotation` (radians, right-handed), a `Mirror` (origin and normal), or a `TurnedAndMoved`; `check_copies` refuses no placements, more than `MAX_COPIES` (999), a value that isn't finite, a point past a kilometre, a direction that isn't a unit vector, and a placement that leaves the copy on the tool. The worker places each copy (a location for a move or a turn, a rebuilt copy for a mirror), runs one boolean with each copy a tool of its own, and returns the history with the base as input 0 and copy k as input k, each copy's elements numbered as the tool's. Every result's `Topology` holds `solid_faces`.
- **Every boolean runs with FreeCAD 1.1.3's automatic fuzzy value** (#383, docs/ports/booleans.md), the copies' included, so a far copy grows it for the whole step.
- **D2, D6, D7** (M2.1): every feature makes one solid and consumes what it takes; every field is written, optional ones left out, never `null`; the canonical layout.
- **S5, S7, S8** (M3.6): a result of more than one solid is refused, `kernel_failed`; a feature that changes nothing is refused by volume, `kernel_failed`, in S7's words, as M2's booleans are (#386); no refine.
- **N2 to N9** (M2.7), and M2.8's references by name: a stored name is found in its base's element map, or fails as `reference_broken` with candidates, never re-pointed.
- **M5.0's chain, suppression, and edits** (section 7 there): the `base` links are each body's chain; suppression rewrites no reference; a child can't be moved before its parent (solidworks.md, §5, *Editing upstream*).
- **M5.9's face frame** (docs/ports/attacher.md, F2): a planar face's origin is the global origin projected onto its plane, o = n(n·p).
- **Behavior follows SolidWorks, visuals and UI follow Fusion** (CLAUDE.md), where both are silent, FreeCAD, then the recommended option (M5.0, section 12).
- **The decisions taken** (the plan, open decisions 3 to 7):
  - **3(a).** Three types, sharing `copies` and the kernel's `Copies`, with no MultiTransform. Originals are pads, pockets, revolves, and grooves, or the whole base (`solid`); fillets, chamfers, booleans, and patterns are refused, naming them. Extent and spacing, each positive, the sense in `reversed`, and no per-gap spacings.
  - **4(a).** Instances are named as off the base only while the base's faces all lie in one solid. Otherwise the step is refused as a base in pieces, naming no instance, with the fuzzy value and the pattern's span in the message.
  - **5(b).** A `pattern` read, as `sketch` is for a sketch: the resolved direction, axis, or plane in 3D, and each instance's index, placement, anchor, and state (`built`, `skipped`, `changed_nothing`, `off_base`), from the model's kept data, asking no worker.
  - **6(a).** No symmetric patterns in M6. Scheme 6 has no negative offsets; a later scheme 7 reads 6 as a superset.
  - **7(a).** Fusion's three commands and its *Type* row, live while a new pattern is being made and fixed in Edit Feature. No *Compute Type*, and no mirror *Operation*: Join only.

## 2. The document

Three feature types, each consuming its `base` (D2) and making one solid (§7, *The document*). After `id`, `type`, `v`, and `name` (D8), and `suppressed` where it's true (M5.0, open item 4), each writes its fields in the order its table gives, then `note` and `fingerprint` where it has them (D6).

### 2.1 The fields

Every field below is required and always written (D6): there are no defaults, so `reversed` is written `false`, as an extent's is (S1), and `skip` is written `[]`.

**`linear_pattern`:**

| Field | What it holds | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `base` | A feature's ID | A feature before this one that makes a solid, not already consumed (M2's base rules), consumed by this one (D2) |
| `copies` | `{"type": "features", "features": [...]}`, or `{"type": "solid"}` | `features`: one or more IDs, none repeated, each a pad, pocket, revolve, or groove in `base`'s chain: `base` itself, or a feature reached from `base` by its `base` links (open decision 3(a), §7). Copying a feature reads its tool and consumes nothing, so D2 holds. `solid`: `base`'s whole solid |
| `directions` | One or two directions, in order: direction 1, then direction 2 | Each as below |
| `directions.N.along` | `x_axis`, `y_axis`, `z_axis`, or an element's name | The model's axes, from the origin along +X, +Y, or +Z (not a revolve's `x_axis`, which is its sketch's); or a straight edge or a planar face of `base`'s solid, by its stable name, found as a fillet's edge is (M2.8). A face points along its outward normal, exactly as `describe` reports it; an edge from its start to its end as `describe` gives them (decision 3) |
| `directions.N.reversed` | `true` or `false` | Turns the direction round (§7) |
| `directions.N.spacing` | `{"type": "extent", "length": L}` or `{"type": "spacing", "spacing": S}` | `extent`: L from the first instance to the last. `spacing`: S between neighbours. Each a `Length` above 0 and at most a kilometre; the direction's span, L or (count − 1) · S in nanometres, at most a kilometre too (section 4.1) |
| `directions.N.count` | A whole number | At least 1, the original included (solidworks.md, §5, *Patterns and mirror*). The directions' counts multiplied are at most 1,000 (decision 8). A direction of count 1 places nothing, and its reference is still checked (§2) |
| `skip` | The instances left out, each an array of offsets | In the names' index form (section 3), one array an instance: sorted, never the original, each in range, none repeated, each in its one spelling (decision 7). `[]` for none |

**`polar_pattern`:**

| Field | What it holds | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `base`, `copies` | As a linear pattern's | |
| `axis` | `x_axis`, `y_axis`, `z_axis`, or an element's name | An origin axis, through the origin; or a straight edge of `base`'s solid, from its start to its end, or a circular edge, about its circle's axis through its centre, each as `describe` gives it (§7, decision 3) |
| `reversed` | `true` or `false` | The instances turn right-handed about the axis, or left-handed when `reversed` (FreeCAD's `Reversed`, PP:57-130) |
| `spacing` | `{"type": "extent", "angle": A}` or `{"type": "spacing", "spacing": A}` | `extent`: A above 0° and at most 360°; exactly 360 spreads the instances over a whole turn (decision 5). `spacing`: A above 0° between neighbours, the instances reaching less than a whole turn (decision 5) |
| `count` | A whole number | 1 to 1,000, the original included (decision 8) |
| `skip` | As a linear pattern's, each instance one offset | |

**`mirror`:**

| Field | What it holds | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `base`, `copies` | As a linear pattern's | |
| `plane` | `xy`, `xz`, `yz`, or an element's name | An origin plane, or a planar face of `base`'s solid by its stable name. A plane has no sense, so a face's orientation is moot (decision 3) |

A mirror has no `skip`: its one copy left out would be the identity. It has no second plane, no *Operation*, and no *Merge solids* (open decision 7(a); §6, *Where it differs*, 5).

**In canonical form** (D7, `document/write.rs`):

- A pattern holds an object (`copies`), so its members are written one to a line. `copies`, a direction's `spacing`, and `skip` hold no object, so each is on one line. A direction holds `spacing`, so its members are written one to a line too.
- Lengths are millimetres and angles degrees, each a plain decimal of at most 6 and 9 places (D4); counts and offsets are whole numbers.
- `copies.features` is written in the order given. The originals are applied in timeline order whatever the list says (§3, *What stays and what breaks*), so its order changes no result and no name; section 12, item 3, asks whether it should have one spelling.
- An `along`, `axis`, or `plane` that's a name is one string, so it isn't one of the lists the canonical form writes a name to a line (`NAME_LISTS`, a fillet's and a chamfer's `edges`).

**Not in M6** (§7; open decisions 3, 6, and 7): symmetric directions and bidirectional circular patterns; per-gap spacings; dress-ups, booleans, and patterns as originals (a pattern of a pattern is a whole-solid pattern of its result); sketch elements as references (later, and additive: decision 3); a cylindrical face as a polar axis, and a circular edge or a conical face as a linear direction (SolidWorks takes them; additive); a mirror's second plane; *Compute Type*; Pattern on Path; and refine (S8).

### 2.4 Where the instances go

Every instance's placement is computed in the core from its index and the document's integers, never accumulated, so no instance's place depends on another's rounding (§7; M6.3). The step's key holds the resolved placements by their bits, never the names, as M5.9's frames are keyed (M6.3).

- **The direction, axis, or plane** is resolved first: an origin word is exact; a name is found in `base`'s element map (`references::look_up`) and read from its description (M4.4): a face's `normal`, an edge's `start` and `end`, a circle's `axis`. `reversed` negates a linear direction or a polar axis's direction. A mirror's face gives its plane through o = n(n·p), the global origin projected (F2).
- **Linear:** instance (i, j) moves by i · s₁ · d₁ + j · s₂ · d₂, where dₙ is direction n's unit vector and sₙ its step: L / (count − 1) for `extent`, with i · L taken exactly in nanometres and divided once, or S for `spacing`. One `Translation` a copy. The plate's corner holes step 40 and 40: instance (2, 1) moves by (80, 40, 0).
- **Polar:** instance k turns by k · A / count when the extent is exactly 360°, by k · A / (count − 1) for any other extent, 359.999999999° included, or by k · A for `spacing`, in nanodegrees (k · A exact, then one division), converted to radians at the boundary by `silico_protocol::radians` through the libm shim (decision 5). One `Rotation` a copy. So 360° ÷ 4 is at 0, 90, 180, and 270, and 180° ÷ 4 at 0, 60, 120, and 180 (M6.3).
- **Mirror:** one `Mirror`, the plane's origin and normal.
- **The tool** is the original's sweep step (its `Prism`, `PrismThroughAll`, or `Revolution` key, reused, never rebuilt), or for `solid` the base's result (§7). A pad's, a revolve's, or a solid's copies are fused, a pocket's or a groove's cut.
- **Several originals** are one step each, in timeline order, each chained on the last (§7, FreeCAD's per-original loop): a pad's copies are fused, then a later pocket's are cut.
- **The identity.** A count of 1 in every direction, or every copy skipped, runs no kernel step: the base's solid and names pass through, and S7 isn't asked; the references are still resolved and checked (§2, decision 6).

## 3. Names: scheme 6

The copy form is `feature.type.Kind(C<index> <name>)`: one offset per direction, direction 1 first, trailing zeros left off, the original unmarked (§3, decided). Its grammar, rules, and examples are N10 in docs/design/m2.7-naming.md. On the plate:

| Element | Name |
|---|---|
| The original corner hole's wall, unchanged by the pattern after it | `q0000002.pocket.Face(G s0000003.sketch.Edge.cc000001)` |
| Copy 2's wall | `t0000001.linear_pattern.Face(C2 q0000002.pocket.Face(G s0000003.sketch.Edge.cc000001))` |
| Copy (0, 1)'s wall | `t0000001.linear_pattern.Face(C0,1 q0000002.pocket.Face(G s0000003.sketch.Edge.cc000001))` |
| Copy (2, 1)'s top rim, the chamfer's edge | `t0000001.linear_pattern.Edge(G p0000001.pad.Face(G s0000001.sketch.Face1), G t0000001.linear_pattern.Face(C2,1 q0000002.pocket.Face(G s0000003.sketch.Edge.cc000001)))` |
| The notch's mirrored wall, from `nb000001` | `m0000001.mirror.Face(C1 q0000001.pocket.Face(G s0000002.sketch.Edge.nb000001))` |
| Boss hole copy 3's wall | `t0000002.polar_pattern.Face(C3 q0000003.pocket.Face(G s0000005.sketch.Edge.ce000001))` |
| In `m0000002`, the mirror of copy (2, 1)'s wall | `m0000002.mirror.Face(C1 t0000001.linear_pattern.Face(C2,1 q0000002.pocket.Face(G s0000003.sketch.Edge.cc000001)))` |

- **The scheme.** `NAMING_SCHEME` becomes 6. A document's `naming` reads as 5 or 6, and none as 5; every save writes 6; any other value is refused, naming `naming` (decision 10). `naming-scheme-5.silico` stays as it is, read under 6 with every reference found, and `silico fmt` of it changes its `naming` line alone (M6.1).
- **Stability.** A copy of an unstable name is unstable, so a stored reference refuses it as any unstable name (M4.1 section 2); the depth bound counts a level for each copy (M6.1).
- **What stays and what breaks** is §3's table: raising a count, skipping or bringing back an instance, changing a spacing, angle, axis, or plane, and adding direction 2 rename no copy. Lowering a count, swapping the directions, taking an original out of `copies.features`, and switching `copies` between `features` and `solid` break the names they remove, loudly, with candidates.

## 4. Refusals and the warning

No error code is added: every refusal takes `invalid_input`, `kernel_failed`, `reference_broken`, or `invalid_result`, as the sketch features' do (M3.6's *Errors*; patterns.md, *Protected files*). The warning `instances_changed_nothing` is **a new warning code**, appended to `warning-codes.txt` (section 11). Each example below is one member of an envelope's `errors`, or of `check`'s `problems` or `warnings`, in the error contract's shape (M4.1 section 2). A message quotes a document's string to its first 40 characters, then `…` after the closing quote; `elements` holds it whole to 200 (M4.1 section 2).

An instance is named in `elements` as index text, its offsets joined by commas with no space (`3`, `2,1`), so a warning's elements paste into `skip` as `[[3], [2, 1]]` (decision 7, §3 rule 9).

## 5. The `pattern` read

A pattern as the named model built it, for the app's instance boxes and marks (M6.7) and for an agent choosing `C2,1` (open decision 5(b)). It's a published read on the session's read lane, as `sketch` is (M5.0, section 7): it answers from the model's kept data, the resolved references, the placements, the profiles, and each step's verdicts, and asks no worker. With `current` it waits for the revision's rebuild, as every read of the current model does (M4.1 section 2).

The result holds:

- **`pattern`** and **`type`**: the feature's ID and its type.
- **The references, resolved in 3D,** the sense of `reversed` included (section 2.4); `null` for what the type hasn't, or where the reference didn't resolve:
  - `directions`, a linear pattern's: each `direction`, a unit vector, and `step`, its spacing in millimetres;
  - `axis`, a polar pattern's: its `point`, its `direction`, and `step`, the angle between neighbours in degrees;
  - `plane`, a mirror's: its `origin` (F2's) and its `normal`.
- **`anchor`**: where the original sits: the centre of area of its profile on its sketch's plane, or for several originals the centre of area of their profiles together; for `solid`, the base's centre of mass (M6.5). A profile's centre is worked out in the core from its loops, which the model keeps with each sketch's solve (M3.5), closed-form for lines, arcs, and circles; the base's is its measure's.
- **`instances`**: every instance but the original, in `skip`'s order, each with:
  - `index`, as `skip` writes it and as its copies' names carry it after `C`;
  - `offset`, a linear instance's move, or `angle`, a polar instance's turn, the other `null`, and both `null` for a mirror's copy, a reflection in `plane`;
  - `anchor`, the original's anchor placed;
  - `state`: `skipped` when `skip` holds it; `off_base` when its copy lies apart from the base (section 4.2); `changed_nothing` when its copy is the warning's (section 4.3); `built` otherwise; and `null` where the rebuild gave no verdict, its step not run or refused for a reason that reads no instance. With several originals, an instance is `off_base` if any of its copies is, else `changed_nothing` if any is.
- **`problems`** and **`problems_total`**: the pattern's own problems, as `check` gives them, the first 100 and their total; none when it built. For a pattern blocked on another feature, the error a read of its solid gives, as `sketch`'s `plane_problem` gives it for a face's feature that didn't build.

Lengths and angles the read computes are rounded once, at the end, to the nanometre and the nanodegree; directions are unit vectors of `f64`, `-0` written as `0` (M4.1 section 2, *Numbers*). Nothing guesses: a reference that didn't resolve leaves its geometry `null` and `instances` empty, as `sketch` gives `plane: null` (M5.0, section 7). The list isn't capped: a pattern has at most 999 instances besides the original (decision 8).

## 6. Timeline edits and suppression

**A pattern is a child of each original** in `copies.features`, as SolidWorks' pattern is of its features (solidworks.md, §5, *Editing upstream*; the plan, M6.0 and M6.2), and of each maker of the names it stores. Every rule follows from M5.0's chain rules (section 7 there), from the text alone. `copies: solid` names no original: the pattern is its base's consumer, as any feature is. The examples are the plate's, at revision 15, inside a transaction.

**`Operation::stored_names`** gains `directions.N.along`, `axis`, and `plane`, where they're names (M6.2). `NameField` gains a variant for each; a direction's names either kind (an edge or a face), an axis an edge, and a plane a face. So the gate's stored-name rules, M5.0's lineage rule (open item 2b there), the references, and suppression's makers all read them as they read a fillet's edges and a sketch's face.

**`add_feature`'s `before`** (M5.1): a new pattern takes its base's consumer's place, rewiring it, as any feature does. Each original must come before the new place and in its base's chain, or the gate refuses it (section 4.1: "which comes after this feature").

**`move_feature`** (M5.1). A child can't be moved before its parent (solidworks.md, §5, *Editing upstream*). So a move is refused, naming both features, that puts a pattern before an original, or an original after a pattern that copies it. After the move, each original must still be in the pattern's base chain, and each maker of a stored name, `directions.N.along`, `axis`, and `plane` among them, must still be in what its base is built from (M5.0's lineage rule), or the move is refused, naming the feature and the original or the maker.

## 9. Determinism

M4.1 section 10 and M5.0 section 11 hold, with these:

- **Placements** are functions of the document's integers and the references' descriptions, computed per instance from its index (section 2.4), with angles through the libm shim, so both checked platforms place every copy alike.
- **Names** come from the tool's map and the step's history, never from OCCT's lists (N10).
- **Orders:** `instances`, `skip`, and the warning's and the refusals' elements are in `skip`'s order; `copies.features`' originals are applied in timeline order whatever the list's order.
- **The read** answers from the model's kept data, so a published read of one revision gives the same bytes every time.

## 12. Open items, for Fable

What this design couldn't settle from its sources, or would decide otherwise than they do. Each is written into the design as the sources have it, with a recommendation.

1. **Where `skip` lives.** §7's example puts a `skip` in each direction; decision 7, §3's rule 9, and the plan's M6.0 and M6.7 rows give one list of instances, whose grid cells (`[2, 1]`) belong to no one direction. *Written:* one `skip` a pattern, after `directions`. *Recommended:* as written.
2. **The `spacing` key.** §7 names a direction's form `spacing`, so its spacing form reads `"spacing": {"type": "spacing", "spacing": 8}`, its path `directions.0.spacing.spacing`. *Written:* as §7 has it. *Recommended:* name the key `distribution`, Fusion's row (Extent, Spacing; Full, Partial), before M6.2 freezes the schema.
3. **`copies.features`' order.** The originals are applied in timeline order whatever the list says (§3), so two orders spell one pattern. *Written:* the order given. *Recommended:* written sorted by ID, as a sketch's members are (M3.2's D5), so a file has one spelling and a move of an original rewrites no list.
4. **Several originals.** §7 makes each original a step of its own but words the warning and the off-base refusal for one copy an instance. *Written:* an instance is named when any of its copies is, the message naming each original's instances, and the read's state is `off_base`, else `changed_nothing`, if any copy is. *Recommended:* as written.
5. **A state with no verdict.** The plan's four states don't cover an instance whose step didn't run, or was refused for a reason that reads no instance. *Written:* `state: null`, as `sketch` gives `plane: null` rather than guess. *Recommended:* as written.
6. **The anchor of several originals.** M6.5 gives "the original's profile centre placed". *Written:* the centre of area of their profiles together. *Recommended:* as written.
7. **Refusals beyond §7's list,** from the kernel's ranges: at the gate, a direction reaching past a kilometre and a step OCCT takes for none; at rebuild, an instance two directions together place past a kilometre. Without them, `check_copies` would refuse the request with a protocol field's name. *Recommended:* as written.
8. **The chain an original must be in.** §7 says `base`'s chain; an original in a boolean's `tool` chain is in the solid too, but is refused. *Recommended:* `base` links only, as written; loosening it later breaks no document.
9. **Open decision 4(a)'s message parts.** Its fuzzy value and span go in the cause of a fuse whose base comes apart. A cut that cuts its base in pieces keeps decision 6's and S5's words, the fuzzy value being no part of why. *Recommended:* as written.
10. **The golden documents** (section 11, item 9). M6.1 makes `fmt` write `"naming": 6`, and `tests/golden.rs` checks that each golden document formats to its own bytes and that `fmt` adds `"naming": 5` to one saved without it. Options: (a) the six documents' `naming` line becomes 6, with the maintainer's OK, and the test's added line 6; (b) they stay at 5, and the test expects their `naming` line alone to change, as M6.1 expects of `naming-scheme-5.silico`. *Recommended:* (a): the goldens stay canonical documents that save to their own bytes, and `naming-scheme-5.silico` keeps scheme 5's proof. The plan's protected list doesn't name them.
11. **The read's size.** At the cap, 999 instances are about 100 KB, past Claude Code's default of 25,000 tokens for a tool's result (docs/mcp.md). *Recommended:* no paging now; add `after` and `limit`, as `elements` has, when a pattern of hundreds meets an agent.
12. **A direction of count 1** is allowed, so an expression can turn a direction off (`c001732663`, §1), its reference still checked (§2), and decision 11's parallel refusal applies to it as written. *Recommended:* as written.
13. **The dialogs' first values** are FreeCAD 1.1.3's defaults, by M5.0's rule. An extent of 100 mm puts the copies off a small part at the first preview. *Recommended:* as written, unless the prototype shows a better first value.
