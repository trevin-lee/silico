# M3.3 design: solving and diagnostics

M3.3 (docs/roadmap/m3-sketcher.md) solves a document's sketch. The core translates a checked sketch into the sketch worker's terms, the worker diagnoses and solves it in FreeCAD's order, and the core reads the result back as data: the solved geometry, or what's wrong, naming constraint and geometry IDs. It changes no file format: writing solved coordinates back is M3.4's. The study behind it is docs/ports/sketcher.md, and FreeCAD 1.1.3's translation (`Sketch.cpp` as of `ca9e1ecdf3^`, whose PlaneGCS is byte for byte the one vendored) was read for this design, with its stated behavior run through the oracle's headless FreeCAD 1.1.3.

Decisions here follow the standing rule (FreeCAD's behavior, and the recommendation where it leaves a choice), and none changes the file format. Two touch protected files, with the maintainer's approval (2026-10-17): two error codes appended (below), and the sketch stress reference regenerated for the worker's fallback past geometry it can't build (11 generated slots, their status only).

## The translation

The core builds a `SketchSystem` from a checked sketch:

- **Values.** The origin (0, 0) and the axes' far ends, (1, 0) and (0, 1), are fixed values: `x_axis` is a line from the origin to (1, 0), `y_axis` from the origin to (0, 1), as FreeCAD's `H_Axis` and `V_Axis`. Then each point's x and y in the sketch's order (points sorted by ID), each circle's radius, and each arc's radius and two angles: all unknowns. Then each driving dimension's value, fixed. Degrees of freedom are PlaneGCS's: 2 per point, 1 per circle (its radius; its centre is a point), and 3 per arc (radius and two angles, less PlaneGCS's 4 arc rules over its 3 points' 6 values: 5 for the arc as FreeCAD counts it).
- **Geometry.** Each point, line, circle, and arc, in that order, made of those values: a line's ends and an arc's centre, start, and end are the points they name, so curves that share a point share its values (the design's D2).
- **Arcs.** The core works out an arc's radius (`sqrt(dx² + dy²)` from its centre to its start) and its angles (`atan2` of the start and the end about the centre, the end's taken past the start by 2π when it isn't above it) from its points (`silico_protocol::arc_from_points`, the `libm` crate's math, the same everywhere: the libm shim's). The roadmap put this in the worker; the math is the same in either process, and doing it in the core keeps the worker's request exactly what it solves. An arc whose start and end come out at one angle (a sweep of 0 or a whole turn) is refused there, naming it; the gate refuses only ends drawn at one place. Every arc gets its rules (tag 0).
- **Tags.** A constraint's tag is its place in the sorted list, plus one, whether it's sent or not (reference dimensions aren't), so a report names constraints by position and the core maps back to IDs.
- **Forms**, each to the PlaneGCS rows FreeCAD 1.1.3 makes for it:

| Form | PlaneGCS | Rows |
|---|---|---|
| `horizontal`, `vertical` (a line or an axis) | `Horizontal(line)`, `Vertical(line)` | 1 |
| `horizontal_points`, `vertical_points` | `Horizontal(a, b)`, `Vertical(a, b)` | 1 |
| `length` | `P2PDistance(start, end, v)` | 1 |
| `distance` | `P2PDistance(a, b, v)` | 1 |
| `distance_x`, `distance_y` | `Difference(a.x, b.x, v)`, `(a.y, b.y, v)` | 1 |
| `point_line_distance` | `P2LDistance(point, line, v)`, unsigned: the side is checked after the solve | 1 |
| `on` a line or axis, a circle, an arc | `PointOnLine`, `PointOnCircle`, `PointOnArc` (an arc's whole circle) | 1 |
| `parallel`, `perpendicular` | `Parallel`, `Perpendicular` | 1 |
| `angle` | `L2LAngle(a, b, v)`, `v` in radians (`Angle`'s degrees × π/180) | 1 |
| `radius`, `diameter` | `CircleRadius`/`ArcRadius(v)`; a diameter as a radius of `v / 2`, exact in binary (FreeCAD's `Proportional` with 0.5 gives the same residual bit for bit) | 1 |
| `equal` | `EqualLength` for lines, `EqualRadius` for circles and arcs | 1 |
| `tangent`, a line and a circle or arc | `Tangent(line, circle)` (`P2LDistance` from the centre, the radius), unsigned: the side is checked after the solve | 1 |
| `tangent`, two circles or arcs | `TangentCircumf(c1, c2, r1, r2, internal)`, `internal` exactly when the side is `inside` (FreeCAD decides it from the geometry; Silico stores it) | 1 |
| `tangent_on` | `PointOnLine`/`PointOnCircle`/`PointOnArc(at, b)`, and `AngleViaPoint(a, b, at, θ)`, under one tag | 2 |
| `tangent_at` | `AngleViaPoint(a, b, at, θ)`: FreeCAD's coincidence of the two ends is the shared point itself (its rows would be zeros) | 1 |
| `symmetric` about a line or axis | `P2PSymmetric(a, b, line)`: `Perpendicular(a, b, line)` and `MidpointOnLine(a, b, line)` | 2 |
| `symmetric` of an arc's own ends about a line its centre is held on (below) | `MidpointOnLine(a, b, line)` | 1 |
| `symmetric` about a point | `P2PSymmetric(a, b, m)`: `PointOnPerpBisector(m, a, b)` and `PointOnLine(m, a, b)` | 2 |
| `midpoint` | `P2PSymmetric(start, end, point)` | 2 |

- **Directions** (`tangent_on`, `tangent_at`). `AngleViaPoint` holds the angle from `a`'s normal to `b`'s at the point, each curve's normal being its own direction turned a quarter counter-clockwise (a line's from `from` to `to`, a circle's or arc's counter-clockwise), so θ = 0 holds the two curves' own directions the same there and θ = π opposite. FreeCAD stores θ − π/2 (−π/2 for 0, +π/2 for π), and decides it from the geometry only for a value of 0 from before 2014. Silico works θ out from the stored direction and which ends the point is:
  - `tangent_on`: `a`'s direction *leaving* `at` against `b`'s own. Leaving `a`'s start is `a`'s own direction, leaving its end the reverse: `same` is θ = 0 at `a`'s start and π at its end; `opposite` the other way.
  - `tangent_at`: a path along `a` *into* `at` and on along `b` *out of* it. Arriving along `a` is its own direction at its end and the reverse at its start; leaving along `b` is its own direction at its start and the reverse at its end. `same` (a smooth join) is θ = 0 when `at` is `a`'s end and `b`'s start, or `a`'s start and `b`'s end, and π when it's both curves' start or both their ends; `opposite` (a cusp) the other way.
- **The symmetric arc** (FreeCAD `365af33fc6`). FreeCAD sends only the midpoint row for an arc's own ends symmetric about a line through the arc's centre: there the perpendicular row repeats what the arc's equal radii already make true. It decides "through the centre" from where the geometry is drawn (within 1e-7), so the same document gets a different system from different coordinates, and once the centre comes off the line FreeCAD under-counts (the oracle: a degree of freedom more than the sketch has). Silico decides from the references alone: the special case applies when `a` and `b` are one arc's start and end and its centre is held on the line by the sketch itself, as an end of that line or by an `on` constraint of the centre on it. Otherwise both rows go, and a centre the other constraints put on the line makes the perpendicular row redundant, which decision 5 (below) checks and keeps.
- **Reference dimensions** (`driving: false`) aren't sent (docs/ports/sketcher.md, *Reference constraints*). The core measures each from the solved geometry: a length, distance, or radius as the solver does (`sqrt(dx² + dy²)`), a diameter as twice the radius, a signed `distance_x` or `distance_y`, a point's distance from a line and the side it's on, and an angle as `atan2` of the two directions' cross and dot products. As 1.1.3 does, a reference angle stays on the turn of its stored value (1.1.3 solves it as an unknown starting from that value, with an error that wraps by whole turns, so it ends on the nearest representative), and is then reduced with `fmod(v, 2π)` (`ba78b194b3`): v = stored + wrap(measured − stored), wrap into (−π, π].

## The solve, in FreeCAD's order

The worker builds PlaneGCS's system once, diagnoses it (degrees of freedom, conflicting, redundant, and partially redundant tags, and the free values), and then solves it with DogLeg, then Levenberg–Marquardt, then BFGS, then SQP on the system with every unknown anchored at its starting value by a tag −1 row (FreeCAD's `Sketch::internalSolve`, case 3), each from the starting values, until one solves. FreeCAD doesn't solve a sketch whose constraints conflict; the worker still does, as M3.1's did, and the core reads the conflict first. The fallback moves from the C++ bridge to Rust, so that after each solve the worker can check the geometry as FreeCAD's `updateGeometry` does and, if it can't be built, take the next algorithm (FreeCAD's `fc128d357d`): a line of no length, a circle or arc whose radius isn't above 0, or an arc whose ends are at one place. If every solve ends there, the status is `SolvedInvalid`, with the first such solution's values, so the core can name what can't be built. `algorithms` gains `AnchoredSqp`, and takes one to four.

**No arc flips.** The roadmap listed "an arc that flipped" among invalid results. There's nothing to detect: PlaneGCS's arc rules hold only the sines and cosines of an arc's angles, so a solve can leave an angle a turn from where it reads, and FreeCAD writes an arc back by trimming its circle from the start angle to the end angle taken a whole number of turns past it (OCCT's periodic trim): the counter-clockwise arc from the solved start to the solved end, which is how Silico defines an arc from its points. A solution's arc is that arc, whichever way its angles went (a centre dragged far off can leave the end angle a turn below where it reads, and the arc is the right one).

## Outcomes, as data

The core reads the worker's `Solved` in this order (docs/ports/sketcher.md, *Solving*):

| Outcome | When | Error |
|---|---|---|
| Conflicting | the diagnosis found conflicting constraints | `over_constrained`, naming them, in sorted order: no joint is ever among them (a shared point is no constraint), so the suggestion is to remove or change one of them |
| Redundant, disagreeing | a redundant or partially redundant constraint isn't met on the solution within decision 5's tolerance | `over_constrained`, naming those constraints as conflicting with the rest |
| Solver failure | no algorithm solved (`Converged` or `Failed`) | `solve_failed`, naming the sketch, with the suggestion to look for constraints that can't hold together, or to draw the geometry nearer its solution |
| Invalid result | `SolvedInvalid`, naming the geometry that can't be built; or a solve that put a `point_line_distance`'s point, or a line `tangent`'s circle, on the other side of its line from the stored one, naming the constraint (D4: 1.1.3's PlaneGCS has no signed distances) | `invalid_result` (the solve was done, its result refused) |
| Solved | | none: the solution, with its redundant constraints (agreeing) as a warning |

Every solution is checked buildable by the core too, whatever the worker says, and an arc's own ends mirrored about a line its centre is held on are checked whole: the single row FreeCAD sends for them is met by a half circle with the ends unmirrored (FreeCAD 1.1.3 solves it so), which is refused as an invalid result naming the symmetry. A solution past a kilometre can't be built either: the worker passes it over, and the core names the geometry that went there.

**Checked by loads.** M3.4 solves a sketch on every load from the solution its file stores, so the answer Silico keeps must be one its own file gives back. PlaneGCS diagnoses where the sketch is drawn, as FreeCAD does, and tells redundant from conflicting by solving from there, so a load, starting from the solution, can drop other redundant constraints, count other degrees of freedom, call the sketch conflicting, fail to converge, or move it. Each was found: a 10 × 10 template redimensioned 100,000 × 0.01 called conflicting on the drawing; a 31,012 × 1 rectangle whose kept equal length fixed a corner only through the square root of its convergence, 12 nm off; a 2.4 × 1,985 rectangle whose kept perpendicular, a sine DogLeg stops below 1e-10, left a corner 45 nm off, and whose load dropped another constraint and moved it; a far triangle saved Ok that every load refused; saves that named other redundant constraints than their loads; and arcs, nothing repeated, that moved by ulps on load, since a load works an arc's radius and angles out from its stored points, not the solver's values (M3.3's break-it and council). So every solve that reaches a solution is checked by loads: the solution is written into the sketch as the file holds it (`SketchSolution::written`'s helper, the same a save uses: points, circles' radii, reference values), translated again, and solved, until a load leaves the file where it was, bit for bit, at most `MAX_LOAD_RESOLVES` times. That load's answer, solved, stands: its file gives it back, outcome and redundant constraints included, since a load runs the same check from the same file and finds it at once. A solve that leaves its file as drawn is its own load and isn't solved again, so loading a saved sketch costs one solve. A refusal writes no file, so its load solves the drawing again and refuses alike: an over-constrained refusal on the drawing stands unless a load reaches a solved fixed point (SolidWorks solves such a sketch), and other refusals (an invalid result) aren't looked past, since a load from them would measure sides from where they put the geometry. A drawing that solved but whose loads don't settle is refused: as a load refused it, if one did (a far triangle that PlaneGCS calls conflicting, or can't converge on, from its exact solution, where its 1e-10 is under the spacing of doubles, 1.16e-10 mm), or as `solve_failed`, "the sketch's solution doesn't settle on load", if every load moved it (a 1 km arc at some angles, moving by ulps round and round). FreeCAD's feature fails on any redundancy (`SketchObject::execute`); its PlaneGCS classes the 45 nm rectangle's extra constraint redundant, not conflicting, and the rough rectangle's as the solver failing (−4).

**Work and time.** A sketch is at most 1,024 unknowns (`MAX_SKETCH_UNKNOWNS`, down from M3.1's 2,048) and 10,000 constraints, which bounds the worker's work, deterministically: at most 100 iterations of each of the four algorithms, each a dense factorization of the equations the diagnosis kept by the unknowns (about equations × unknowns² a step). The time that work takes is the machine's, bounded only by a sketch solve's own time, 300 s (`SKETCH_TIMEOUT`, settable). Measured: a chain of 512 points held too far apart, which no algorithm solves, took 74 s on a loaded M4 Pro and 206 s at a load of 80, and at 2,048 unknowns about 28 minutes; the same chain with one line given `horizontal` 2,000 times (2,514 equations, every one kept) took DogLeg alone 135 s at a load of 97, and all four pass 300 s: it ends as `worker_timeout` naming the sketch, loud, with nothing written and the worker restarted. No size rule makes time a promise, so none is added (decided on the maintainer's behalf, 2026-10-17). Every solve that reaches a solution, from a drawing off it, is at least two solves (the first and one load), and 1 + `MAX_LOAD_RESOLVES` at most, each with the sketch timeout; a load from a solution DogLeg meets as it starts takes little more than the diagnosis, and a load of a saved sketch, already its solution, is one solve. The count is of the whole sketch; counting its largest part, which PlaneGCS solves apart, is the next step when imports bring bigger sketches.

A solution is each point's coordinates, each circle's radius, each reference dimension's measured value, the degrees of freedom (0 is fully constrained), the geometry the constraints don't fix (any with a free value: SolidWorks' under-defined geometry), the redundant constraints, and the algorithm that solved. Errors name the feature and, as elements, constraint and geometry IDs.

Two error codes are appended to the protected list (the maintainer, 2026-10-17): `over_constrained`, the sketch's constraints conflict, or some repeat others and disagree with them (its elements the constraints to remove or change); and `solve_failed`, no algorithm could meet the constraints from where the geometry is drawn. A solution that can't be built keeps `invalid_result`, whose meaning it is: done, but refused; the feature and the elements it names say it's a sketch's.

## Decision 5: when redundant constraints agree

Decided (the maintainer, 2026-10-16): a sketch whose only problem is redundancy solves and keeps its result if the core, measuring each redundant and partially redundant constraint on the solution geometrically, finds it met within a tolerance tied to the solver's convergence. Its rule was made positional (Fable, 2026-10-18, standing unless the maintainer vetoes it), after break-it found the two fixed tolerances it began with refusing agreeing sketches at both ends of the scale: a 1.6 µm triangle's implied angle 8.1e-9 rad off, past 1e-9 rad, and a 4,289 mm slot's equal sides 2.4e-8 mm off, past 1e-8 mm. The rule:

- **A redundant constraint is met when its gap ≤ ε = max(1e-8 mm, 1e-9 × L).** Its **gap** is the least distance a point of the sketch would have to move for it to hold exactly on the solution, in millimetres. **L** is the sketch's longest lever on the solution: its longest line, or its largest circle or arc radius, whichever is more (construction geometry included, the axes never; 0 for a sketch of points only). `LENGTH_TOLERANCE` (1e-8) is the floor; `LEVER_TOLERANCE` (1e-9) the growth per millimetre of lever.
- **Each form's gap:** a distance's, length's, radius's, or diameter's difference from its value; a point's distance from the curve it's on; a tangency's gap between its curves (`|d − (r1 ± r2)|`, or the centre's distance from the line less the radius); a symmetry's distance between one point and the other's mirror image, and a midpoint's from the line's middle; `horizontal_points` and `vertical_points` the points' offset. `horizontal` and `vertical` are a line's rise and run (PlaneGCS's own residual for these rows). A direction's angle (`parallel`, `perpendicular`, an `angle`'s difference from its value, wrapped) is carried by the smaller lever of its own two lines, and a tangency's directions at its point (`tangent_at`) by the smaller of its curves' levers (a line's length, a circle's or arc's radius); an axis has no lever, since it can't move, so a line against an axis uses the line's length. A `tangent_on`'s gap is the larger of its point's distance off the curve and its directions' carried angle. A gap that isn't a number isn't met.
- **Why, by unit** (Fable, from 1.1.3's vendored PlaneGCS): at a solve that succeeds, every kept row's residual is within 1e-10 in that row's own unit (DogLeg's `fx_inf ≤ 1e-10`, Levenberg–Marquardt's `‖e‖² ≤ 1e-20`, BFGS's and SQP's `½Σr² ≤ 1e-20`). Positional rows (equalities, distances, points on curves, the arc rules) are in millimetres, so a point they hold is within 1e-10 mm; parallel and perpendicular are a sine or cosine, and angles at a point radians, so a direction they hold is within 1e-10 rad, which moves the far end of a lever L by 1e-10 × L. A dropped constraint's gap is those slacks carried through the geometry that implies it, so one positional unit measures every form in the unit the solver's floor is in. The relative term uses the sketch's longest lever, not the constraint's own curves, since the slack reaches a dropped row through whatever carries the kept angular rows (the long slot's sides through its arcs' tangencies), and "its own curves" isn't defined for every form (two points, two circles).
- **The numbers:** 1e-8 mm is a hundredth of the file's nanometre, a hundred times the solver's positional slack, and 86 times the spacing of doubles at a kilometre (1.16e-10 mm). 1e-9 per millimetre is ten times its angular slack, the margin the maintainer chose for angles; it keeps a crisp property: on any sketch under a metre, two dimensions a nanometre apart conflict (1e-9 × 999 mm < 1e-6 mm). The rule accepts all the earlier fixed tolerances did, and loosens only where the solver's own slack says it must: at the gate's limit (L up to 2√2 km) a redundant dimension may disagree by up to 2.8 µm and be kept, as the solver can't tell it apart there.
- **Reported, never silent:** each kept redundant constraint carries its gap (`SketchSolution::redundant`, a map from constraint to gap), logged as "k0000009 repeats others, met within 1.3e-11 mm". A refusal says "constraints repeat others but disagree with them, by up to {gap} mm, past this sketch's {ε} mm", naming them, and suggests SolidWorks' way out too: make a dimension among them a reference (`driving: false`).
- **Evaluated on the answer that stands,** a load's (*Checked by loads*): the gaps and ε are functions of that solution's values, so a save and every load agree. The rule is measured in the core; PlaneGCS's own rows aren't made positional (rescaling its angular rows by their levers would sharpen every solution but change its bits against FreeCAD's and the protected stress reference, the maintainer's to call).
- **Checked by tests:** the oracle's 30°–60°–90° triangle from 200 starts, its implied angle kept, each gap within 1e-9 mm (two kept angles each within 1e-10 rad, carried by its 5 mm side); a length and a horizontal distance a nanometre apart, conflicting, here and a kilometre from the origin; a line 999 mm long given again as 999.000001, conflicting, and 1001 as 1001.000001, kept with its gap of 1e-6 reported; a right angle given 5e-8 rad off between 10 mm lines, conflicting alone and kept beside a 1,000 mm construction line; the µm triangle and the long slot kept; the lever's own cases (points alone 1e-8; a 50 mm line and an 80 mm circle 8e-8; a line parallel to an axis carried by the line); each of the 28 forms at 1% within and past 1e-8 on a sketch whose levers are 10 mm; and a property that on rectangles under 999 mm two dimensions a nanometre apart always conflict.

## The core's API

`silico-core` gains `sketch` (`sketch::solve`): a checked document's sketch feature and a `SketchSolver` (the sketch worker through `worker::Worker`, behind a trait so core tests can use a fake) to a `SketchSolution` or an `Error`. Recompute runs sketches with M3.4, which writes their solutions back. The typed client checks each reply against the request (as many values, tags it sent), as `kernel::KernelRequest` does.

## The oracle

A sketch case kind in `silico-oracle` (`silico_oracle::sketch`): each sketch feature of `tests/oracle/sketches.silico`, a Silico document (its name the case's, its note what the case checks), is solved by Silico's sketch worker and by headless FreeCAD 1.1.3's `sketch.solve()`, built from the same sketch in FreeCAD's model: each shared point as coincident ends (less those an endpoint-to-endpoint tangency joins itself, since it makes its own coincidence), Silico's constraints first, in Silico's order, so FreeCAD's tags are Silico's. Compared: the degrees of freedom; the conflicting, redundant, and partially redundant constraints, FreeCAD's coincidences left out; how each side ends; and, where both solve, every point and radius to 1e-8 mm, every reference value, and the value FreeCAD locked each tangency at an end to, against Silico's direction. FreeCAD refuses a sketch with redundant constraints, as redundant (−2) where they hold on the drawing and as the solver failing (−1) where they don't (PlaneGCS checks them on the starting values); Silico solves it if they agree on the solution and refuses it as over-constrained if they don't, and either matches, only the lists compared. Reference dimensions only in fully constrained cases (docs/ports/sketcher.md). The cases are added only once the maintainer approves their list.

## Tests

Hand counts and analytic solutions for every form; FreeCAD's `CreateRectangleSketch` (degrees of freedom 0), `testBoxCase` and `testSlotCase` (the derived corners and apex), the circle–line distance cases and their reference read-back, `TestCoincidentCommandGui.py`'s solver fixtures (a conflicting coincidence, a horizontal stated twice, a collapsed line refused as invalid); the cases FreeCAD lacks (docs/ports/sketcher.md, *Tests to port*); each direction of `tangent_on` and `tangent_at` at each pair of ends; the symmetric arc with and without its centre held; the anchored SQP step reached; decision 5's cases; the oracle cases above. Then `/break-it` and the council.
