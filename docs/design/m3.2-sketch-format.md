# M3.2 design: sketches in the document

**Status: proposed**, for the maintainer; D2 decided (2026-10-16), the rest open. M3.2 (docs/roadmap/m3-sketcher.md) adds sketches to the `.silico` format: the Rust types, the reader and canonical writer, the schema snapshot (`crates/silico-core/tests/contract/document-schema.json`, protected), and the validation gate's rules for sketches. It doesn't solve them (M3.3), store their solved coordinates beyond reading and writing them (M3.4), or make faces (M3.5). Each decision below has a recommendation and the alternative weighed; docs/ports/sketcher.md is the study behind them.

## Already decided

ARCHITECTURE.md's *Document model* and M2.1's design hold: strict JSON, keys in schema order, unknown keys refused, every field written (no defaults, D6), lengths and angles as `Length` and `Angle` (whole nanometres and nanodegrees, read exactly), features with `id`, `type`, `v`, `name` first, and IDs of 8 lower-case Crockford base32 characters. A sketch is a feature of type `sketch`; ARCHITECTURE.md's example sketches one (`"plane": "XY"`, `points`, `lines` from and to points, `constraints` with `on` and `value`). The study adds: every constraint *form* explicit, so the gate refuses what doesn't fit and the solver never sees a malformed constraint; every choice FreeCAD makes from the current geometry stored instead; tags from positions.

## Decisions for the maintainer

### D1. Which geometry M3.2 has

- **Recommended:** points, lines, circles, and arcs of circles, and construction geometry of each. The sketch worker solves these now (M3.1). Ellipses, arcs of conics, and B-splines come in a later step, each with its internal-alignment geometry (axes, foci, control points), the worker's support, and FreeCAD's tests for it; a new kind in the format is an addition a reader of today refuses, which D5 of M2.1 allows before the first release.
- Alternative: every kind FreeCAD has, now (the roadmap's row lists ellipses and B-splines). That's three times the forms, the internal-alignment rules, and the B-spline end-point question (open decision 6) in one step.

### D2. Shared points or FreeCAD's coincidences

FreeCAD gives each curve its own end points and joins them with `Coincident` constraints; ARCHITECTURE.md's example has lines going `from` and `to` points the sketch lists.

**Decided (the maintainer, 2026-10-16): shared points, merged the way SolidWorks merges points** (Fusion 360 does the same). A sketch lists its points; a line goes `from` one point `to` another; an arc has a `center`, a `start`, and an `end` point (counter-clockwise from start to end); a circle has a `center` point and a `radius`. Two curves that meet share a point, so there is no coincidence constraint at all: a closed profile is visible in the file as points used twice, a diff of a moved corner is one line, and deleting a curve leaves its neighbours joined without the re-pointing FreeCAD does (`SketchObjectConstraints.cpp:1036-1247`). The solver gets the same point's two values for both curves, which is what PlaneGCS's own substitution of coincidences does anyway (`initSolution`). The alternative weighed was FreeCAD's model, each curve with its own ends and coincidences between them: it imports one to one, but a file grows a constraint per joint, and an agent has to add four constraints to close a rectangle.

What follows from it, each settled here or recorded where it's built:

- **Ends that touch but aren't joined.** Two curves whose ends are separate points at the same place look closed and aren't: the profile is open there. M3.5's profile check finds them: an open profile whose two open ends are within FreeCAD's joining distance (1e-7 mm, `makeElementWires`, where FreeCAD would have closed it) is refused as data naming both points, with the suggestion "share the point". `silico check` warns about any two non-construction curve ends within that distance that are separate points, naming them. `silico fmt` never merges them: it may offer to (`silico fmt --merge-touching` names each pair it merges), never silently.
- **Arcs** (the one point for the maintainer to see): a center, a start, and an end point are six numbers for an arc's five degrees of freedom, so the stored points must lie on one circle, and a hand edit of a dimension leaves them off it until M3.4's re-solve on load. **Recommended: (a), the file holds the points only, and their agreement is the solve's, checked after it, not by the gate.** The gate refuses only what no solve can mend (below: an arc's start or end at its center, or start and end the same point). The solver takes the arc's radius and angles as values it works out from the points (the radius from the center to the start, the angles of the start and the end, the end's taken past the start's counter-clockwise), computed in the sketch worker, whose math is the libm shim's, so they're the same on every platform; PlaneGCS's arc rules (`addConstraintArcRules`, as M3.1 sends them) then hold the ends on the arc, and a solve that fails names the arc (M3.3). Saved, the points lie on the circle to the solver's convergence, written in full (D3).
  - (b), FreeCAD's storage (a center, a radius, and start and end angles, the ends derived), doesn't avoid it with shared points: an arc's end that a line shares is a point the sketch lists with its coordinates, so the end is stored twice (as the point, and as the angles) and a hand edit can make them disagree just the same; and two arcs sharing an end would need a coincidence between one's angles and the other's, the constraint shared points remove. FreeCAD's own storage can't disagree with itself because it has no shared points; its joints are coincidences, which a hand edit can break and only its recompute's solve mends, which is what (a) does for an arc's radius.
- **Diagnostics** (M3.3): with no coincidence constraints, a joint is never something to remove. A conflict at a joint names other constraints to remove (PlaneGCS's diagnosis only lists constraints, and the arc rules, tag 0, never), and no suggestion points at the joint itself; unjoining is an edit, not a fix the diagnosis offers.
- **Editing** (M5's sketch mode and the command API): trim, split, delete, and pulling a corner apart create or split shared points explicitly; unmerging is a deliberate act, as in SolidWorks; deleting a curve keeps its neighbours' shared point. Recorded in ROADMAP.md, M5.
- **FreeCAD import:** coincident ends merge into one point, chains of three or more and arc centers included; a coincidence that can't merge (a point on a curve) becomes an `on` constraint (D4). Recorded in docs/roadmap/freecad-import.md, with what happens to suppressed coincidences and to unsolved sketches.

### D3. How solved coordinates are written (open decision 4)

A point's coordinates are the solver's output. ARCHITECTURE.md says "Solved sketch coordinates are written in full", and also that lengths are whole nanometres.

- **Recommended: in full.** A point's `x` and `y` are a `Coordinate`: an `f64`, finite, within a kilometre, written as Rust writes it (the shortest decimal that reads back as the same `f64`, the same on every platform) and read correctly rounded (Rust's own parser, the same on every platform). The solver starts from exactly where it last ended, so re-solving an unchanged sketch gives the same bits, and M3.4's "unchanged sketch, unchanged bytes" holds by construction. Dimensions (D4's `value`s) stay `Length` and `Angle`: those are what people type, exact. A hand-written `40` is 40.0.
- Alternative: whole nanometres. Diffs read `40` instead of `39.99999999999999`, but the solver then starts from rounded values, so each save could move a point by up to half a nanometre from the last, and a check that stored coordinates still satisfy the constraints needs a tolerance per constraint form that rounding allows (an angle on a 1 mm line, off by a nanometre, is off by 1e-6 rad).

### D4. Constraints, form by form

Every constraint has an `id`, a `type`, and the references and values its form takes; a reference names a point, line, circle, or arc of this sketch by its ID. The forms, from FreeCAD's types (docs/ports/sketcher.md, *Constraints*), one type each so none has optional references:

| `type` | Takes | Means | FreeCAD |
|---|---|---|---|
| `horizontal` | `line` | the line parallel to x | `Horizontal` |
| `vertical` | `line` | parallel to y | `Vertical` |
| `horizontal_points`, `vertical_points` | `a`, `b` (points) | the two points level, or one above the other | `Horizontal`/`Vertical` on two points |
| `length` | `line`, `value` (length) | the line's length | `Distance` on a line |
| `distance` | `a`, `b` (points), `value` | the distance between two points | `Distance`, point to point |
| `distance_x`, `distance_y` | `a`, `b` (points), `value` (signed length) | x(b) − x(a), y(b) − y(a) | `DistanceX`, `DistanceY` |
| `point_line_distance` | `point`, `line`, `value`, `side` | the point's distance from the line, on the given side of it (`left` or `right`, facing from the line's start to its end) | `Distance`, point to line |
| `on` | `point`, `curve` (line, circle, or arc) | the point on the curve (a line extended, an arc's whole circle, as FreeCAD) | `PointOnObject` |
| `parallel`, `perpendicular` | `a`, `b` (lines) | | `Parallel`, `Perpendicular` |
| `angle` | `a`, `b` (lines), `value` (angle) | the angle from `a`'s direction to `b`'s, counter-clockwise | `Angle` on two lines |
| `radius`, `diameter` | `curve` (circle or arc), `value` | | `Radius`, `Diameter` (sent as a radius of half the value) |
| `equal` | `a`, `b` (two lines, or two circles or arcs) | equal lengths, or equal radii | `Equal` |
| `tangent` | `a`, `b` (a line and a circle or arc, or two circles or arcs), `side` | tangent along their edges: for a line, `side` is the side of the line the curve is on; for two curves, `outside` or `inside` | `Tangent`, edge to edge |
| `tangent_at` | `a`, `b` (curves sharing the point `at`), `at`, `direction` | tangent where they meet, their directions there the `same` (a smooth join) or `opposite` (a cusp) | `Tangent`, endpoint to endpoint (its value ±π/2) |
| `symmetric` | `a`, `b` (points), `about` (a line or a point) | | `Symmetric` |
| `midpoint` | `point`, `line` | the point midway along the line | `Symmetric` of the line's ends about a point |

- **Reference dimensions:** every dimensional form (`length` through `diameter`) has `driving` (`true` or `false`, always written); a reference one's `value` is what the last solve measured, written back by M3.4, never solved for.
- **Stored choices:** `side` and `direction` are required where a form has them, never decided from the geometry. With 1.1.3's PlaneGCS, which has no signed distances (open decision 1), the core checks them after the solve: a solve that lands on the other side is refused as an invalid result, naming the constraint.
- **Not in M3.2:** FreeCAD's `Block`, `SnellsLaw`, `Weight`, `InternalAlignment` (with D1's later kinds), an angle via a point (`Angle` with three references), and a point fixed in place (FreeCAD fixes points with `DistanceX`/`DistanceY` from the origin, which `distance_x` and `distance_y` from the origin point do here: D6).
- Alternative: FreeCAD's types with optional references (`distance` with one, two, or three of them). Fewer type names, but the gate then decides the form from which fields are there, which is what FreeCAD's "malformed" comes from.

### D5. IDs and order inside a sketch

ARCHITECTURE.md leaves open "Sketch entity ordering: creation order or sorted by ID."

- **Recommended:** points, lines, circles, arcs, and constraints each have an 8-character ID like a feature's, unique within the sketch, from the same generator; each list is written **sorted by ID**. Random IDs keep parallel branches from colliding, and sorting spreads two branches' additions through the list instead of both at its end, so fewer merges conflict; `fmt` restores the order after a hand edit. A constraint's solver tag is its place in the sorted list, plus one.
- Alternative: creation order, IDs `p1`, `l1`, `c1` as in ARCHITECTURE.md's example. Reads well, but two branches that each add a point both make `p5`.

### D6. Where a sketch sits

- **Recommended:** `"plane": "xy"`, `"xz"`, or `"yz"`, FreeCAD's origin planes with their orientation (XZ: the sketch's x along +X, its y along +Z, its normal −Y; YZ: x along +Y, y along +Z, normal +X), and the origin point and the two axes as fixed references every sketch has: `origin`, `x_axis`, `y_axis` (FreeCAD's `RootPoint`, `H_Axis`, `V_Axis`), which constraints and a revolve's axis may name. Sketches on a model's faces and on offset planes come with naming (M2.7–M2.8).
- Alternative: a full placement (origin and rotation) now. Exact rotations need care (D3's question again, for angles), and nothing in M3 needs them.

### D7. Construction geometry

- **Recommended:** `"construction": true` or `false` on every point, line, circle, and arc (always written). Construction geometry is solved like any other and left out of the profile (FreeCAD's rule).

## The gate's rules for sketches

From the study's list of what FreeCAD misses (docs/ports/sketcher.md, *The sketch feature*), each refused as `invalid_input`, naming the feature, the field (`constraints.3.line`), and the element:
- every ID is of the right form and unique within its sketch; every reference names an element of this sketch (or `origin`, `x_axis`, `y_axis`) of a kind the form takes;
- a line's ends are two points; a circle's radius and every `length`, `distance`, `radius`, and `diameter` value is above zero (`distance_x`, `distance_y`, and `angle` are signed); a point's coordinates are finite and within a kilometre;
- a dimension names at least one element of the sketch's own (not only the origin and axes: FreeCAD's `3d7871fca5`); a constraint names no element twice where its form needs two (a line parallel to itself);
- no geometry drawn with no size: a line whose ends are the same point, a circle of radius 0, an arc whose start or end is its center or whose start and end are the same point;
- the sizes the sketch worker takes (`MAX_SKETCH_UNKNOWNS` and the others, silico-protocol), so a document the gate admits is one the worker can solve.

Not the gate's: an arc's start and end at different distances from its center (D2, arcs), which the re-solve mends or names; and two ends at the same place that aren't one point (D2), which `silico check` warns about and M3.5 refuses in a profile.

## How M3.2 is built

- **Where:** `silico-core`'s `document` module: a `Sketch` operation with its own types (`SketchPoint`, `SketchLine`, ..., `SketchConstraint`), and a `Coordinate` beside `Length` and `Angle` (D3).
- **Reader and writer:** as M2.1's: exact, canonical, one entity per line (M2.1's D7). Lists sorted by ID on writing (D5); the reader takes any order.
- **Schema:** generated, and the snapshot updated, with the maintainer's agreement (it's protected).
- **Errors:** `invalid_input`, no new code.
- **Tests:** a byte-stable round trip of the example and of generated sketches; every gate rule refused with its field and element; FreeCAD's `testValidateConstraintsCase` (a constraint naming missing geometry) and `testGetPointFromGeom*` (what start, end, and centre mean, for each kind M3.2 has).

## Not in M3.2

Solving (M3.3), writing solved coordinates back and re-solving on load (M3.4), faces (M3.5), pad, pocket, and revolve (M3.6), D1's later kinds, sketches on faces, and expressions in dimensions.
