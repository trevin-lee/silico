# M3.5: profile to face

M3.5 (docs/roadmap/m3-sketcher.md) turns a solved sketch into the faces a pad, pocket, or revolve sweeps (M3.6). The port note, docs/ports/profile.md, says what FreeCAD does, what it gets wrong (measured on 1.1.3), and what the core computes. This note settles its open questions. Decision 2 (the maintainer, 2026-10-16) stands: the core decides loops, refusals, nesting, and direction from the solved 2D geometry, and the worker only builds the faces it's told to.

## The two halves

- **`silico_core::profile`** takes a sketch and its `SketchSolution` (M3.3, M3.4) and gives its `Profile`: regions, each an outer loop and its holes, every loop its curves in walking order, already oriented. Or it gives the refusals, as data. It's pure computation on solved coordinates, so it needs no worker, and its tests need no OCCT.
- **The kernel's `MakeFaces` request** takes a plane and the regions, and builds one face per region, each edge from one curve and each vertex from one point. It returns them as a compound with its history, so naming reads which curve each edge came from. It checks the faces and classifies nothing. A face OCCT calls invalid is an `invalid_result`.

## The open questions, settled

1. **The tolerance:** 1e-7 mm, inclusive, wherever M3.5 asks whether two things meet: near-closed ends, touching curves, and slivers (area ≤ 1e-7 × perimeter). It's `LENGTH_TOLERANCE`, OCCT's `Precision::Confusion()`, and what `Profile::check` already uses. It joins and moves nothing. Exact predicates would still have to refuse a hole 1e-9 from its boundary, which OCCT builds as touching, so they'd need the same distance test anyway.
2. **The order:**
   - regions by their outer loop's smallest curve ID;
   - holes in a region by their smallest curve ID;
   - each loop starting at its smallest curve ID and walking from there.
   
   IDs are stable and independent of coordinates, so a drag that moves geometry never reorders faces or edges, and naming (M2.7, sketch edges from their IDs) doesn't depend on it at all. FreeCAD's order (largest squared diagonal, then the sketch's geometry order) follows size, so a drag can reorder faces. That's the order behind its topological naming problem.
3. **Arcs to the worker:** centre, start, and end points, with the angles the core works out with `libm` (as M3.3's translation does), so the worker builds exactly what it's sent and the edge's ends are the shared vertices.
4. **A request of its own:** `MakeFaces` is a kernel step, keyed and cached like any other, and an extrusion (M3.6) takes its result as an input. The faces then have a history of their own (curve to edge), and a sweep's history composes with it, as a boolean's composes with its inputs'.
5. **The error code:** `invalid_input`, naming the sketch (its feature) and the elements at fault, with their total, as the gate does. It's the sketch, as the user drew it, that can't make a profile. No new code joins the protected list.
6. **When it's checked:** only when a feature uses the sketch (M3.6), as in FreeCAD and SolidWorks. A sketch nothing sweeps may be open or crossing (construction drawings, reference geometry), and `silico check` doesn't complain about it. `silico show` can say whether a sketch makes a profile once M3.6 exists.
7. **Several regions:** the profile has every region. Whether a feature takes several is M3.6's (decision 3: a pad refuses more than one, naming them; a pocket may take several, since it leaves one solid).
8. **Ellipses and B-splines** aren't in the format yet (D1's later kinds). When they come, ellipses get closed-form tests and B-splines their own design.

## What's refused

Each refusal is an `invalid_input` naming the sketch and the elements at fault, with a suggestion. In the order the core checks them:

1. **No profile curve** (none, or construction only): "the sketch has no curves to make a profile of".
2. **An open end** (a point that ends exactly one profile curve), naming the point and its curve. Two open ends within 1e-7 are named together with "share the point" (D2).
3. **A point where three or more profile curves meet**, naming it and its curves: "a profile's points each join two curves".
4. **Two curves that meet away from a shared point** (crossing, overlapping, touching, or a point of one on the other, within 1e-7), naming both, and the point where one is a point.
5. **A loop with no area** (|area| ≤ 1e-7 × perimeter), naming its curves.

Every refusal of a kind is listed, not just the first. A sketch at the size limit has at most 10,000 curves.

## Tests

As the port note lists them:
- every *Edge cases* row as a core test: the refusal, its elements and their total, or the regions, their depth and their direction;
- FreeCAD's FaceMakerBullseye tests that port, as faces from the worker, with areas against analytic values;
- the nesting cases also as oracle cases, which the maintainer adds, since `tests/oracle/` is protected;
- `/break-it` at each tolerance boundary and at scale.

docs/ports/sketcher.md's *Profiles to faces* section is corrected where the port note found it wrong.
