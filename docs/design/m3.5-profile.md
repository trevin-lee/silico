# M3.5: profile to face

M3.5 (docs/roadmap/m3-sketcher.md) turns a solved sketch into the faces a pad, pocket, or revolve sweeps (M3.6). The port note, docs/ports/profile.md, says what FreeCAD does, what it gets wrong (measured on 1.1.3), and what the core computes. This note settles its open questions. Decision 2 (the maintainer, 2026-10-16) stands: the core decides loops, refusals, nesting, and direction from the solved 2D geometry, and the worker only builds the faces it's told to.

## The two halves

- **`silico_core::profile`** takes a sketch of a document the gate checked (so at most 1,024 unknowns, and at most 341 loops, a circle's 3 apiece) and its `SketchSolution` (M3.3, M3.4) and gives its `Profile`: regions, each an outer loop and its holes, every loop its curves in walking order, already oriented. Or it gives the refusals, as data. It's pure computation on solved coordinates, so it needs no worker, and its tests need no OCCT.
- **The kernel's `MakeFaces` request** takes a plane and the regions, and builds one face per region, each edge from one curve and each vertex from one point. It returns them as a compound with its history, so naming reads which curve each edge came from. It checks the faces and classifies nothing. A face OCCT calls invalid is an `invalid_result`.

## The open questions, settled

1. **The tolerance:** 1e-7 mm, inclusive, wherever M3.5 asks whether two things meet: near-closed ends, two curves within it of each other away from a point they share, and slivers (a loop's |area|, or a region's net area against its total perimeter, at most 1e-7 × the perimeter). It's `silico_protocol::LENGTH_TOLERANCE`, OCCT's `Precision::Confusion()`, and what `Profile::check` already uses. It joins and moves nothing. Decided on the maintainer's side (2026-10-18), with OCCT's behaviour measured: OCCT builds a hole 1e-9 from its boundary as a valid face with the right area, and then a pad fused onto it can silently drop (docs/ports/profile.md, *The tolerance*), so exact predicates would let near loops through to come back valid and wrong; and an arc's end lies on its circle only to the solve's convergence, so no predicate is exact for arcs anyway. `silico_core::sketch`'s 1e-8, decision 5's redundancy gap, is `REDUNDANCY_GAP_MM`, not this.
2. **The order:**
   - regions by their outer loop's smallest curve ID;
   - holes in a region by their smallest curve ID;
   - each loop starting at its smallest curve ID and walking from there.
   
   IDs are stable and independent of coordinates, so a drag that moves geometry never reorders faces or edges, and naming (M2.7, sketch edges from their IDs) doesn't depend on it at all. FreeCAD's order (largest squared diagonal, then the sketch's geometry order) follows size, so a drag can reorder faces. That's the order behind its topological naming problem.
3. **Arcs to the worker:** centre, start, and end points, with the angles the core works out with `libm` (as M3.3's translation does), so the worker builds exactly what it's sent and the edge's ends are the shared vertices.
4. **A request of its own:** `MakeFaces` is a kernel step, keyed and cached like any other, and an extrusion (M3.6) takes its result as an input. The faces then have a history of their own (curve to edge), and a sweep's history composes with it, as a boolean's composes with its inputs'.
5. **The error code:** `invalid_input`, naming the sketch (its feature) and the elements at fault, with their total, as the gate does. It's the sketch, as the user drew it, that can't make a profile. No new code joins the protected list.
6. **When it's checked:** only when a feature uses the sketch (M3.6), as in FreeCAD and SolidWorks. A sketch nothing sweeps may be open or crossing (construction drawings, reference geometry), and `silico check` doesn't complain about it. `silico show` can say whether a sketch makes a profile once M3.6 exists.
7. **Several regions:** the profile has every region. Whether a feature takes several is M3.6's (decision 3: a pad refuses more than one, naming them; a pocket may take several, since it leaves one solid).
8. **Ellipses and B-splines** aren't in the format yet (D1's later kinds). When they come, ellipses get closed-form tests and B-splines their own design.

## What's refused

Each refusal is an `invalid_input` naming the sketch and the elements at fault, with a suggestion. In the order the core checks them:

1. **No profile curve** (none, or construction only): "the sketch has no curves to make a profile of".
2. **A curve too small to build**: a radius of at most 1e-7 (an arc's or a circle's), or a line's or an arc's ends at most 1e-7 apart, by the kernel request's own rule (`silico_protocol::Curve::size_problem`) on the curve as the request would get it. Each is named with its points, once each, with the measured value: "line n is 5e-8 mm long: a profile's lines and arcs are longer than 1e-7 mm between their ends" ("lengthen it, or remove it and end its neighbours at one point"), "arc a's ends are … mm apart: …" ("draw a circle instead, or open the arc"), "arc a's radius is … mm: a profile's arcs and circles have a radius above 1e-7 mm" ("draw it larger, or remove it"). It's the tolerance's rule applied to one curve (two places within 1e-7 are one place), so "share the point" is never the advice for one curve's own ends. In the same step, by the request's `Curve::off_circle`, an arc whose end is more than 1e-7 from its circle (the circle about its centre through its start), naming the arc, its centre, and its ends: "arc a's end is … mm from its circle (the circle about its centre through its start): a profile's arcs end within 1e-7 mm of their circles" ("solve the sketch again, or put the arc's end on its circle"). Checked before the rest, which they would only confuse.
3. **An open end** (a point that ends exactly one profile curve), naming the point and its curve. Two open ends within 1e-7 are named together with "share the point" (D2).
4. **A point where three or more profile curves meet**, naming it and its curves: "a profile's points each join two curves".
5. **Two curves that meet away from a shared point**, within 1e-7 of each other, naming both, and the point where it's an end of one that's near the other. The message says what was found: "line a and line b cross", "arc a and arc b overlap", "line a and arc b are 5e-8 mm apart: a profile's curves must be more than 1e-7 mm apart except at a point they share", or "point p, the end of line a, is 0 mm from arc b: …" (the rule likewise).
6. **A loop with no area** (|area| ≤ 1e-7 × perimeter), naming its curves: "loop a, b, c encloses no area to speak of (… mm², against a perimeter of … mm)"; and **a region with no area**, its outer loop's area less its holes' against the perimeter of them all (a ring between loops that each have area), naming its loops' curves: "the region inside loop a, b, c less its holes encloses no area to speak of (…)". On a thin rectangle the rule's width is about 2e-7.

Every refusal of a kind is listed, not just the first. A sketch at the size limit has at most 10,000 curves.

## Tests

As the port note lists them:
- every *Edge cases* row as a core test: the refusal, its elements and their total, or the regions, their depth and their direction;
- FreeCAD's FaceMakerBullseye tests that port, as faces from the worker, with areas against analytic values;
- the nesting cases also as oracle cases, which the maintainer adds, since `tests/oracle/` is protected;
- `/break-it` at each tolerance boundary and at scale.

docs/ports/sketcher.md's *Profiles to faces* section is corrected where the port note found it wrong.
