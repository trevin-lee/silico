# M3.6: sketch-driven features

**Status: approved by the maintainer** (2026-10-18, relayed by Main), as revised by Fable's review of the same day: S10 records what was approved. M3.6 (docs/roadmap/m3-sketcher.md) adds pad, pocket, revolve, and groove: features that sweep a sketch's profile into a solid, and fuse it with a base or cut it from one. It changes the document's schema, a protected contract. The port note is docs/ports/sketch-features.md: what FreeCAD 1.1.3 does in the modes M3.6 builds, its edge cases and bug fixes, and the tests that port. This note settles the rest, each with a recommendation, what FreeCAD and SolidWorks do, and the alternative weighed.

## Already decided

These hold, and M3.6 builds on them as they stand:

- **D2** (M2.1): every feature makes one solid, and consumes the solids it takes as inputs.
- **Decision 2** (M3): the core decides loops, nesting, refusals, and direction from the solved sketch; the worker builds what it's told.
- **Decision 3** (M3): a pad of disjoint regions is refused, naming them. A pocket may take several.
- **M3.5** (docs/design/m3.5-profile.md): the profile (`silico_core::profile`), its refusals as `invalid_input` naming the sketch and its elements, the 1e-7 tolerance, regions ordered by their outer loop's smallest curve ID, and the kernel's `MakeFaces` request as a step of its own, which a sweep takes as its input.
- **N8, the operation in element names** (Fable's decision of 2026-10-18, being built by another worker, not merged): every name an operation makes writes its type word (`cccccccc.cut.Edge(...)`), and N7's collapse holds only where `OpCode::bites(input)`. Pieces kept inside another operand get a one-`M` derived name. `NAMING_SCHEME` becomes 3.
- **N9, the side of a source** (Fable's decision of 2026-10-18, being built into #42, recorded as N9 in docs/design/m2.7-naming.md when it lands): what a cut keeps of its tool and what a common keeps of its operands is the feature's own, with a new source letter `I` (inside), whole or trimmed. The rule, for every feature: a name fixes the side of every source from another feature, by the type word where the word fixes it, and by `I` where it doesn't. `bites` is per (type word, step, input). `NAMING_SCHEME` becomes 4, so M3.6 takes 5. No `I` ever appears in a sweep feature's names (S4).
- **MA1** (Worker 1's commit `8751ec4`, on `m2-milestone-fixes`, not merged): a fillet's or chamfer's step waits only for its base, and when it runs it names its base's map from the maps already named (`Plan::sources`), waiting for any solid not yet built.
- Behavior follows SolidWorks; visuals and UI follow Fusion 360. Never copy FreeCAD's UX.

## S1. The features in the document

### The fields

**Recommended:** four feature types, `pad`, `pocket`, `revolve`, and `groove`, after `id`, `type`, `v`, and `name`:

| Field | `pad` | `pocket` | `revolve` | `groove` | What it holds |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `base` | optional | required | optional | required | The feature whose solid it fuses with (pad, revolve) or cuts (pocket, groove), consumed (D2). |
| `sketch` | required | required | required | required | The sketch whose profile it sweeps: a feature's ID. |
| `axis` | | | required | required | What it turns about: `x_axis`, `y_axis`, or the ID of a line of the sketch. |
| `extent` | required | required | required | required | How far it goes, as below. |

`extent` is an object whose `type` says its form, and each form holds exactly the values it needs:

| Form | For | Holds | Means |
|---|---|---|---|
| `{"type": "one_side", "length": L, "reversed": false}` | pad, pocket | a length above 0 | from the sketch's plane, `L` along the feature's direction (below), or against it when `reversed` |
| `{"type": "symmetric", "length": L}` | pad, pocket | a length above 0 | `L/2` each way from the plane: one prism (FreeCAD's *Symmetric*, one prism since `c384a52fa5`) |
| `{"type": "two_sides", "length": L, "length2": L2}` | pad, pocket | two lengths above 0 | `L` along the direction and `L2` against it: one prism (FreeCAD's *Two sides*, one prism since `a01cab21cc`) |
| `{"type": "through_all", "reversed": false}` | pocket | | through everything of its base along the direction (FreeCAD's `ThroughAll`) |
| `{"type": "one_side", "angle": A, "reversed": false}` | revolve, groove | an angle above 0° and at most 360° | `A` right-handed about the axis, or left-handed when `reversed` |
| `{"type": "symmetric", "angle": A}` | revolve, groove | the same | `A/2` each way from the sketch (FreeCAD's `Midplane`) |

- **Units:** lengths in millimetres (`Length`, whole nanometres), angles in degrees (`Angle`, whole nanodegrees), as every value the file holds.
- **Directions.** A pad goes along the sketch's normal (D6: +Z on `xy`, −Y on `xz`, +X on `yz`), a pocket against it, as in FreeCAD and SolidWorks. A revolve turns right-handed about its axis's direction: `x_axis` along the sketch's +x, `y_axis` along +y, a line from its `from` point to its `to`.
- **Defaults:** none. Every field is written (M2.1's D6), except `base` where it's optional, which is left out when there's none, never `null`.

Why these forms:

- **`reversed` only where it changes the solid.** FreeCAD's flat properties (`Type`, `Length`, `Length2`, `Midplane`, `Reversed`) spell some solids several ways: two lengths reversed is the same prism as the lengths swapped, and a symmetric pad reversed is the same prism as one that isn't. Here neither `symmetric` nor `two_sides` has `reversed`, so those spellings are gone. Not every duplicate is: `two_sides` with `length` and `length2` both L is the same prism as `symmetric` with 2L. That one is harmless (SolidWorks and Fusion offer both modes too), and the two keep their own meanings when an edit makes the lengths differ.
- **No optional values.** Each form holds what it needs and nothing else, so the gate never decides a form from which fields are present, the source of FreeCAD's "malformed" constraints.
- **The words FreeCAD 1.1.3 and Fusion share.** FreeCAD 1.1.3's pad and pocket say *One side*, *Two sides*, and *Symmetric* (`SideType`, `a346c266e7`, which deprecated `Midplane` and `TwoLengths`), and `ThroughAll`. Fusion 360's extrude says *One Side*, *Two Sides*, *Symmetric*, and *All* (visuals and UI follow Fusion). SolidWorks says *Blind*, *Mid Plane*, *Direction 2*, and *Through All*. FreeCAD 1.1.3's revolution still says `Angle` with `Midplane`.
- **`sketch`, not ARCHITECTURE.md's `profile`.** The field names a sketch feature; a profile is what the core makes of it. When regions of a sketch can be chosen (decision 8), a `regions` field joins it. ARCHITECTURE.md's example (`"profile": "k7f2qx3m", "length": 10`) is updated with the schema.
- **The base is the pad's, not a body's.** A pad with a `base` fuses with that feature's solid and consumes it, as a boolean does (D2): one modeling workflow, any solid as the target (invariant 7). A first pad has no base. SolidWorks' *Merge result* and FreeCAD's body tip do the same with state the file can't diff.
- **Depth above 0, with `reversed`**, as SolidWorks: it refuses a depth of 0 or less and offers *Reverse Direction*. FreeCAD takes negative lengths and refuses only a total of zero (`a346c266e7`, after `6167e9f77a` and `b2520d489a`).
- **A pocket and a groove need a base.** FreeCAD's pocket with no base silently becomes its tool, a positive solid (since `ecf7e51ab3` dropped the error), and so does its groove (`df5c75d6b0`). SolidWorks' cut needs a body. Silico refuses it by the schema.
- **Not in M3.6:** up-to modes (first, last, face, shape), through all both ways, offsets, tapers, custom directions, two-angle revolves (main's, after 1.1.3), thin features, and through all for a groove (a whole turn says it). Each is a later form of `extent`, an addition a reader of today refuses (M2.1's D5).
- Alternative weighed: FreeCAD's flat fields, `length`, `length2`, `midplane`, `reversed`. Fewer objects, but two spellings of some solids, and fields that mean nothing in some modes.

### The gate's rules

Each refused as `invalid_input`, naming the feature and the field, as the gate does today:

- `sketch` is the ID of a feature before this one, and that feature is a sketch ("sketch k7f2qx3m is Block, a box, not a sketch"). A sketch isn't consumed: several features may use one (S2).
- `base`, where present, follows the boolean's rules: a feature before this one that makes a solid, not already consumed.
- `extent.length` and `extent.length2` are above 0 and at most a kilometre (`MAX_LENGTH`); for `two_sides`, their sum, taken in nanometres, is at most a kilometre too, since that's the prism's length. (Any length above 0 is at least 1 nm, ten times OCCT's tolerance.)
- `extent.angle` is above 0° and at most 360°, in nanodegrees, exactly.
- `axis` is `x_axis`, `y_axis`, or the ID of a line of the sketch (construction or not). `origin`, a point, a circle, an arc, or an ID the sketch doesn't have is refused, naming the field: "axis 0a1b2c3d is a point of sketch s0000003: a revolve turns about a line".
- `through_all` on a pad is refused by the schema (FreeCAD's pad has no through all; its "up to last" waits for the up-to modes).

What only a rebuild can tell, once the sketch is solved, is refused there (S5, S6, S7).

## S2. A sketch is used, not consumed

**Recommended:** any number of features may use one sketch. A sketch makes no solid, so D2's consumption doesn't apply to it. SolidWorks shares a sketch between features, and FreeCAD lets several features take one profile. S4's rule makes it safe: nothing in a solid keeps a sketch's name, so two features of one sketch never give two elements of one solid the same name.

Alternative: one feature per sketch, as SolidWorks absorbs a sketch into its feature by default. It keeps N2's premise (each element's lineage a tree) without S4's rule, but it's a limit no user expects, and S4 needs the rule anyway for the bottom face to say which feature it's in.

## S3. Steps, keys, and when they're planned

### Solve first, then plan

A sweep's steps depend on its profile, and the profile on the sketch's solve, which only the sketch worker gives. M3.4 solves sketches one at a time, beside the kernel's steps, keyed by the sketch as written.

**Recommended:** a recompute solves every sketch some feature uses, and makes its profile, before it plans; then it plans every step, as today, so every key is still known before any kernel step runs. Sketches nothing uses solve beside the steps, as now. Unchanged sketches cost nothing: their solves are kept in memory by key (M3.4), and so are their profiles. A solve takes milliseconds, so the kernel's first step waits that long.

- A sketch that doesn't solve fails as now (M3.4), and each feature using it is `Blocked` on it.
- A sketch that solves but makes no profile fails each feature using it, with every refusal M3.5 words (each `invalid_input` naming the sketch and its elements): the first hundred and how many, as `FeatureOutcome::Broken` lists broken references. Features using a feature that failed so are blocked on it, as on any failure. The sketch itself is solved, not failed: a sketch nothing sweeps may be open (M3.5's question 6).
- Alternative weighed: keys made late, as each solve lands, so other features' steps start sooner. But every key downstream of a late one is late too, and the scheduler would need a second way to wait, beside MA1's wait for a solid whose key is known. Not worth it while a solve takes milliseconds.

### The steps

A sweep feature is two steps, or three with a base:

1. **Faces**: the profile's regions as `KernelRequest::make_faces` sends them (`Profile::faces`). No inputs.
2. **Sweep**: the faces moved or turned to where the sweep starts, then swept. A new kernel operation over a shape, beside M1's polygon `Extrude` and `Revolve` (which the stress test and the oracle's sweep cases keep using): a prism by a start offset and a vector, or a revolution by an axis, a start angle, and an angle. One step rather than a `Translate` and a sweep, so the history has one level. Its history, per profile element (face, edge, vertex), from OCCT's own answers:
   - its **start** counterpart (OCCT's `FirstShape(element)`: the element itself, or its moved copy) as its one `modified` element, never `kept`, as `Translate` reports a moved element (docs/ports/booleans.md);
   - its **end** counterpart (OCCT's `LastShape(element)`) as `generated`, of the element's own kind;
   - its **sides** as `generated`, as OCCT's `Generated` gives them (an edge's face, a vertex's edge, a face's solid).

   So the start and end are per source, and `History::ends`' whole-shape lists aren't needed for these operations: they're left empty (M1's polygon sweeps keep them, for their own tests). A whole turn has neither. docs/ports/history.md says so when it's built, and `History::validate` and `fits` accept it.
3. **Boolean**, with a base: M2's `Boolean` step, `Fuse` for pad and revolve, `Cut` for pocket and groove, base first.

The sweep's values come from the document's integers, so they're exact or rounded once:

- The sketch's normal `n` is a unit axis (±1 in one component), so `L · n` is exact. The feature's direction `d` is `n` for a pad, `−n` for a pocket, negated when `reversed`.
- `one_side`: start 0, vector `L · d`. `symmetric`: start `−(L/2) · d` (halving is exact), vector `L · d`. `two_sides`: start `−L2 · d`, vector `(L + L2) · d`, the sum taken in nanometres before it's converted.
- A revolve's axis is placed by `SketchPlane::place`: `x_axis` from the origin along the plane's x, `y_axis` along its y, a line from its solved `from` to its solved `to`. The angle goes to radians by `silico_protocol::radians`, FreeCAD's conversion. `symmetric` starts at minus half of it (halving is exact).
- `through_all`: FreeCAD's length, 2.02 × the diagonal of the box around the base and the profile (`43de589732`), from the sketch's plane along `d`. The base's box is known only once it's built, so the through-all sweep step takes the base's solid as a second input, used only for its size: its key holds the base's key, and the worker sizes the prism by the rule in `silico-protocol`, which the core's tests check. Its history has one input, the faces. The box is OCCT's tight one (`BRepBndLib::AddOptimal`, as Silico's measures take it), not FreeCAD's `BRepBndLib::Add`, which reads a triangulation when the shape has one, so FreeCAD's length depends on whether the shape was displayed; any length past the base cuts the same. A length past a kilometre (a base about 500 m across) is refused, as any sweep past `MAX_LENGTH` is.

### Keys

Each step's key is SHA-256 of the domain line `silico recompute 1` and a line saying what it asks, every number by its bits, as `Step::key` spells the others. New step kinds add new spellings, so no existing key changes, and the domain line stays at 1.

- **Faces:** `faces <plane>`, then, region by region and loop by loop (outer loop, then holes, each marked: S12), each curve as sent: `line x0 y0 x1 y1`, `arc cx cy sx sy ex ey r a0 a1`, or `circle cx cy r`. Not the sketch's ID or its element IDs: two sketches of one profile share the step, and a construction line toggled, added, or moved leaves the key alone.
- **Signed zeros.** The request's zeros are all +0 already (`Loop::sent`, `SketchPlane::place`). The key takes the bits as sent and normalizes nothing: −0 and +0 can be different requests (an arc's angle from `atan2(±0, −1)` is ±π), so a key that merged them could hand one request the other's result. A test pins that `Profile::faces` never sends a −0 (a property over the hostile profiles), and that the key of a −0 differs from +0's.
- **Sweep:** `prism <faces> <start> <vector>`, `prism-through-all <faces> <base> <direction>`, or `revolution <faces> <origin> <direction> <start> <angle>`.
- **Boolean:** as M2's, `fuse <base> <sweep>` or `cut <base> <sweep>`.
- `fits`, which checks a cached result against its step, learns that a faces step has a history of one input (the regions) and no input keys, and that a through-all sweep has two input keys and a history of one.

### The faces' history, and how a sweep composes with it

`MakeFaces`' history has the regions as its one input: curve `n` in the order sent is kept as one edge, point `k` as one vertex, and region `n` is face `n`. The core holds what each sent curve and point is in the sketch (`profile::Edge`: the curve's ID and its ends' IDs), so it names the faces' elements from the sketch's IDs (S4). That map is the sketch's, and the sweep's input map: the sweep's history names its elements from it, as a boolean's names its result from its inputs' maps. The boolean then names the feature's result from the base's map and the sweep's. So the feature's map is built in three passes, one per step, each one level.

### Naming keys, and MA1

A feature's naming key is of its last step, its ID, how it names, and its inputs' naming keys. A sweep feature's adds the faces' naming: the sketch's ID and the IDs of the curves and points in the order sent. The step keys leave IDs out, but names come from them: two profiles alike but for an ID make one faces step and two maps. Since a fillet's step is keyed by its base's naming key (M2.5), a fillet on a pad whose sketch's IDs changed doesn't take a cached result made from the old names.

MA1 fits as it is, with one change. `Plan::sources` walks a feature's inputs from the maps already named, and waits for a solid not yet built. A sweep feature's map is named from its own steps' results (faces, sweep, boolean), so `sources` treats a sweep feature as built when all of its steps are, and waits for the first that isn't (a cache can hold the boolean's result without the sweep's, which the schedule then runs, since it's in the plan). `planned.uses` stays the features whose solids it consumes: the base only. The sketch isn't one; its faces step is the feature's own. The faces' map is kept by its own naming key, so features sharing a sketch name it once.

## S4. Names

### A sketch's elements

The parser refuses a sketch's name today (`naming/parse.rs`: "makes no solid (a sketch)"). M3.6 gives a sketch's elements names from their IDs, which never move when geometry is dragged or the sketch's lists are reordered:

| Element of the faces | Name |
|---|---|
| curve `6t7v8w9x`'s edge | `k7f2qx3m.sketch.Edge.6t7v8w9x` |
| point `0a1b2c3d`'s vertex | `k7f2qx3m.sketch.Vertex.0a1b2c3d` |
| a circle's one vertex, at angle 0, which no point is | `k7f2qx3m.sketch.Vertex(U k7f2qx3m.sketch.Edge.c1rc1e00)` |
| the profile's face, when it's the only region | `k7f2qx3m.sketch.Face1` |
| a region's face, when there are several | `k7f2qx3m.sketch.Face(L k7f2qx3m.sketch.Edge.6t7v8w9x)`: named from below, by its outer loop's curve with the smallest ID |

**Recommended:** the grammar (N8's, in `naming/parse.rs`) gains a base form and five type words. For `sketch`, the number form is `Face1` only, and it admits the ID forms and the `U` and `L` forms (a circle's vertex, a region of several), never an `M` or `G` derived form: a sketch derives nothing.

```
base     = …                                            N8's forms
         | feature ".sketch." ( "Edge" | "Vertex" ) "." id    a sketch's curve or point, by its ID
         | feature ".sketch.Face1"                      a profile's only region
type     = … | "pad" | "pocket" | "revolve" | "groove" | "sketch"
id       = 8 characters of the ID alphabet
```

- The kind is written, as in every name, so a name says what it names without the document. `Edge.` names a line, arc, or circle; `Vertex.` a point. The dot keeps an ID that's all digits apart from a number (`Edge.12345678`, not `Edge12345678`).
- **A profile's only region is `Face1`**, whatever its curves. A pad and a revolve always have one (decision 3), so their top and bottom faces keep their names through any edit to the outline: a construction line toggled into the profile, a side split, a rectangle redrawn as a circle. That's `testConstructionToggleTNP`'s point, and SolidWorks', whose extrude keeps its end face through sketch edits. It's never silent: when a second region appears, `Face1` stops naming anything, and a reference to it breaks.
- **Several regions are each named by one curve**, the outer loop's smallest ID, as FreeCAD names a profile's face by one edge (`FaceMaker::postBuild`, `minElementNames = 1`) rather than the lower pass's five: each curve is in one loop and each outer loop bounds one region, so one curve names it uniquely, and an edit changes the name only if it removes that curve or adds a smaller one. It's the curve that orders the regions (M3.5's question 2). FreeCAD's first edge is the oldest, which tends to survive edits; Silico's IDs are random, so for a pocket of several regions this is the weaker name. Removing that curve breaks it loudly, but re-wiring it into another region's outer loop moves the reference to that region's floor silently (S11).
- A whole name that's a sketch's element is refused as a reference, saying why and what to write: "s0000001 is a sketch: its elements are in no solid; name what a feature made of it (p0000001.pad.Edge(M s0000001.sketch.Edge.6t7v8w9x))". Sketch names appear only as sources inside a feature's names.
- FreeCAD's element map names sketch elements alike, by each geometry's facade ID (`g815`, a vertex `g815v1`, op code `SKT`), not by its place (`Edge3` is only the index name). It reuses an ID for a new curve whose ends match a deleted one (`geoHistoryLevel`), a heuristic Silico doesn't copy: a new curve gets a new ID.

### A sweep's elements

**Recommended:** every element of a sweep's solid is the sweep feature's own, named from the sketch's elements in one level:

| Element of a pad's solid | Name | FreeCAD 1.1.3 |
|---|---|---|
| a side face, from curve `c` | `p.pad.Face(G s.sketch.Edge.c)` | generated from the edge (`;:G;XTR`) |
| a side edge, from point `v` | `p.pad.Edge(G s.sketch.Vertex.v)` | generated from the vertex |
| the top face | `p.pad.Face(G s.sketch.Face1)` | from the generated solid, `;:G0` (`checkForParallelOrCoplanar`) |
| a top edge, over curve `c` | `p.pad.Edge(G s.sketch.Edge.c)` | from the side face above it (`Edge1;:G;XTR;:U;XTR`), by `MapShapes` order |
| a top vertex, over point `v` | `p.pad.Vertex(G s.sketch.Vertex.v)` | from above |
| the bottom face | `p.pad.Face(M s.sketch.Face1)` | kept, with the pad's tag |
| a bottom edge, curve `c` | `p.pad.Edge(M s.sketch.Edge.c)` | kept, with the pad's tag |
| a bottom vertex, point `v` | `p.pad.Vertex(M s.sketch.Vertex.v)` | kept, with the pad's tag |

A revolve's are alike: faces of revolution `G` from their curves, circles `G` from their points, the start face and edges `M`, the end face and edges `G` of their own kind; an edge or point on the axis, whose start counterpart is itself, `M`. A whole turn has no ends: OCCT reports the profile's edges deleted, and each face's seam is named from above by the face it bounds (`r.revolve.Edge(U r.revolve.Face(G s.sketch.Edge.c))`).

On the axis, as the kernel reports it (measured when step ii was built, docs/ports/history.md):

| Element of a revolve's solid | Name |
|---|---|
| an edge of the profile along the axis (a turn short of a whole one) | `r.revolve.Edge(M s.sketch.Edge.c)`: its start, which is its end; it sweeps nothing |
| a point of the profile on the axis | `r.revolve.Vertex(M s.sketch.Vertex.v)`: its start, which is its end |
| the degenerate edge where a face of revolution closes on the axis (a cone's apex) | `r.revolve.Edge(G s.sketch.Vertex.v)`, generated from that point as any point's side edge is. Where two faces close at one point (a diamond's tip), `MakeRevol` makes one for each, both generated from the point: they're told apart as any two elements of one name are, by rank (`G1/2`, `G2/2`). A fillet or chamfer can't take a degenerate edge, so no reference a user stores names one, and their names only need to be unique. |

Two corrections from what the kernel reports (step ii): a whole turn's edge that sweeps a face of revolution starts at that face's seam (OCCT's `FirstShape`), so the seam has a history, `M` from its curve, which naming (step v) may use in place of the name from above; and a whole turn's edge square to the axis reports the flat face it sweeps (`G`), with no start.

Two departures from FreeCAD make this, both approved, recorded in docs/ports/naming.md when built:

1. **The ends are OCCT's.** The end counterpart of each profile element is OCCT's `LastShape(element)` on `BRepPrimAPI_MakePrism` and `BRepPrimAPI_MakeRevol`, reported as `generated` (S3), so the core names it `G` from its source, of the source's own kind. The kind tells it from the side the same source generates (an edge's top edge is an `Edge`, its side face a `Face`). FreeCAD names the top face from the solid its profile generates (`;:G0`, misaligned, docs/ports/naming.md) and the top edges and vertices from above. A pad's top edges are the most referenced edges of a part (the edges a fillet rounds first), so their names should be short and stable: these are one level, exact, and independent of the top face's name, where naming them from above would name a top edge by two faces and a top vertex by three edges, two of those named from above themselves.
2. **Nothing in a solid is a sketch's.** The start counterpart is reported `modified`, and the sweep step bites nothing (`bites(sweep, 0)` is false, below), so the bottom face, its edges, and its vertices are the feature's, one `M` source: `p.pad.Edge(M s.sketch.Edge.c)`. No exception in the builder: the sweep's profile is an input the step keeps neither side of, `bites`' third value (below), which writes one `M`, uncollapsed. (N8 wrote a non-biting input's piece with one `M`; N9 writes it `I`, and "inside" is the wrong word for a profile that is in no solid, so the table gets the third value.) FreeCAD keeps the name and adds the pad's tag; Silico has no tags (N2), and its kept rule rests on each lineage being a tree, which a shared sketch breaks (S2). It also makes every element of a pad say `pad`, so a reference never finds a pad's bottom face in a revolve made of the same sketch.

### The boolean: a sweep feature's own sweep bites

**Recommended**, stated generally: a sweep feature's boolean bites its base as N8's table has it (a fuse's and a cut's base both bite), **and its own sweep is a biting input**, for the cut as for the fuse. So a pocket's and a groove's cut bite inputs 0 and 1, where a `cut` feature's tool doesn't. The sweep's elements already say the feature and its type, and no other feature ever operates on the intermediate sweep, so `q.pocket.Face(G s.sketch.Edge.c)` has one reading, "the wall of pocket q from curve c". Were the own sweep not biting, its pieces would wrap `q.pocket` in `q.pocket`, and siblings would differ in form by OCCT's reporting alone: measured on the oracle's FreeCAD, a blind pocket's walls come back modified one from one though nothing trims them (`#16:n;:M;CUT`), and its floor kept (`#24:1;:H`).

So, after the boolean, an element the base or the sweep keeps or trims keeps its name, and the elements the boolean makes are the feature's:

- **Walls** are `q.pocket.Face(G s.sketch.Edge.c)` whether OCCT reports them kept, modified one from one, or trimmed by the base, and whether the pocket is blind or through. The **floor** of a blind pocket is `q.pocket.Face(G s.sketch.Face1)`; a through pocket has none. A wall the base splits is ranked, as any split is: `q.pocket.Face(M1/2 q.pocket.Face(G s.sketch.Edge.c))`, and the one-piece name breaks, with both pieces as candidates.
- **Rims: which case gives which.** These are the edges fillets name, so the two cases are told apart:
  - **A profile drawn in the base's face** (a sketch on the plane of the base's top, the common case): the rim is the sweep's start edge, modified one from one by the boolean (measured on the oracle: `#d:n;:M;FUS` for a pad, `#d:n;:M;CUT` for a pocket). It's `p.pad.Edge(M s.sketch.Edge.c)` for a pad and `q.pocket.Edge(M s.sketch.Edge.c)` for a pocket.
  - **A profile whose sweep passes through a face of the base** (a pocket from a plane below the base, cutting up through its top; a cylinder through a plate): the rim is a section edge, generated by the boolean from the base's face and the wall: `q.pocket.Edge(G b.box.Face6, G q.pocket.Face(G s.sketch.Edge.c))`.
- **The base's face** a flush pad or pocket sits in keeps its bare name (`b.box.Face6`): measured, it's `Face6;:M;FUS` or `;:M;CUT` with one source, which biting collapses.

### `bites`, per type word

| Type word | Step | Input | `bites` | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `sketch` | faces | (the regions) | n/a | named by ID, as a primitive by number |
| `pad`, `revolve`, `pocket`, `groove` | sweep | 0, the faces | neither: keeps neither side, writes `M` uncollapsed | nothing in a solid is a sketch's; the profile is in no solid, so neither outside (bare) nor inside (`I`) |
| `pad`, `revolve` | fuse | 0, the base | yes | Fuse: outside both |
| `pad`, `revolve` | fuse | 1, its own sweep | yes | Fuse |
| `pocket`, `groove` | cut | 0, the base | yes | Cut keeps the base's outside |
| `pocket`, `groove` | cut | 1, its own sweep | yes, unlike a `cut` feature's tool | its own sweep (above) |

`bites` is three-valued: outside (the name collapses to its source's, N7), inside (`I`, N9), and neither (one `M`, uncollapsed: a sweep's profile). Every input of a sweep feature's boolean bites, so no `I` ever appears in a sketch feature's names (N9's M3.6 section confirms the design).

Every type word is the document's type, so a feature made another type under its ID breaks every reference to its elements (`reference_broken`, with the new type's names as candidates), as N8 has it for operations and Trevin's 2026-10-17 rule for primitives: a pocket made a pad offers `q.pad.…`.

FreeCAD's op codes stay as data for FreeCAD's form, one per step: `XTR` for a prism, `RVL` for a revolution, `FUS` and `CUT` for the booleans. The ported prism and revolve fixtures compare with FreeCAD's names where FreeCAD's history names the element (the sides), and are noted where the two departures differ by design.

**`NAMING_SCHEME`** becomes 5, after N8's 3 and N9's 4: the grammar and the builder change, though no existing document's names do. A fillet's cached result keyed by scheme 4 is then not taken (M2.5), costing one rebuild.

## S5. Several regions, and one solid

**Decided**, as decision 3 has it, strict (Fable's review: strict is the reversible direction, since relaxing it later breaks no document; it costs nothing, and the error names the fix; revisit with region profiles, decision 8, where `regions` arrives):

- A **pad or revolve** of a profile with more than one region is refused before any kernel step: `invalid_input`, the feature the sketch (its elements are the sketch's, and that's where they're fixed, as in every M3.5 refusal), the elements every region's outer-loop curves in region order, and their total. The message names the feature that refused: "sketch s0000001 makes 2 separate regions (inside loops a, b, c, d and e, f, g, h), and pad p0000001 makes one solid of one region". The suggestion: "pad each region from its own sketch, or join them into one region; a pocket takes several". This holds with a base that would join the regions, too. A revolve's regions would make separate solids too (disjoint in the plane, so disjoint when turned).
- A **pocket or groove** takes every region: one face each, one sweep of them all (OCCT sweeps a compound of faces into a compound of solids), one cut.
- **A result of more than one solid is refused**, for every pad, pocket, revolve, and groove: the boolean's history already counts them (`History::result.solids > 1`), so it costs nothing. `kernel_failed`, the feature, the message "pad p0000001 doesn't reach its base b0000001: the result is 2 solids" (for a pocket, "pocket q0000001 cuts its base b0000001 in two: the result is 2 solids"), the suggestion "move the sketch or lengthen the pad, or pad it with no base" (for a pocket, "move the sketch or shorten the pocket"). A floating boss, or a pocket that cuts its base in two, is a body nothing can address until multi-body features exist, and accepting it now would be the choice that's hard to reverse. This is D2's letter and decision 3's intent. M2's booleans are untouched: a `fuse` of two separate boxes still gives a compound of two.
- SolidWorks makes a multibody part from disjoint contours and from a boss that misses its body; FreeCAD makes several solids, since its single-solid rule ("Result has multiple solids", made to count solids by `eb52dd4a9a`) applies only when a body's `AllowCompound` is false, and it's true by default since `406c3ba42c` (2025-09). Both departures are deliberate.

## S6. The revolve's axis

**Recommended:** the axis is `x_axis`, `y_axis`, or any line of the sketch, construction or not (S1). SolidWorks revolves about a centerline or any line of the sketch, including a side of the profile; FreeCAD about the sketch's axes, its construction line segments (`AxisN`), an origin axis, or any edge (a sketch's included; a circle by its axis). A profile line as the axis lies on the profile, which the profile may touch. The model's origin axes need nothing new: on an origin plane they're the sketch's axes.

Checked once the sketch is solved, in the core, from the solved coordinates (decision 2):

- **The axis mustn't cross the profile.** Every profile curve lies on one side of the axis's line, a point within 1e-7 of it counting as on it, so the profile may touch the axis or run along it (FreeCAD: "Revolve axis intersects the sketch", `8371982dfc`, which samples each outer wire near where it touches the axis for a change of sign). Each curve's extreme signed distances from the line are closed-form: a line's ends; an arc's ends, and its circle's two points farthest from the line where they lie on the arc (the cross-product test M3.5 uses); a circle's centre ± its radius. A crossing is refused as `invalid_input`, the feature the sketch, the elements the curves on both sides (and the axis, if it's a line), with "move the profile to one side of the axis, or turn it about another line; a profile may touch its axis".
- **An axis of no length.** A line of no length gives no direction. "No length" is M3.2's zero-size rule, the one the gate and `SketchSystem::check` use for a line of no size: the solver's own squared length, dx² + dy², is exactly 0 (`silico_protocol::no_distance`). So ends at one place are refused, and so are ends so close that their squared distance underflows to 0 (5e-324 apart, which OCCT's `gp_Dir` would throw on); ends 1e-154 apart, whose square is about 1e-308 (subnormal, but not 0), pass. The gate can't see it: the solve can bring two points together. Refused in the core, not left to OCCT's throw: `invalid_input`, the feature the revolve, the parameter `axis`, "axis 6t7v8w9x, a line of sketch s0000003, has its ends at one point: it gives no direction to turn about", with "give the line some length, or turn about another line".
- **An angle that moves nothing**, as M1's revolve refuses one: the profile's farthest point from the axis times the angle in radians at most 1e-7. `invalid_input`, the feature the revolve, the parameter `extent.angle`.
- A side exactly 1e-7 from the axis makes OCCT fail (docs/ports/extrude-revolve.md); the kernel's refusal is passed on with M1's suggestion, as FreeCAD's OCCT fails alike.

## S7. What OCCT gets wrong

docs/ports/profile.md measured two wrong results near the tolerance on the oracle's FreeCAD (1.1.3, OCCT 7.9.3), each valid to OCCT: a pad whose profile has a wall g ≤ 3e-7 wide, fused onto a 10 mm cube, drops the whole pad (volume 1000, 6 faces); the pocket of the same is valid with its volume off by about 30 × g. The profile refuses walls up to 1e-7 (the maintainer's tolerance), so walls between that and about 3e-7 reach OCCT. The rule is to accept OCCT's wrong results as FreeCAD does, document them, and verify nothing at cost, but to measure volumes precisely.

Those were measured through FreeCAD's PartDesign, whose booleans use an automatic fuzzy value (1e-6 × the arguments' diagonal, about 1.7e-5 on that cube, since `cf8ad66373`), and Silico's booleans use none (M2). Measured again on FreeCAD's Part booleans without the fuzzy value, as Silico's run (the port note's table): the wrong results need the thin wall to lie flush with a face of the base; there, a pad is dropped for 1e-7 < g ≤ 3e-7 and right from 4e-7, and a pocket is off by 20 × g up to 3e-7; inset, both are right at every g. M3.6's kernel tests pin it on Silico's own booleans. The rule below doesn't depend on the cause.

**Decided:**

- **A sweep feature that changes nothing is refused, by volume alone.** A pad or revolve is refused when V(result) − V(base) ≤ 1e-9 × V(base); a pocket or groove when V(base) − V(result) ≤ 1e-9 × V(base). The 1e-9 is the precise measure's own relative precision (`crates/silico-kernel/cpp/bridge.cpp`, `measure`'s `BRepGProp::VolumeProperties(…, 1e-9)`), kept as one named constant that the measure and this check both use, so neither can change alone. `kernel_failed`, M2's empty-result family: the geometry is valid, so not `invalid_result`, and the input is valid, so not `invalid_input`. The message gives the counts as evidence, not as a condition: "pad p0000001 adds nothing to its base b0000001: the result's volume is the base's, 1000 mm³ (7 faces against the base's 6)", with "if the pad points into its base, reverse it; if it lies inside it, move its sketch; if neither, OCCT lost it: a thin wall of its profile flush with a face of the base can do this (docs/ports/sketch-features.md), so thicken it". Likewise "pocket q0000001 removes nothing from its base …".
  - **Why not counts too.** A fuse only adds and a cut only removes, so the change in volume is the exact witness of "nothing", to the measure's resolution; counts aren't. Measured on the oracle's FreeCAD: a pad reversed into its base from a sketch on the base's top face, the commonest wrong-way pad, and a pocket reversed out of it, each give **7 faces at the base's volume** (the top split along the outline), so a test of counts and volume both would build a no-op that scars the top. A history witness fails the same case (the split piece is attributed to both inputs, `#14:1;:M#1c;FUS`).
  - It costs nothing: both solids are measured for their fingerprints already. It catches the dropped thin pad (the base alone, 6 faces, 1000), which no fingerprint would (a new pad has none), and the wrong-way pad and pocket, which FreeCAD builds as no-ops. SolidWorks errors on a feature that changes nothing. A pad under 1e-9 of its base (under about a 0.1 mm cube on a 100 mm part) is refused too, loudly, the message saying why.
  - It extends the standing decision's "the result is nothing" (M2's empty boolean) to "the feature's change is nothing", and verifies nothing at cost.
- **The pocket's volume error is accepted and documented**: a valid solid with the right topology and a volume off by about 20 × g (under 1e-5 mm³) where a thin wall lies flush with the base, recorded by its fingerprint. Finding it would take a second boolean or sampling, which M2's review measured at up to 30 s a boolean and the maintainer declined.
- **The tolerance stays 1e-7.** Raising it to 4e-7 to hide OCCT's band would refuse real geometry by a heuristic.
- M2's booleans keep FreeCAD's behavior (`fuse` of a box 2e-7 from another gives the box alone, accepted, docs/ports/booleans.md). A sweep feature checks more because it always means to change its base, and a `fuse` feature needn't.

## S8. Refine

**Recommended:** no refine in M3.6, as in M2: a sweep feature's result is OCCT's boolean, unrefined, and refine comes as its own step with its own port note (docs/ports/booleans.md). FreeCAD 1.1.3 refines PartDesign features by default (`e490531510`, from the user's `RefineModel` preference), so every oracle case sets `Refine = False` on each FreeCAD feature, as `oracle.py` already does for chamfers. Face and edge counts are then OCCT's: a pad flush with its base's side leaves the side split in two, in Silico and in FreeCAD unrefined. No `refine` field now: SolidWorks always merges, and when refine comes it's a change of behavior for a feature version (`v`), with its own design.

## S11. Residual risk

From Fable's review, accepted:

- **A multi-region pocket's floor** is named by its outer loop's smallest curve, so re-wiring that curve into another region's outer loop moves a reference to the floor: silent, bounded to floors of pockets and grooves of several regions, and FreeCAD's too. The loud alternatives rename on every outline edit.
- **A pad under 1e-9 of its base's volume** (under about a 0.1 mm cube on a 100 mm part) is refused as adding nothing: loud, the message says why.
- **The pocket's volume error** of about 20 × g where a thin wall lies flush with the base: silent, recorded by the fingerprint, under the standing decision on OCCT's wrong results.
- **A whole turn's seam names** depend on OCCT's reporting: pinned by the tests, so a change is loud.
- **OCCT's kept and modified reporting** varies by case; with every input of a sketch feature's boolean biting, it never reaches a name.

## S12. As built: steps iv and v

Recompute builds the four features, and naming names them, as S3 and S4 say, with these choices made while building them, each by FreeCAD and the principles above:

- **The faces key marks its regions and loops** (S3): `faces <plane>`, then ` region` before each region and ` loop` before each loop, then each curve as S3 spells it. Without the marks a region with a hole and two regions of the same curves would be one line, and one key for two requests.
- **A whole turn's seam is named by history** (S4, and step ii's correction): `r.revolve.Edge(M s.sketch.Edge.c)`, the edge's start as OCCT reports it, not `Edge(U r.revolve.Face(G …))` from above. FreeCAD's order takes history before what's above, and the name is one level and independent of the face's. Pinned on the real kernel (a tube and a 360° groove, their seams stable across a rebuild and an edit of the base).
- **FreeCAD's op code is the feature's, not the step's** (S4): every element a sweep feature names says `XTR` (pad, pocket) or `RVL` (revolve, groove) in FreeCAD's form, its fuse's or cut's too, where FreeCAD writes `FUS` or `CUT`. The names hold one operation word for every step (the feature's type), and FreeCAD's form is only for comparison and FreeCAD's orders; no stored name depends on it.
- **`bites` keeps its two-valued form for a feature's last step**, and the three-valued table is `OpCode::side(stage, input)`, `Stage::Sweep` or `Stage::Last`, `Side::Outside`, `Inside`, or `Neither`.
- **The axis's crossing and an angle that moves nothing** (S6) are checked in recompute's step iv with the axis's length (`sweep::check_axis_turn`), before any kernel step: step iii had built the length's check alone.
- **An axis line whose ends the solve brings together** is refused first by M3.4's solve, which refuses geometry of no size, naming the line: the revolve is blocked on its sketch, with no kernel call. `sweep::check_axis_length` stays, behind it, for what the solve lets through.
- **The result's checks (S5, S7) are part of the fuse or cut's step**: it measures its result (and its base, if its own measure hasn't yet) and refuses as a step does, `kernel_failed` kept with the step's key, so what uses the feature never runs on a refused result; the result comes measured, so it isn't measured again.
- **A sketch that makes no profile** fails each sweep of it as `FeatureOutcome::Broken`, the outcome that lists a feature's errors and counts them (its doc says so now): every refusal names the sketch and its elements.
- **A whole name of a sketch's element** is refused by the parser at its top ("s is a sketch: its elements are in no solid; name what a feature made of it"), and the gate adds what to write where the fillet's base sweeps that sketch (`(p.pad.Edge(M s.sketch.Edge.c))`, the base's own word). A sketch's `Face1` inside a name is stable (the gate's check for operations' elements by number leaves it alone).
- **The fake kernel** models a boolean whose tool is a sweep of faces as one solid (a fuse's) or its base's (a cut's), and such a cut as its base less the share of its tool inside the base's box, so pads add, pockets remove, and the checks pass where the real kernel's would; M2's booleans on the fake are as they were.
- **S9's tests, as measured**: renaming a profile's IDs makes no kernel call when the rename keeps their order (a loop is walked from its smallest curve ID, M3.5, so a rename that reorders them sends the same outline from another curve). In `testConstructionToggleTNP`'s essence the chamfer removes 1²/2 × 30 before the toggle and 1/3 more after it: the top edge's end at (0, 30) then meets the zigzag's slanted last side, and the chamfer runs on to that side's plane (∫ 2u dA over its section, worked by hand), as any chamfer of an edge ending at a slanted face does.
