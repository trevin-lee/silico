# M4 walkthrough: an agent designs a bracket through MCP

This is written for you, the maintainer, not for agents. It walks through M4.9's done test: an agent with nothing but `silico serve` (the MCP server) designs an L-bracket from a written spec, gets one edit wrong and rolls it back, renders the part, measures it, and exports a 3MF for printing. The session is recorded in [crates/silico-mcp/tests/bracket/session.jsonl](../../crates/silico-mcp/tests/bracket/session.jsonl) and replayed by [crates/silico-mcp/tests/bracket.rs](../../crates/silico-mcp/tests/bracket.rs); the spec is [crates/silico-mcp/tests/bracket/spec.md](../../crates/silico-mcp/tests/bracket/spec.md). The design is docs/design/m4.9-mcp.md, *The bracket*.

## The spec

Base leg 60 (x) × 40 (y) × 5 (z) on z = 0; upright leg 5 thick (x 0 to 5), 40 wide, from the base to z = 50; two M5 clearance holes (5.5 across) through each leg, 16 apart, centred across the width (base at x = 40, y = 12 and 28; upright at z = 35, y = 12 and 28); the inside corner filleted, radius 5, along the whole width.

By hand: V = 21,000 (legs) − 151.25π (four holes) + 1,000 − 250π (the fillet's spandrel) = 22,000 − 401.25π ≈ 20,739.4359 mm³.

## The session, step by step

Each step is one `tools/call`; what the agent sees is the reply's `structuredContent` (the command's envelope: `ok`, the session's revision, the model read, and the result). The agent supplies its own feature and sketch IDs, so the replay is the same every time (a live agent may leave them out, and the server makes random ones).

1. **`open`** `bracket.silico` with `create: true`: a new, empty document, session `s1` at revision 1.
2. **`begin`** a transaction. Until `commit`, nothing touches the file.
3. **`add_feature`**, the L-profile: a sketch on the XZ plane (its x along +X, its y along +Z), six points, six lines, fully constrained: six horizontal or vertical constraints, its first corner at the origin, and the lengths 60, 5, 5, and 50.
4. **`add_feature`**, the wrong edit: a pad of the profile 4 deep, not 40. The XZ plane's normal is −Y, so the pad is `reversed` to go +Y, where the spec puts the part.
5. **`check`**: no problems. The model builds; nothing is wrong with it but its size.
6. **`mass_properties`**: 2,100 mm³ (the profile's 525 mm² × 4), where the spec's legs are 21,000. The agent sees the mistake.
7. **`rollback`**: every edit since `begin` undone; the file was never written.
8. **`begin`** again, **`add_feature`** the profile again, and the pad, 40 deep.
9. **`mass_properties`**: 21,000 mm³, area 9,850 mm². The legs are right.
10. **`add_feature`** the base's holes: a sketch on the XY plane, two circles 5.5 across at (40, 12) and (40, 28); then a pocket through everything of the legs, `reversed` (a pocket goes against its sketch's normal, and the base is above the XY plane).
11. **`add_feature`** the upright's holes: a sketch on the YZ plane (its x along +Y, its y along +Z), circles at (12, 35) and (28, 35); a pocket through the upright, `reversed` to go +X.
12. **`mass_properties`**: 21,000 − 151.25π ≈ 20,524.8341 mm³.
13. **`query`** the straight edges 40 long: six, one along y at each of the profile's corners, each with its name, ends, and midpoint. The agent takes the one from (5, 0, 5) to (5, 40, 5), the inside corner: `p0000001.pad.Edge(G s0000001.sketch.Vertex.pa000004)`, the pad's edge generated from the profile's corner point.
14. **`add_feature`** a fillet of radius 5 on that edge, by name.
15. **`commit`**: the document is saved, with the fingerprints of the four solids the edits reached.
16. **`check`**: no problems, no warnings.
17. **`mass_properties`**: 20,739.435948 mm³, area 9,930.398194 mm², centre (18.019555, 20, 13.014015), each as worked out by hand.
18. **`query`** the smallest planar face facing +X: the base's end face, 200 mm², at x = 60. **`query`** the circles of radius 2.75: the eight rims of the four holes, each with its axis.
19. **`distance`** from the rim of the hole at (40, 12) to the end face: 17.25 (20 − 2.75), and `center_to_element` 20, the hole's centre from the end.
20. **`render`** from the top front right (the isometric corner), 800 × 600, the rim and the end face highlighted, written to `bracket.png`; the PNG also comes back to the agent as an image.
21. **`export`** `bracket.3mf` at FreeCAD's 0.1 mm: one object, 2,180 triangles and 1,084 vertices enclosing 20,739.948255 mm³ (a mesh of a part whose curved faces are concave holds slightly more than the part).
22. **`close`** the session.

## What the replay checks

- Every reply's `structuredContent`, byte for byte, against the recording, except what's never compared: `render`'s `png_bytes` and the PNG itself (GPUs differ), progress notifications (timing), and `initialize`'s `serverInfo.version` and the workspace's absolute path (the session uses only relative paths, so nothing else quotes it). Each reply also holds to its tool's `outputSchema`.
- By hand, never from the recording: the wrong pad's 2,100; the legs' 21,000; the holes' cut; the bracket's volume, area, and centre of mass, each to the measure's precision (a relative 1e-9, then 6 decimals); the saved fingerprint's box, [0, 0, 0] to [60, 40, 50], and its 13 faces, 33 edges, and 22 vertices; the end face's area; the hole's distances; the render's size, target, and view direction.
- The 3MF, read from its bytes: closed, manifold, consistently oriented, no degenerate triangle, facing outward, of genus 4 (V − F/2 = −6 for four through holes), its box the part's, and its volume within the tolerance's bound of the hand calculation: at least V less what rounding to 32 bits can move (0.06 mm³), and at most V plus the area between each curved face's arcs and their chords (1.003π ≈ 3.15 mm³, from FreeCAD's angular deflection of 0.1 rad).
- FreeCAD 1.1.3 reads the 3MF back: its mesh a solid, with no non-manifold edge or misoriented facet, and the shape made from it (`makeShapeFromMesh`, then `makeSolid`) a valid, closed solid with the mesh's volume.

To record the session again (only for a change you've approved, since every reply is part of it): `SILICO_BRACKET_RECORD=1 cargo nextest run -p silico-mcp -E 'test(bracket)'`, then check the diff.
