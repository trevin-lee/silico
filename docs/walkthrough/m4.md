# M4 walkthrough: an agent designs a bracket through MCP

This is written for you, the maintainer, not for agents. It walks through M4.9's done test: an agent with nothing but `silico serve` (the MCP server) designs an L-bracket from a written spec, gets one edit wrong and rolls it back, renders the part, measures it, and exports a 3MF for printing. The session is recorded in [crates/silico-cli/tests/bracket/session.jsonl](../../crates/silico-cli/tests/bracket/session.jsonl) and replayed by [crates/silico-cli/tests/bracket.rs](../../crates/silico-cli/tests/bracket.rs); the spec is [crates/silico-cli/tests/bracket/spec.md](../../crates/silico-cli/tests/bracket/spec.md). The design is docs/design/m4.9-mcp.md, *The bracket*.

## The spec

Base leg 60 (x) × 40 (y) × 5 (z) on z = 0; upright leg 5 thick (x 0 to 5), 40 wide, from the base to z = 50; two M5 clearance holes (5.5 across) through each leg, 16 apart, centred across the width (base at x = 40, y = 12 and 28; upright at z = 35, y = 12 and 28); the inside corner filleted, radius 5, along the whole width.

By hand: V = 21,000 (legs) − 151.25π (four holes) + 1,000 − 250π (the fillet's spandrel) = 22,000 − 401.25π ≈ 20,739.4359 mm³.

## The session, step by step

Each step is one `tools/call`; what the agent sees is the reply's `structuredContent` (the command's envelope: `ok`, the session's revision, the model read, and the result). The agent supplies its own feature and sketch IDs, so the replay is the same every time (a live agent may leave them out, and the server makes random ones).

1. **`open`** `bracket.silico` with `create: true`: a new, empty document, session `s1` at revision 1.
2. **`begin`** a transaction. Until `commit`, nothing touches the file.
3. **`add_feature`**, the L-profile: a sketch on the XZ plane (its x along +X, its y along +Z), six points, six lines, fully constrained: six horizontal or vertical constraints, its first corner at the origin, and the lengths 60, 5, 5, and 50.
4. **`add_feature`**, the wrong edit: a pad of the profile 4 deep, not 40. The XZ plane's normal is −Y, so the pad is `reversed` to go +Y, where the spec puts the part.
5. **`check`**: no problems. The model builds; nothing is wrong with it but its size.
6. **`mass_properties`**: 2,100 mm³ (the profile's 525 mm² × 4), where the spec's legs are 21,000. The agent sees the mistake.
7. **`rollback`**: every edit since `begin` undone; the file was never written.
8. **`begin`** again, **`add_feature`** the profile again, and the pad, 40 deep.
9. **`mass_properties`**: 21,000 mm³, area 9,850 mm². The legs are right.
10. **`add_feature`** the base's holes: a sketch on the XY plane, two circles 5.5 across at (40, 12) and (40, 28); then a pocket through everything of the legs, `reversed` (a pocket goes against its sketch's normal, and the base is above the XY plane).
11. **`add_feature`** the upright's holes: a sketch on the YZ plane (its x along +Y, its y along +Z), circles at (12, 35) and (28, 35); a pocket through the upright, `reversed` to go +X.
12. **`mass_properties`**: 21,000 − 151.25π ≈ 20,524.8341 mm³.
13. **`query`** the straight edges 40 long: six, one along y at each of the profile's corners, each with its name, ends, and midpoint. The agent takes the one from (5, 0, 5) to (5, 40, 5), the inside corner: `p0000001.pad.Edge(G s0000001.sketch.Vertex.pa000004)`, the pad's edge generated from the profile's corner point.
14. **`add_feature`** a fillet of radius 5 on that edge, by name.
15. **`commit`**: the document is saved, with the fingerprints of the four solids the edits reached.
16. **`check`**: no problems, no warnings.
17. **`mass_properties`**: 20,739.435948 mm³, area 9,930.398194 mm², centre (18.019555, 20, 13.014015), each as worked out by hand.
18. **`query`** the smallest planar face facing +X: the base's end face, 200 mm², at x = 60. **`query`** the circles of radius 2.75: the eight rims of the four holes, each with its axis.
19. **`distance`** from the rim of the hole at (40, 12) to the end face: 17.25 (20 − 2.75), and `center_to_element` 20, the hole's centre from the end.
20. **`render`** from the top front right (the isometric corner), 800 × 600, the rim and the end face highlighted, written to `bracket.png`; the PNG also comes back to the agent as an image.
21. **`export`** `bracket.3mf` at FreeCAD's 0.1 mm: one object, 2,180 triangles and 1,084 vertices enclosing 20,739.948255 mm³ (a mesh of a part whose curved faces are concave holds slightly more than the part).
22. **`close`** the session.

## What the replay checks

- Every reply's `structuredContent`, byte for byte, against the recording, except what's never compared: `render`'s `png_bytes` and the PNG itself (GPUs differ), progress notifications (timing), and `initialize`'s `serverInfo.version` and the workspace's absolute path (the session uses only relative paths, so nothing else quotes it). Each reply also holds to its tool's `outputSchema`.
- By hand, never from the recording: the wrong pad's 2,100; the legs' 21,000; the holes' cut; the bracket's volume, area, and centre of mass, each to the measure's precision (a relative 1e-9, then 6 decimals); the saved fingerprint's box, [0, 0, 0] to [60, 40, 50], and its 13 faces, 33 edges, and 22 vertices; the end face's area; the hole's distances; the render's size, target, and view direction.
- The 3MF, read from its bytes: closed, manifold, consistently oriented, no degenerate triangle, facing outward, its box the part's. Its mesh is the one OCCT's rule makes at 0.1 mm, counted by hand: each edge meshed at half FreeCAD's angular deflection (0.1 rad), so each hole's rim is 126 points and each of the fillet's arcs 33, which with the profile's ten other corners is 1,084 vertices, and, closed and of genus 4, 2,180 triangles. Its volume is the part's plus the segments between those arcs and their chords (about 0.512 mm³), to what rounding the coordinates to 32 bits can move (0.06 mm³).
- FreeCAD 1.1.3 reads the 3MF back: its mesh a solid, with no non-manifold edge or misoriented facet, and the shape made from it (`makeShapeFromMesh`, then `makeSolid`) a valid, closed solid with the mesh's volume.

To record the session again (only for a change you've approved, since every reply is part of it), build the workers, then record, then check the diff:

```bash
pixi run cargo build -p silico-kernel -p silico-sketch -p silico-render --bins
SILICO_BRACKET_RECORD=1 pixi run cargo nextest run -p silico-cli -E 'test(bracket)'
```

## The live run (for the maintainer)

Run on 2026-10-29, as the second half of M4.9's done test: the spec above, given to a fresh agent whose only tools were `silico serve`'s. Its record is in [m4-live/](m4-live/): the prompt, the MCP config, the transcript, the document it saved, and its render.

- **Agent and client:**
  - Claude Code 2.1.212 in print mode (`claude -p`), its model `claude-opus-4-8` (the account's default).
  - Started with `--mcp-config` pointing at a release `silico serve --workspace` (built from main at 00b2ac74, every worker found), `--strict-mcp-config`, `--tools ""` (no built-in tools), and `--allowedTools mcp__silico`.
  - It ran in an empty directory outside the repository, so no project instructions reached it, and it saw silico's 26 tools and nothing else.
  - Its prompt was the spec's quoted text, word for word ([prompt.md](m4-live/prompt.md)).
- **The session:** [m4-live/transcript.jsonl](m4-live/transcript.jsonl): the run itself, from the client's stream-JSON transcript: the prompt, the agent's messages, and each tool call with its result (the PNG's bytes left out, and the workspace's path made generic). It took 21 turns and 2 min 36 s. Its 20 calls, in order:
  - `version`, then `open` `bracket.silico` with `create`, then `begin`.
  - Two `box`es, the base leg (60 × 40 × 5) and the upright (5 × 40 × 50), fused into the L.
  - A `query` for the fused body's line edges, which found the inside corner, `merge000.fuse.Edge(G base0000.box.Face6, G rght0000.box.Face2)`, from (5, 0, 5) to (5, 40, 5). Then a `fillet` of radius 5 on it.
  - A sketch of two circles on `xy` and a through-all `pocket`, then the same on `yz` for the upright's holes.
  - `check`, which found no problems and no warnings.
  - A `query` of the cylindrical faces of radius 2.75, which found the four holes by name.
  - `render` from `top_front_right`, 1000 × 1000, to [bracket-iso.png](m4-live/bracket-iso.png).
  - `mass_properties`, then a `query` for the planar face facing +x (the base's end), then `distance` from a base hole to it.
  - `export` `bracket.3mf` at 0.1 mm, then `commit`.
- **Where it went wrong, and how it recovered:** nowhere.
  - No call was refused and nothing was rolled back. It built the part another way than the recorded session: boxes, a fuse and a fillet, where the recording pads a sketched L profile.
  - It measured the hole's rim to the end face, 17.25, with `distance`, and gave the centre's 20 from the spec's coordinates rather than with `center_to_element`.
  - It exported before committing, inside its transaction, which the API allows; the export read the current model at revision 9.
- **The exported 3MF's volume against the hand calculation:**
  - The part (`mass_properties`) is 20,739.435948 mm³, against 22,000 − 401.25π = 20,739.4359 by hand.
  - The 3MF's mesh, read back from its bytes: 1,090 vertices and 2,192 triangles, closed and consistently oriented, genus 4 (V − E + F = −6), its box [0, 0, 0] to [60, 40, 50], `type="model"` in millimetres. It encloses 20,739.948 mm³, the part plus the segments between the holes' and the fillet's arcs and their chords at 0.1 mm, as the recorded session's does.
- **PrusaSlicer:** 2.9.6, from its command line (`--export-gcode`) with its built-in default print settings, not a printer profile. It sliced the 3MF with no repair and no warning, in one pass, to 541 KB of G-code (an estimated 1 h 4 min). The 3MF is at `~/Desktop/silico-bracket.3mf`, for slicing in the app with a real printer's profile.
